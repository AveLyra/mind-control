## Selected sources and roles

<a id="p01"></a>
### P01 — Prediction error governs pharmacologically induced amnesia for learned fear

Dieuwke Sevenster et al. (2013 Feb 15). [Prediction error governs pharmacologically induced amnesia for learned fear](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23413355/). DOI: `10.1126/science.1231357`. **Primary associative-fear study; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Supported prediction-error dependence in a specific pharmacological fear-memory paradigm. Does not establish a necessary condition for every kind of human memory change or an AI mechanism.

<a id="p02"></a>
### P02 — Prediction error demarcates the transition from retrieval, to reconsolidation, to new learning

Dieuwke Sevenster et al. (2014 Nov). [Prediction error demarcates the transition from retrieval, to reconsolidation, to new learning](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25320349/). DOI: `10.1101/lm.035493.114`. **Primary boundary-condition study; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Same research line as P01. Expectancy changes differentiated retrieval/reconsolidation/new-learning interpretations. Target of the later P03 replication attempt; not a second independent research-team confirmation.

<a id="p03"></a>
### P03 — Demarcating the boundary conditions of memory reconsolidation: An unsuccessful replication

Lotte E Stemerding et al. (2022 Feb 10). [Demarcating the boundary conditions of memory reconsolidation: An unsuccessful replication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35145138/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-022-06119-5`. **Primary unsuccessful replication attempt; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Same lab, modified conditioned stimuli. The predicted selective effect was absent, but weak differential retention in comparison groups limited interpretation. Neither an unqualified successful replication nor a clean universal disproof.

<a id="p04"></a>
### P04 — Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories

Alyssa H Sinclair et al. (2021 Dec 21). [Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34911768/). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.2117625118`. **Primary fMRI study; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Also catalogued as M11 in the earlier follow-up. Two new PMC attempts returned internal errors and the web route returned a browser challenge. Fresh main-body inspection failed; quantitative reuse must consult the corrected publication and P05.

<a id="p05"></a>
### P05 — Correction for Sinclair et al., Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories

Correction notice (2026 Jun 30). [Correction for Sinclair et al., Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42330295/). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.2618281123`. **Published correction; complete PMC notice checked; no abstract supplied.** June 30, 2026 notice describes a preprocessing join error and revisions to numbers/figures. Authors report key findings and conclusions unchanged. No independent data reanalysis here; not a new participant experiment.

<a id="p06"></a>
### P06 — Visuospatial word search task only effective at disrupting declarative memory when prediction error is present during retrieval

Gretchen Hanson Gotthard et al. (2018 Dec). [Visuospatial word search task only effective at disrupting declarative memory when prediction error is present during retrieval](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415000/). DOI: `10.1016/j.nlm.2018.11.003`. **Primary declarative-interference experiment; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Prediction-error-related interference affected free recall but not recognition. Positive-memory task; no general original-trace loss or AI/sleep/hypnosis interaction established.

<a id="p07"></a>
### P07 — Prediction error is out of context: The dominance of contextual stability in structuring episodic memories

Berna Güler et al. (2025 Dec). [Prediction error is out of context: The dominance of contextual stability in structuring episodic memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40571834/). DOI: `10.3758/s13423-025-02723-4`. **Four primary event-segmentation experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Separately manipulated context stability and prediction error. Results favor context stability for the measured temporal segmentation endpoints. Different task and endpoint from episodic false-memory updating; not an exact replication of P04.

<a id="p08"></a>
### P08 — The effect of prediction error on episodic memory encoding is modulated by the outcome of the predictions

Francesco Pupillo et al. (2023 May 29). [The effect of prediction error on episodic memory encoding is modulated by the outcome of the predictions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37248232/). DOI: `10.1038/s41539-023-00166-x`. **Primary encoding experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Model-derived error effects depended on correct versus incorrect prediction outcomes. Recognition improved or worsened accordingly. Computational estimates and encoding outcomes are distinct from an independently isolated reconsolidation mechanism.

<a id="p09"></a>
### P09 — The dark side of testing memory: repeated retrieval can enhance eyewitness suggestibility

Jason C K Chan et al. (2011 Dec). [The dark side of testing memory: repeated retrieval can enhance eyewitness suggestibility](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21859229/). DOI: `10.1037/a0025147`. **Four primary repeated-retrieval experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** More initial retrieval sometimes increased RES, but effects depended on delay and within/between-subject design. The same report identifies benefits to original memory. A research-program extension, not a single universal dose-response law.

<a id="p10"></a>
### P10 — Paradoxical effects of testing: retrieval enhances both accurate recall and suggestibility in eyewitnesses

Jason C K Chan et al. (2011 Jan). [Paradoxical effects of testing: retrieval enhances both accurate recall and suggestibility in eyewitnesses](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20919785/). DOI: `10.1037/a0021204`. **Primary RES extension; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Testing helped uncontaminated details while increasing misinformation reporting. The report found reconsolidation blockage unnecessary for RES in the tested conditions. Same broad research line as P09; endpoints/mechanisms require separate evaluation.

<a id="p11"></a>
### P11 — Retrieval enhances eyewitness suggestibility to misinformation in free and cued recall

Miko M Wilford et al. (2014 Mar). [Retrieval enhances eyewitness suggestibility to misinformation in free and cued recall](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24000960/). DOI: `10.1037/xap0000001`. **Three primary free/cued-recall experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Initial free recall could produce RES. Peripheral details showed clearer effects than central details; the central effect required aggregation. Counters a universal protection claim for a test format.

<a id="p12"></a>
### P12 — Testing potentiates new learning in the misinformation paradigm

Leamarie T Gordon et al. (2014 Feb). [Testing potentiates new learning in the misinformation paradigm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24027043/). DOI: `10.3758/s13421-013-0361-2`. **Three primary new-learning experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Testing influenced processing/attention without reduced original-information accessibility under its modified test. Test-potentiated new learning is a viable explanation; not unique proof of original-memory rewriting.

<a id="p13"></a>
### P13 — Telling a good story: The effects of memory retrieval and context processing on eyewitness suggestibility

Jessica A LaPaglia et al. (2019). [Telling a good story: The effects of memory retrieval and context processing on eyewitness suggestibility](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30789952/). DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0212592`. **Three primary context-processing experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Context reinstatement and coherence mattered across narrative/question formats. The authors caution against interpreting conditional analyses without accounting for item-selection artifacts.

<a id="p14"></a>
### P14 — Discrepancy detection in the retrieval-enhanced suggestibility paradigm

Brendon Jerome Butler et al. (2018 Apr). [Discrepancy detection in the retrieval-enhanced suggestibility paradigm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28899262/). DOI: `10.1080/09658211.2017.1371193`. **Three primary discrepancy-detection experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Concurrent consistency assessment negated overall RES. Retrospective detection associated with resistance, but detected subgroups did not show uniform immunity. Detection measured after treatment cannot be assumed a randomized causal moderator.

<a id="p15"></a>
### P15 — How prior testing impacts misinformation processing: A dual-task approach

Leamarie T Gordon et al. (2020 Feb). [How prior testing impacts misinformation processing: A dual-task approach](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31385240/). DOI: `10.3758/s13421-019-00970-0`. **Two primary dual-task experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Changing processing demands during the later account altered RES and new-account learning. Supports an attention/learning explanation without isolating a neural reconsolidation process.

<a id="p16"></a>
### P16 — Protective effects of testing across misinformation formats in the household scene paradigm

Rosemary S Pereverseff et al. (2020 Mar). [Protective effects of testing across misinformation formats in the household scene paradigm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31561745/). DOI: `10.1177/1747021819881948`. **Primary protective-testing experiment; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Protective results occurred in conditionalized free recall and source testing across formats. Intrusions made during initial retrieval could persist. Conditionalized endpoints do not automatically establish unconditional population protection.

<a id="p17"></a>
### P17 — Warning weakens retrieval-enhanced suggestibility only when it is given shortly after misinformation: The critical importance of timing

Jason C K Chan et al. (2022 Dec). [Warning weakens retrieval-enhanced suggestibility only when it is given shortly after misinformation: The critical importance of timing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34990152/). DOI: `10.1037/xap0000394`. **Three primary warning-delay experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Early warnings retained benefit when the final test was delayed; delaying the warning itself removed benefit. Delay placement matters; no causal sleep comparison. Related warning literature, not an exact replication of the different 2025 designs.

<a id="p18"></a>
### P18 — Examining the impact of warnings on eyewitness memory

McKinzey G Torrance et al. (2025 Sep 29). [Examining the impact of warnings on eyewitness memory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41023107/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-025-17377-4`. **Two primary warning-frequency experiments; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Accuracy/confidence benefits for misinformation came with underconfidence costs on uncontaminated material after repeated warnings. Same broader Thomas/Karanian/Race research line as earlier M32, with changed questions and design.

<a id="p19"></a>
### P19 — Retrieval potentiates new learning: A theoretical and meta-analytic review

DOI: `10.1037/bul0000166`. **Theoretical/meta-analytic review; complete abstract/metadata checked.** Test-potentiated new learning can improve correct recall or reduce intrusions, with important moderators. ## Interpretation boundary

The evidence supports restricted RES effects, test-potentiated learning, contextual and discrepancy-related moderation, and state-dependent neural processing. A common prediction vocabulary is not evidence that these are one process. The strongest missing inference is still the combined endpoint: an AI interaction that causally uses a specified human prediction-error/retrieval pathway, with independently established sleep/hypnosis contributions and durable personal-memory change. Supportive results, protective results, nulls and correction status remain visible together.
