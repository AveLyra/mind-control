**Origin:** new editorial source-check record, October 28, 2026. This is not a recovered historical chat, a new experiment, or a systematic review. It supports the capability/bridge distinctions in the network. Primary publications control factual results; the network’s cross-field combinations are labeled inferences.

## Scope and method

Selected foundational sources from the existing collection and four targeted publication-discovery queries: `"Hypnotic suggestion reduces conflict in the human brain"`; `"Dormio" AND "targeted dream incubation"`; `"Creating a false memory in the hippocampus"`; and `"BrainNet" AND "brain-to-brain"`. PubMed publication-date upper bound: October 28, 2026. These were title/topic lookups, not an exhaustive field search. The four first pages returned 3/11, 2/2, 3/10, and 1/1 records respectively; broad query expansion also returned irrelevant results. Only relevant named records were selected. Unread search-result pages are not negative evidence.

All 24 selected PMIDs below had their citation metadata and complete abstracts retrieved through the connected PubMed service in this pass; no selected PMID was missing. The fresh abstract checks supplement the earlier [awareness intake](../../sources/awareness-update-intake.md), whose fuller claim checks retain their own scope. Author and indexing lists were capped in these lookups; no complete indexing inventory is claimed.

BrainNet’s rights-eligible PMC body was retrieved in two contiguous parts: offsets 0–17,999 and 18,000–38,602, totaling 38,603 characters. Selected methods, results, and discussion were inspected for its actual communication channel, recipient interpretation, task, hardware dependence, and limitations. The Konkoly lucid-dreaming body was retrieved in two contiguous parts, totaling 55,773 characters; the optical example, response denominator, differing team procedures, and experience/report limitations were checked. No figures, code, or participant data were independently reanalyzed. A batched full-text request failed; a smaller retry succeeded for BrainNet. Two attempts to obtain the Raz paper’s full text through the connector returned internal errors. Its entry therefore remains an abstract-level check; these errors do not establish that the source is absent or incorrect.

Fresh checks of the other 22 entries are **metadata/abstract checks**, not claims of newly completed full-text review. Private tool-response receipts are retained outside the public repository. No new experiments, simulations, participant recruitment, or causal reanalysis were performed. Independent replication was not systematically assessed except where a specific replication source is explicitly paired below.

## Sources and permitted scope

<a id="s01"></a>
### S01 — Subjective invisibility and delayed discrimination

Franco-Martínez et al. (2026), [Replicating the unconscious working memory effect: a multisite Registered Report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41561361/). DOI: `10.1093/nc/niaf046`. **Primary multisite study; abstract checked here, selected full-text checks in the awareness intake.** Reports above-chance delayed orientation choices on unseen-rated trials in 531 complete participants across 19 laboratories. The authors’ unconscious-working-memory interpretation is distinct from the measured responses and must retain its awareness assumptions.

<a id="s02"></a>
### S02 — Why post hoc invisibility selection is disputed

Schmidt et al. (2026), [Unconscious cognition without post hoc selection artifacts: From selective analysis to functional dissociations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545763/). DOI: `10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6`. **Methodological argument with empirical examples; abstract checked here.** Challenges treating zero-visibility trial selection as isolation of zero sensitivity. This is an objection to an inference method, not proof that every unconscious-processing effect is an artifact.

<a id="s03"></a>
### S03 — Low perceived flicker with an EEG response

Hansen et al. (2024), [Light-based gamma entrainment with novel invisible spectral flicker stimuli](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-024-75448-4`. **Primary human study; abstract checked here, selected full-text checks in the awareness intake.** Reports frequency-specific responses and improved comfort/perceived flicker. “Almost imperceptible” does not establish absent awareness of all stimulus dimensions or message comprehension.

<a id="s04"></a>
### S04 — Optical responses during sleep

Hainke et al. (2025), [40 Hz visual stimulation during sleep evokes neuronal gamma activity in NREM and REM stages](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39700417/). DOI: `10.1093/sleep/zsae299`. **Primary proof-of-concept; abstract checked here, full-text checks in the awareness intake.** Thirty healthy participants had control and experimental nights. Frequency-specific EEG responses establish physiological engagement under that protocol, not an encoded-message endpoint. Fixed night order and blinding limits remain relevant to ancillary sleep-quality conclusions.

<a id="s05"></a>
### S05 — Engineered infrared vision

Ma et al. (2025), [Near-infrared spatiotemporal color vision in humans enabled by upconversion contact lenses](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/). DOI: `10.1016/j.cell.2025.04.019`. **Primary engineering/behavioral study; abstract checked.** Human discrimination depended on the wearable upconversion receiver. It does not establish the same channel without that receiver or during ordinary sleep.

<a id="s06"></a>
### S06 — Targeted memory reactivation synthesis

Hu et al. (2020), [Promoting memory consolidation during sleep: A meta-analysis of targeted memory reactivation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027149/). DOI: `10.1037/bul0000223`. **Meta-analysis; abstract checked.** Combines 91 experiments, 212 effect sizes, N = 2,004; pooled Hedges’ g = 0.29, 95% CI [0.21, 0.38]. Supports a bounded consolidation effect, with stage/task dependence. This synthesis is not an extra experiment and is not evidence for broad new instruction learning.

<a id="s07"></a>
### S07 — Sleep-acquired tone–odor associations

Arzi et al. (2012), [Humans can learn new information during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22922782/). DOI: `10.1038/nn.3193`. **Primary human study; abstract checked.** Reports learned tone-related sniff differences during sleep and subsequent wakefulness, without later awareness of the learning process. That last measure is not direct proof of absent experience at encoding. No language-comprehension endpoint was established.

<a id="s08"></a>
### S08 — Auditory association with later image-choice transfer

Koroma et al. (2022), [Learning New Vocabulary Implicitly During Sleep Transfers With Cross-Modal Generalization Into Wakefulness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35356055/). DOI: `10.3389/fnins.2022.801666`. **Primary human study; abstract checked.** N = 22 analyzed; auditory word–sound pairings were followed by waking image choices. Restricted associative transfer is the endpoint. Cross-modal testing does not establish visual input during sleep or unrestricted vocabulary acquisition.

<a id="s09"></a>
### S09 — Prompted dream incorporation

Haar Horowitz et al. (2020), [Dormio: A targeted dream incubation device](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32480292/). DOI: `10.1016/j.concog.2020.102938`. **Primary device/protocol validation; abstract checked.** Reports serial auditory incubation around sleep onset with incorporation into reported dream content. Detailed dream scripting, independence from the report procedure, and durable behavioral effects are separate questions.

<a id="s10"></a>
### S10 — Lucid REM dialogue

Konkoly et al. (2021), [Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/). DOI: `10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026`. **Primary multi-group study; complete body retrieved, selected sections checked here.** Reports 29 correct answers in 158 attempts during signal-verified lucid dreams, across six of 36 individuals; 60.1% of attempts produced no response. The groups used different sensory/task arrangements. One example used a visually presented Morse-coded arithmetic problem. Neither four optical replications nor ordinary unconscious sleep communication follows.

<a id="s11"></a>
### S11 — Transient verbal-task responsiveness during sleep

Türker et al. (2023), [Behavioral and brain responses to verbal stimuli reveal transient periods of cognitive integration of the external world during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37828228/). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-023-01449-7`. **Primary human study; abstract checked.** Forty-nine napping participants, including 27 with narcolepsy, used previously instructed facial responses in a lexical task. Windows of responsiveness do not establish continuous access, arbitrary instruction learning, or absent subjective experience.

<a id="s12"></a>
### S12 — Conditioned immune endpoints

Goebel et al. (2002), [Behavioral conditioning of immunosuppression is possible in humans](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12468450/). DOI: `10.1096/fj.02-0389com`. **Primary double-blind placebo-controlled human study; abstract checked.** Prior drug–flavor pairing was followed by changes in specified immune measures on cue re-exposure. No arbitrary behavioral-control or clinical drug-replacement endpoint follows from this demonstration.

<a id="s13"></a>
### S13 — Selected suggestion and Stroop conflict

Raz, Fan, and Posner (2005), [Hypnotic suggestion reduces conflict in the human brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15994228/). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.0503064102`. **Primary human task study; abstract checked; new full-text retrieval failed.** Reports suggestion-related neural and task changes in highly hypnotizable participants relative to specified controls. It does not resolve the mechanism of involuntariness or establish general loss of will.

<a id="s14"></a>
### S14 — Competing hypnosis theories

Kirsch and Lynn (1998), [Dissociation theories of hypnosis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9461855/). DOI: `10.1037/0033-2909.123.1.100`. **Theory review; abstract checked.** Describes and evaluates neodissociation and dissociated-control accounts. The existence of a theory is not evidence that its proposed mechanism is established; it is not an independent experiment.

<a id="s15"></a>
### S15 — Social evaluation after sleep cueing

Zhao et al. (2025), [Updating social evaluation during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40885761/). DOI: `10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9`. **Primary human study; abstract checked here; fuller discussion in the supplied awareness review.** Reports changed evaluations of face representations associated with cued names. This is not evidence of durable population behavior. Reactivation versus new learning and the control/durability limits identified in the supplied review remain open; that review’s access labels are not new independent full-text checks.

<a id="s16"></a>
### S16 — Positive counterbias TMR report

Hu et al. (2015), [Unlearning implicit social biases during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26023137/). DOI: `10.1126/science.aaa3841`. **Primary human study; abstract checked.** Reports cue-related benefits after counterstereotype training in 40 participants, including a one-week endpoint. Must be considered with the direct replication below; task-based bias measures are not synonymous with later social conduct.

<a id="s17"></a>
### S17 — Direct failure to replicate S16

Humiston and Wamsley (2019), [Unlearning implicit social biases during sleep: A failure to replicate](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30682167/). DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0211416`. **Primary direct replication; abstract checked.** N = 31; no cueing benefit immediately or one week later. It weakens confidence in the original procedure. It does not directly replicate or refute the later, different Zhao study.

<a id="s18"></a>
### S18 — Semantic decoding with fMRI

Tang et al. (2023), [Semantic reconstruction of continuous language from non-invasive brain recordings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37127759/). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-023-01304-9`. **Primary decoding study; abstract checked.** Recovers aspects of meaning under an instrumented trained-decoder setup; the study found cooperation necessary during both training and application. This is not an arbitrary remote thought-reading or semantic-writing result.

<a id="s19"></a>
### S19 — Plausibility can exceed recovered information

Shirakawa et al. (2025), [Spurious reconstruction from brain activity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40499302/). DOI: `10.1016/j.neunet.2025.107515`. **Primary methodological case study/analysis; abstract checked.** Identifies generalization and generative-prior problems in selected visual reconstruction pipelines. It is a counterexample to using realism as proof of recovered detail, not proof that all neural decoding is spurious.

<a id="s20"></a>
### S20 — Instrumented binary brain-to-brain communication

Jiang et al. (2019), [BrainNet: A Multi-Person Brain-to-Brain Interface for Direct Collaboration Between Brains](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30992474/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-019-41895-7`. **Primary human engineering study; complete body retrieved, selected methods/results/discussion checked.** Five triads used EEG decisions, networked computers, and TMS-induced visual cues in a binary game. Recipient interpretation and an agreed task mapping were integral. The authors distinguish their binary information channel from proposed richer content transfer. No independent reanalysis of accuracy or bandwidth was performed here.

<a id="s21"></a>
### S21 — An implanted magnetic receiver

Woods et al. (2024), [Miniature battery-free epidural cortical stimulators](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38608028/). DOI: `10.1126/sciadv.adn0858`. **Primary engineering study; abstract checked.** Acute human motor-cortex activation and 30-day porcine operation depended on an implanted receiver and external transmitter. Neither chronic human efficacy nor ordinary uninstrumented magnetic communication is established by that result.

<a id="s22"></a>
### S22 — Human misinformation and neural patterns

Shao et al. (2023), [Cross-stage neural pattern similarity in the hippocampus predicts false memory derived from post-event inaccurate information](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37085518/). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-023-38046-y`. **Primary human experiment; abstract checked.** Behavioral misinformation effects accompany neural pattern relationships. The pattern correlations support an interpretation of reconstructive memory; they are not a causal demonstration of every proposed neural operation or arbitrary autobiographical replacement.

<a id="s23"></a>
### S23 — Causal engineered memory association in mice

Ramirez et al. (2013), [Creating a false memory in the hippocampus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23888038/). DOI: `10.1126/science.1239073`. **Primary animal experiment; abstract checked.** Tagged hippocampal cells and optical manipulation produced context-specific fear behavior. The evidence is substantial within its engineered mouse preparation; translation to detailed human memory writing is a further hypothesis.

<a id="s24"></a>
### S24 — Drug effects on memory and monitoring

Mintzer et al. (2010), [Dose effects of triazolam and scopolamine on metamemory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20158291/). DOI: `10.1037/a0018061`. **Primary controlled human study; abstract checked.** Reports episodic-memory and monitoring/control changes. Its measured outcomes do not establish reliable obedience or selective installation of goals.

## Interpretation rules

1. Replication status belongs to an exact endpoint and protocol. A failure in one task is not a refutation of a different task; a positive result elsewhere does not repair that failure.
2. Abstract checks support the narrow statements above. Stronger numerical, safety, efficacy, or mechanistic claims require the relevant methods, results, and subsequent literature.
3. Shared vocabulary is not a mechanism: “memory,” “unconscious,” “control,” and “communication” refer to different operational endpoints across these sources.
4. No cross-field bridge or science-fiction scenario has been experimentally validated by this documentation task. The network records what would discriminate an extension from a superficial analogy.
