### What the evidence supports

Light can carry a measurable neural signal without conspicuous flicker. Closed-eyelid stimulation can also elicit sleep responses. Neither endpoint establishes participant comprehension. [Norton et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12807); [Hainke et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsae299).

The imperceptible property must be specified. Visible targets with unnoticed modulation, unrecognized codes, unperceived words, and invisible wavelengths are different cases. Near-infrared upconversion lenses, for example, make a carrier perceptible through a conversion device. [Ma et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.04.019).

Above-chance behavior on “unseen” trials is a replicable observation in some paradigms. Its interpretation remains conditional on reporting criteria, relevant-feature measurement, and statistical assumptions. [Franco-Martínez et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf046); [Schmidt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6).

Lucid-dream communication demonstrates meaningful responding during sleep, including a trained optical example. It does not establish absent conscious experience. [Konkoly et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026).

No reviewed study establishes the full combination of unaided optical reception, arbitrary-message comprehension, delayed retention, and absent experience during ordinary non-lucid sleep. This is a bounded literature finding, not proof that every restricted version is impossible.

### Measurement and inference rules

Distinguish awareness of light, modulation, pattern, code, meaning, experimental regularity, and later recollection. Perceptual awareness, confidence, and memory answer different questions. In sleep, physiological stage, arousal, dream content, lucidity, and later recall also require separate measures.

A participant who reliably chooses a code's meaning already shows objective content access. The further question can concern absent **subjective experience**, or an **indirect-task advantage** over direct reporting under explicit assumptions. Success on one choice task plus failure on an ostensibly identical “awareness” task demands investigation of timing, attention, and measurement differences. [Reingold & Merikle, 1988](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207490); [Schmidt & Vorberg, 2006](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193692).

Nonsignificant awareness does not demonstrate absence. Use intervals, justified equivalence bounds, model/prior sensitivity, and enough observations per participant. Do not select low scorers and reuse their noisy scores as proof of unawareness. Preregistration makes decisions inspectable; it does not eliminate selection bias. [Shanks, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1170-y); [Lakens, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177).

Every positive result should be labeled by achievement: physical delivery, evoked response, neural discrimination, participant discrimination, access to learned meaning, new association learning, retention, or transfer. An EEG classifier's access is not automatically the participant's access. A new association to a familiar concept is narrower than acquiring a new concept or understanding an arbitrary sentence.

### Highest-priority next test

A preregistered conceptual sequence should validate the same simple code with eyes open, eyes closed while awake, and separately staged sleep. Counterbalance meanings; distinguish trained associations from new learning; use optical-path and leakage controls; test participant behavior independently of neural decoding; report all randomized opportunities, including arousals and missing responses. Compare results against specified weak-perception, criterion, and rare-awareness models.

Awareness constraints must match the information used in the main task. If contemporaneous experience cannot be measured adequately during sleep, state the narrower conclusion: processing during physiologically scored sleep with specified arousal and recall findings.
