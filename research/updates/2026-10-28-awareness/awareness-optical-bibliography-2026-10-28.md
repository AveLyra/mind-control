# Awareness and optical information — annotated bibliography

**New literature update. Search cutoff: 28 October 2026.**

This selected bibliography supports the accompanying critical review and evidence matrix. It identifies newly checked literature; it does **not** recover the unidentified original citation *turn3file2*, establish a mapping to an original upload, or recreate missing source files. Those provenance questions remain unresolved.

The 53 numbered records prioritize primary experiments and methodological papers. Secondary reviews and reanalyses are labeled explicitly. Article titles retain authors' terminology; labels such as “unconscious” or “invisible” are not accepted as conclusions without examining their operational definitions. Short annotations identify the source's role and principal interpretive limit.

## Access and version key

- **FT:** the full article body was available and relevant sections were inspected. This does not mean every supplement, dataset, or analysis was independently audited.
- **PFT:** only selected or indexed full-text sections were inspected, or a manuscript was only partly accessible. Important inaccessible fields remain unknown.
- **A:** claims used were limited to abstract and bibliographic metadata. A linked full-text location may exist without having been fully inspected.
- **Version:** published journal version is canonical unless another inspected version is specified. Online-first and issue dates are distinguished where verified. PMID is supplied only when independently verified; omission means not verified here, not necessarily unindexed. Long author lists use “et al.” after verified named authors.

Links lead directly to publishers, PubMed/PMC, or identified primary manuscripts. DOI links provide persistent identification. Some full-text locations intermittently returned access blocks; presence of a link is not a claim that unrestricted access was obtained. This is a targeted critical review, not an exhaustive systematic-search census.

## Awareness measurement, statistical inference, and methodological reanalysis

**1. Reingold, E. M., & Merikle, P. M. (1988). Using direct and indirect measures to study perception without awareness.** *Perception & Psychophysics, 44(6), 563–575*.

DOI: [10.3758/BF03207490](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207490); PMID: [3200674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3200674/). **Primary method/theory; A.** [Abstract and metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3200674/).

Develops matched direct–indirect comparisons, clarifying why an inference of unconscious processing depends on the awareness test's sensitivity.

**2. Schmidt, T., & Vorberg, D. (2006). Criteria for unconscious cognition: Three types of dissociation.** *Perception & Psychophysics, 68(3), 489–504*.

DOI: [10.3758/BF03193692](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193692); PMID: [16900839](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16900839/). **Primary method/theory; PFT.** [Publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03193692.pdf).

Separates simple, sensitivity, and double dissociations; their evidential force depends on distinct assumptions about measurement, scaling, and functional relationships.

**3. Ramsøy, T. Z., & Overgaard, M. (2004). Introspection and subliminal perception.** *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences, 3, 1–23*.

DOI: [10.1023/B:PHEN.0000041900.30172.e8](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:PHEN.0000041900.30172.e8). **Primary empirical/method; A.** [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:PHEN.0000041900.30172.e8).

Foundational graded perceptual-awareness scale; experiential categories require clear instructions and are not automatically equal intervals or comparable across participants.

**4. Sandberg, K., Timmermans, B., Overgaard, M., & Cleeremans, A. (2010). Measuring consciousness: Is one measure better than the other?.** *Consciousness and Cognition, 19, 1069–1078*.

DOI: [10.1016/j.concog.2009.12.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2009.12.013); PMID: [20133167](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20133167/). **Primary empirical/method; FT.** [Author-hosted full text](https://axc.ulb.be/uploads/2015/11/10-ccsandbergetal.pdf). Author-hosted article-in-press PDF inspected; published DOI is canonical.

Compares awareness ratings, confidence, and wagering; their relationships to identification accuracy differ, and low confidence need not mean absent experience.

**5. Maniscalco, B., & Lau, H. (2012). A signal detection theoretic approach for estimating metacognitive sensitivity from confidence ratings.** *Consciousness and Cognition, 21(1), 422–430*.

DOI: [10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021); PMID: [22071269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22071269/). **Primary method; A.** [Abstract and metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22071269/).

Introduces meta-d′ to quantify confidence discrimination between correct and incorrect responses; this measures metacognitive sensitivity rather than content awareness directly.

**6. Fleming, S. M. (2017). HMeta-d: hierarchical Bayesian estimation of metacognitive efficiency from confidence ratings.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2017(1), nix007*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/nix007](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/nix007); PMID: [29877507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29877507/). **Primary method; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5858026/).

Models uncertainty in hierarchical metacognitive-efficiency estimates; sparse data and near-zero first-order sensitivity require caution before interpreting low estimated efficiency.

**7. Shanks, D. R. (2017; online 2016). Regressive research: The pitfalls of post hoc data selection in the study of unconscious mental processes.** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 752–775*.

DOI: [10.3758/s13423-016-1170-y](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1170-y); PMID: [27753047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27753047/). **Primary method/simulation; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5486877/).

Shows how selecting supposedly unaware participants using noisy scores can create apparent unconscious effects through regression to the mean.

**8. Yaron, I., Zeevi, Y., Korisky, U., Marshall, W., & Mudrik, L. (2024; version of record 1 August 2023). Progressing, not regressing: A possible solution to the problem of regression to the mean in unconscious processing studies.** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 31(1), 49–64*.

DOI: [10.3758/s13423-023-02326-x](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-023-02326-x); PMID: [37528278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37528278/). **Primary method/simulation and reanalysis; FT.** [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02326-x).

Confirms measurement-error concerns while proposing a nonparametric diagnostic; solutions differ in power, specificity, and assumptions and do not validate awareness constructs.

**9. Goldstein, A., Sklar, A. Y., & Siegelman, N. (2022; online first 24 February). Accurately Measuring Nonconscious Processing Using a Generative Bayesian Framework.** *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice, 9(4), 336–355*.

DOI: [10.1037/cns0000316](https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000316). **Primary method/model; FT.** [Publisher online-first PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/cns-cns0000316.pdf).

Jointly models awareness and indirect processing with measurement error; conclusions remain conditional on generative assumptions, identifiability, and prior choices.

**10. Vadillo, M. A., Malejka, S., Lee, D. Y. H., Dienes, Z., & Shanks, D. R. (2022; online 15 June 2021). Raising awareness about measurement error in research on unconscious mental processes.** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 29(1), 21–43*.

DOI: [10.3758/s13423-021-01923-y](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-021-01923-y); PMID: [34131891](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131891/). **Primary method/model plus secondary psychometric meta-analysis; PFT.** [Publisher full-text location](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01923-y).

Shows that unreliable measurements can obscure performance–awareness relationships; weak observed correlations need not imply independent underlying conscious and unconscious processes.

**11. Lakens, D. (2017). Equivalence Tests: A Practical Primer for t Tests, Correlations, and Meta-Analyses.** *Social Psychological and Personality Science, 8(4), 355–362*.

DOI: [10.1177/1948550617697177](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177); PMID: [28736600](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28736600/). **Primary statistical method/tutorial; A.** [Full-text location; abstract used](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/).

Explains prespecified equivalence bounds and two one-sided tests; supports excluding meaningful effects within bounds, not proving an exactly zero effect.

**12. Meyen, S., Zerweck, I. A., Amado, C., von Luxburg, U., & Franz, V. H. (2022; online 2021). Advancing research on unconscious priming: When can scientists claim an indirect task advantage?.** *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 151(1), 65–81*.

DOI: [10.1037/xge0001065](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001065); PMID: [34264714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34264714/). **Primary method/empirical work plus reanalysis; A.** [Author PDF located; not fully inspected](https://www.tml.cs.uni-tuebingen.de/team/luxburg/publications/MeyenEtal2021.pdf).

Advocates comparing direct and indirect sensitivity on compatible scales; separate significance decisions do not establish an indirect-task advantage.

**13. Schmidt, T., Wolkersdorfer, M. P., Lee, X. Y., & Jubran, O. (2026; 16 January). Unconscious cognition without post hoc selection artifacts: From selective analysis to functional dissociations.** *Behavior Research Methods, 58, 39*.

DOI: [10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6); PMID: [41545763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545763/). **Primary method/theory with empirical illustrations; FT.** [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6).

Critiques selecting lowest-visibility trials and favors full functional comparisons; the critique itself depends on assumptions about decision axes and measurement.

**14. Lublinsky, S., Yaron, I., Marshall, W., & Mudrik, L. (2026; 28 July). Increasing power for detecting awareness: a new approach to test group level objective performance.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2026(1), niag039*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/niag039](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag039); PMID: [42524410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42524410/). **Primary statistical method with reanalysis; FT.** [Publisher full text; selected sections inspected](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niag039/8746109).

Develops a more sensitive group awareness test; detecting residual objective information changes null-test conclusions without automatically identifying its experiential basis.

**15. Streicher, J., Meyen, S., Franz, V. H., & Stein, T. (2025; 11 November). Neural correlates of unconscious processing in functional magnetic resonance imaging: does brain activity contain more information than can be consciously reported?.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2025(1), niaf042*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/niaf042](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf042); PMID: [41230018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41230018/). **Secondary dataset reanalysis with methodological comparison; PFT.** [Publisher full-text location](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf042/8320572).

Reassesses neural versus reported information using common sensitivity comparisons; surviving decoding advantages still do not demonstrate participants' independent use.

**16. Stockart, F., Schreiber, M., Amerio, P., et al. (2025; online 22 July). Studying unconscious processing: Contention and consensus.** *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, advance online publication, 1–77*.

DOI: [10.1017/S0140525X25101489](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X25101489); PMID: [40692329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40692329/). **Secondary consensus/review with methodological recommendations; A.** [Publisher record and article location](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/studying-unconscious-processing-contention-and-consensus/36A74876AEBF472BF7B3597FFB3A4DC4). Accepted target-article version; not an empirical replication.

Collects areas of agreement and continuing dispute about awareness measurement; methodological recommendations are useful but are not independent experimental evidence.

## Waking perception, priming, working memory, and report confounds

**17. Vorberg, D., Mattler, U., Heinecke, A., Schmidt, T., & Schwarzbach, J. (2003). Different time courses for visual perception and action priming.** *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 100(10), 6275–6280*.

DOI: [10.1073/pnas.0931489100](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0931489100); PMID: [12719543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12719543/). **Primary empirical; A.** [Full-text location; abstract/metadata used](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC156362/).

Foundational dissociation between masked-arrow visibility and response priming; later intensive replication tests its robustness without extending results to arbitrary semantics.

**18. Peters, M. A. K., & Lau, H. (2015; corrected 2016). Human observers have optimal introspective access to perceptual processes even for visually masked stimuli.** *eLife, 4, e09651*.

DOI: [10.7554/eLife.09651](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.09651); PMID: [26433023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26433023/). **Primary empirical/model; FT.** [Corrected full text](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09651). Corrected article used; correction DOI 10.7554/eLife.16332.

Masked discrimination and confidence fit a common-access model; mismatched performance scales alone can produce misleading apparent dissociations between perception and awareness.

**19. Frässle, S., Sommer, J., Jansen, A., Naber, M., & Einhäuser, W. (2014). Binocular Rivalry: Frontal Activity Relates to Introspection and Action But Not to Perception.** *Journal of Neuroscience, 34(5), 1738–1747*.

DOI: [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4403-13.2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4403-13.2014); PMID: [24478356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24478356/). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6827584/).

Calibrates eye and pupil proxies against rivalry reports; reduced reporting confounds do not make these proxies valid measures of arbitrary content awareness.

**20. Cohen, M. A., Ortego, K., Kyroudis, A., & Pitts, M. (2020). Distinguishing the Neural Correlates of Perceptual Awareness and Postperceptual Processing.** *Journal of Neuroscience, 40(25), 4925–4935*.

DOI: [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0120-20.2020](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0120-20.2020); PMID: [32409620](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32409620/). **Primary empirical; FT.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7326348/).

Report and no-report conditions separate neural consequences of reporting; absence of overt reporting requires validated proxies and does not establish unawareness.

**21. Berkovitch, L., & Dehaene, S. (2019; online 26 December 2018). Subliminal syntactic priming.** *Cognitive Psychology, 109, 26–46*.

DOI: [10.1016/j.cogpsych.2018.12.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2018.12.001); PMID: [30593889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30593889/). **Primary empirical; FT.** [Author-hosted full text](https://www.unicog.org/publications/Berkovitch%20Dehaene%20Subliminal%20Syntactic%20Priming%20CogPsych2019).

Tests familiar grammatical features using masked words; residual visibility, post hoc exclusions, and later reliability reanalysis constrain unconscious-semantic interpretations.

**22. Zerweck, I. A., Kao, C.-S., Meyen, S., Amado, C., von Eltz, M., Klimm, M., & Franz, V. H. (2021). Number processing outside awareness? Systematically testing sensitivities of direct and indirect measures of consciousness.** *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, 83, 2510–2529*.

DOI: [10.3758/s13414-021-02312-2](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-021-02312-2); PMID: [33973133](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33973133/). **Primary empirical; FT.** [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-021-02312-2). Publisher copyediting correction DOI 10.3758/s13414-021-02332-y.

Matched number-task sensitivity comparisons found no indirect advantage across tested conditions; significant priming alongside nonsignificant discrimination was insufficient evidence.

**23. Micher, N., Mazenko, D., & Lamy, D. (2024). Unconscious Processing Contaminates Objective Measures of Conscious Perception: Evidence From the Liminal Prime Paradigm.** *Journal of Cognition, 7(1)*.

DOI: [10.5334/joc.402](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.402); PMID: [39372100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39372100/). **Primary empirical; preregistered; FT.** [Full text](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/joc.402?tab=article).

Offers a competing interpretation of discrimination on zero-visibility trials; criterion, selection, and response-time assumptions remain central to the proposed dissociation.

**24. Hernández-Gutiérrez, D., Sorrel, M. A., Shanks, D. R., & Vadillo, M. A. (2025; 7 January). The Conscious Side of ‘Subliminal’ Linguistic Priming: A Systematic Review With Meta-Analysis and Reliability Analysis of Visibility Measures.** *Journal of Cognition, 8(1), 13*.

DOI: [10.5334/joc.419](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.419); PMID: [39803184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39803184/). **Secondary systematic review/meta-analysis plus original data reanalysis; FT.** [Full text](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/joc.419?tab=article).

Finds pooled residual visibility and reliability problems in syntactic priming; group-level sensitivity does not identify every participant's or trial's experience.

**25. Jimenez, M., Andrade, C., Hinojosa, J. A., Montoro, P. R., & Prieto, A. (2026; online 27 February; April issue). New approaches to subliminal perception: exploring the unconscious perception of masked primes with Bayesian regression models and General Recognition Theory (GRT).** *Consciousness and Cognition, 140, 104018*.

DOI: [10.1016/j.concog.2026.104018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.104018); PMID: [41762559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41762559/). **Primary empirical/method comparison; PFT.** [Publisher record; abstract and indexed sections](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810026000267).

Different awareness definitions and modeling assumptions yield different conclusions for masked shapes; this newly verified editorial lead does not identify turn3file2.

**26. Franco-Martínez, A., Rey-Sáez, R., Adrián-Ventura, J., et al. (2026; 19 January). Replicating the unconscious working memory effect: a multisite Registered Report.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2026(1), niaf046*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/niaf046](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf046); PMID: [41561361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41561361/). **Primary empirical; multisite Registered Report; FT.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12814970/).

Large preregistered replication supports delayed orientation performance on unseen-rated trials; selection, construct mismatch, and latent-model assumptions remain interpretive objections.

**27. Sánchez-Fuenzalida, N., van Gaal, S., van den Hurk, Z., Stein, T., & Fahrenfort, J. J. (2026; 20 August). Evidence for unconscious priming using a Bayesian single-subject approach.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2026(1), niag052*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/niag052](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag052); PMID: [42626369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626369/). **Primary empirical; intensive conceptual replication; FT.** [Publisher full text](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niag052/8767335).

Intensive individual testing supports restricted arrow priming under selected awareness bounds; task-delay asymmetry and substantial individual variation limit broader inference.

**28. Remiszewski, M., Łacny, J., Cwalina, F., Masłowska, K., Cleeremans, A., & Binder, M. (2026; 26 October). Is it possible to make unconscious inferences? The limits of the influence of unconsciously perceived stimuli on conscious decisions.** *Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2026(1), niag068*.

DOI: [10.1093/nc/niag068](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag068). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Publisher record and indexed sections](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2026/1/niag068/9002258).

Reports limited decision influences with stronger inferential limits; lexical-awareness testing differs from the main semantic task, and independent replication is lacking.

## Optical delivery, imperceptible modulation, and equipment-mediated decoding

**29. Bierman, A., Figueiro, M. G., & Rea, M. S. (2011). Measuring and predicting eyelid spectral transmittance.** *Journal of Biomedical Optics, 16(6), 067011*.

DOI: [10.1117/1.3593151](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.3593151); PMID: [21721832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21721832/). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Publisher PDF](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/journal-of-biomedical-optics/volume-16/issue-6/067011/Measuring-and-predicting-eyelid-spectral-transmittance/10.1117/1.3593151.pdf).

Quantifies wavelength-dependent, individually variable light transmission through eyelids; physical transmission alone supplies no estimate of symbol resolution or semantic access.

**30. Lim, J. H., Hwang, H. J., Han, C. H., Jung, K. Y., & Im, C. H. (2013). Classification of binary intentions for individuals with impaired oculomotor function: 'eyes-closed' SSVEP-based brain-computer interface (BCI).** *Journal of Neural Engineering, 10(2), 026021*.

DOI: [10.1088/1741-2560/10/2/026021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2560/10/2/026021); PMID: [23528484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23528484/). **Primary empirical; A.** [Abstract and metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23528484/). Supplementary-video corrigendum DOI 10.1088/1741-2560/10/3/039501.

Shows intentional closed-eye control of an external classifier; decoded intentions do not establish independent comprehension of unseen incoming messages.

**31. Alais, D., Locke, S. M., Leung, J., & Van der Burg, E. (2016; 5 July). No attentional capture from invisible flicker.** *Scientific Reports, 6, 29296*.

DOI: [10.1038/srep29296](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep29296); PMID: [27377759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27377759/). **Primary empirical; conceptual replication/null; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4932510/).

Finds no attentional-capture benefit in the tested invisible-flicker conditions; this bounded null does not establish that every behavioral influence is impossible.

**32. Norton, J. J. S., Umunna, S., & Bretl, T. (2017; online 18 January). The elicitation of steady-state visual evoked potentials during sleep.** *Psychophysiology, 54(4), 496–507*.

DOI: [10.1111/psyp.12807](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12807); PMID: [28098351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/). **Primary empirical; A.** [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psyp.12807).

Demonstrates sleep visual evoked responses through closed eyelids; awareness, learned-code discrimination, and semantic communication were not established by this endpoint.

**33. Sharon, O., & Nir, Y. (2018; online 2017). Attenuated Fast Steady-State Visual Evoked Potentials During Human Sleep.** *Cerebral Cortex, 28(4), 1297–1311*.

DOI: [10.1093/cercor/bhx043](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhx043); PMID: [28334175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334175/). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Publisher full-text location](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/28/4/1297/3052755).

Shows substantial state- and frequency-dependent attenuation during sleep; preserved responses indicate physiological processing, without evidence of participant access to meaning.

**34. Brickwedde, M., Bezsudnova, Y., Kowalczyk, A., Jensen, O., & Zhigalov, A. (2022). Application of rapid invisible frequency tagging for brain computer interfaces.** *Journal of Neuroscience Methods, 382, 109726*.

DOI: [10.1016/j.jneumeth.2022.109726](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2022.109726); PMID: [36228894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36228894/). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615063/).

Uses invisible frequency tagging to support deliberate attention-based machine decoding; the target and the target's temporal modulation have different awareness requirements.

**35. Ming, G., Pei, W., Gao, X., & Wang, Y. (2023). A high-performance SSVEP-based BCI using imperceptible flickers.** *Journal of Neural Engineering, 20(1), 016042*.

DOI: [10.1088/1741-2552/acb50e](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/acb50e); PMID: [36669202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36669202/). **Primary empirical; FT.** [Author-uploaded primary full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367308786_A_high-performance_SSVEP-based_BCI_using_imperceptible_flickers).

Reports effective external decoding with reduced flicker visibility; postexperiment ratings do not establish zero modulation awareness or independent semantic reception.

**36. Hansen, L. S., Carstensen, M. H., Henney, M. A., et al. (2024; 29 November). Light-based gamma entrainment with novel invisible spectral flicker stimuli.** *Scientific Reports, 14, 29747*.

DOI: [10.1038/s41598-024-75448-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-75448-4); PMID: [39613776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/). **Primary empirical; PFT.** [Full-text location](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11606973/).

Spectral modulation evokes neural responses, but visibility ratings exceed continuous-light controls; a technology's invisibility label is not an awareness equivalence test.

**37. Hainke, L., Dowsett, J., Spitschan, M., & Priller, J. (2025; online 19 December 2024). 40 Hz visual stimulation during sleep evokes neuronal gamma activity in NREM and REM stages.** *Sleep, 48(3), zsae299*.

DOI: [10.1093/sleep/zsae299](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsae299); PMID: [39700417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39700417/). **Primary empirical; preregistration reported; FT.** [Publisher full text](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/48/3/zsae299/7928860).

## Deduplication and independence

- Publisher, PubMed, PMC, author-uploaded, and institutional copies of one DOI are one work. Online-first dates, later issue dates, accepted manuscripts, and production updates do not create independent evidence.
- Jimenez's matching preprint and 2026 article are one report; Remiszewski's preprint and October 2026 article are one report. Schmidig's reviewed-preprint versions and version of record are one study. Corrections are linked to their parent studies, not counted as additional experiments.
- Cicero's February 2026 preprint (PMID 41684934) and June 2026 journal article (PMID 42270639) describe one study; the published article is the record used here.
- Hernández-Gutiérrez's systematic review reuses the Berkovitch data and other existing experiments. Meyen, Streicher, Yaron, Vadillo, Lublinsky, and Schmidt include methodological reanalysis or simulation; their reanalyzed datasets must not be added to original participant totals. The bibliography therefore reports no global pooled sample size.
- Multiple tasks, sessions, or experiments within a paper may reuse people. Peters' experiments partly overlap; Sánchez-Fuenzalida's intensive sessions reuse the same participants. A new analysis or a second session is not an independent replication.
- Züst's original learning data were reused in later analyses identified during the search; those analyses were not counted as new replication cohorts. Hu's 2015 experiment and Humiston's 2019 replication are distinct studies. Schmidig follows the Henke/Ruch research program with a changed protocol; Koroma offers an independent conceptual extension, not an exact protocol replication.
- The four teams in Konkoly's combined paper used heterogeneous tasks and modalities. Their combined result is not four independent demonstrations of optical semantic communication. Related spectral-flicker studies share investigators and commercial ties; sample independence beyond reported descriptions was not completely audited.

## Material limits

Several supplied optical/sleep papers were accessible only as abstracts or partial indexed text. Uninspected sample details, confidence intervals, exclusions, preregistration timestamps, and correction contents were not filled in from inference. Search results, earlier assistant answers, and secondary summaries served as discovery leads; substantive conclusions rely on the identified primary records where inspected. No experiments, participant interactions, or repository publication were performed.
