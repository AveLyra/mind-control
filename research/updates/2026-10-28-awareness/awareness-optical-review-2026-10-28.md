# Awareness and optical information
## New literature update — 28 October 2026

**Search cutoff:** 28 October 2026. This is a new critical literature review, not recovery of an earlier citation or uploaded file.

**Main conclusion.** Human visual systems can respond to optical modulation that is poorly noticed, and light through closed eyelids can evoke responses during sleep. Some behavioral dissociations between performance and reported awareness are also reproducible. These observations do not, by themselves, identify the information as wholly unconscious or establish an unaided channel for understanding arbitrary optical messages. The defensible claim depends on the particular feature, task, state, awareness measure, and uncertainty bound.

This update includes the [study evidence table](awareness-optical-evidence-2026-10-28.md), [annotated bibliography](awareness-optical-bibliography-2026-10-28.md), and [proposed wiki addition](wiki-awareness-addition-2026-10-28.md). The sections below supply the reasoning, methodological comparison, gaps, and conceptual preregistration.

## 1. What is established, contested, inferred, or unknown?

| Assessment | Conclusion | Strongest objection or limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Established within studied conditions** | Frequency-tagged neural responses and equipment-mediated target selection can occur with low reported flicker visibility. | The illuminated object can remain visible; an external decoder's success does not demonstrate the participant's access to an incoming hidden message. [Brickwedde et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2022.109726); [Ming et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/acb50e). |
| **Established physiological finding** | Optical stimulation through closed eyelids can affect sleeping-brain activity. | Scored sleep and an evoked response establish neither absence of experience nor semantic comprehension. State and frequency matter. [Norton et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12807); [Sharon & Nir, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhx043); [Hainke et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsae299). |
| **Reproducible observation; contested interpretation** | People sometimes perform above chance on visual trials they rate as unseen. A 2026 multisite Registered Report strengthens that observation. | Residual features, reporting criteria, selection, and mismatched awareness tasks remain possible. Conversely, above-chance discrimination is not necessarily consciousness-specific. [Franco-Martínez et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf046); [Micher et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.402). |
| **Positive, conditional evidence** | Some priming experiments show stronger indirect than direct sensitivity or different functional dependencies. | Interpretation requires defensible assumptions about task sensitivity and measurement; it does not license transfer to unfamiliar codes or sleep. [Sánchez-Fuenzalida et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag052); [Schmidt & Vorberg, 2006](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193692). |
| **Established proof of concept** | Meaningful communication can occur during some lucid REM dreams, including a trained optical-code example. | Dreamers can consciously experience the signal. Successes pooled across sensory modalities are not an optical-channel success rate. [Konkoly et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026). |
| **Restricted positive findings; transfer unknown** | Some auditory sleep experiments support associations, later categorization, or task-dependent responses. | Prior training, arousal, forgotten experience, selection, and consolidation must be separated. Auditory input cannot validate optical reception. [Koroma et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.801666); [Türker et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01449-7). |
| **Reasonable research inference** | A previously learned optical cue is a more tractable first test than a new sentence or new meaning. | This is a proposal about fewer required processing steps, not evidence that unconscious optical cueing already works. |
| **Missing direct evidence in this search** | A reliable, unaided, entirely unexperienced optical channel that lets ordinary non-lucid sleepers independently understand and retain arbitrary messages. | The search is not exhaustive; restricted future demonstrations remain possible. Absence of a demonstrated channel is not proof of physical or cognitive impossibility. |

A useful result should be described at its demonstrated level. “Information” in a neural signal, successful perception, comprehension, and durable learning are different outcomes.

## 2. Define the object and time of awareness

Awareness is not a single checkbox. The following operational distinctions are the review's framework, informed by direct/indirect measurement theory and the sleep experiments.

| Object of assessment | Suitable question | What a negative answer leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus presence | Was there any light or visual event? | Awareness of another feature; task misunderstanding; insufficient sensitivity. |
| Modulation | Did brightness or color vary? | A visible steady-looking target; different mean brightness or color; content perceived through another cue. |
| Spatial or temporal pattern | Which arrangement, rhythm, or sequence was presented? | Awareness of fragments that help the main task without supporting complete identification. |
| Symbol or learned code | Which trained token was it? | Perception of the carrier without recognizing its conventional label. |
| Meaning | Which referent, category, relation, or proposition did it convey? | Perception of the code without comprehension, or semantic fragments without full reportability. |
| Experimental regularity or hypothesis | Did the participant know what was being manipulated? | Fully conscious perception of every stimulus despite ignorance of the study's purpose. |
| Metacognitive access | Can confidence distinguish correct from incorrect decisions? | First-order information with weak insight; reporting or confidence-scale differences. |
| Later memory | Can the participant remember or recognize the earlier event? | A consciously experienced event that was forgotten. |

**Perceptual awareness** concerns experience at presentation. **Metacognitive reporting** concerns judging that experience or a decision. **Recall** concerns later retrieval. These can dissociate; confidence and visibility instructions must not be silently exchanged. [Sandberg et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2009.12.013); [Maniscalco & Lau, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021).

During sleep, independently record physiological stage, brief arousals, responsiveness, dream experience, lucidity, and later recall. Lucidity means recognizing that one is dreaming; a non-lucid dream can still contain conscious experiences. “No awakening detected,” “no lucidity reported,” and “nothing remembered next morning” answer different questions. Neither a sleep stage nor a later memory failure establishes absence of experience at encoding. [Konkoly et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026); [Türker et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01449-7).

## 3. Awareness methods and their limits

| Method | What it measures well | Main limitation | Best use here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Binary seen/unseen report | A simple subjective classification | Criterion shifts; weak glimpses forced into two bins | Supplement a graded, feature-specific report and catch trials. |
| Perceptual Awareness Scale (PAS) | Graded reported clarity, including weak experience | Ordinal categories are not equal intervals; instructions and thresholds vary; lowest-category selection is not a sensitivity estimate | Train comprehensible anchors; retain all categories; specify whether rating concerns light, code, or meaning. |
| Confidence | Belief that the task response is correct | Low confidence need not mean no experience; can incorporate inference, memory, or postdecision evidence | Collect separately from visibility and analyze calibration. |
| Detection with absent trials | Sensitivity to presence and response criterion | Does not automatically measure the identity or semantic feature driving behavior | Balance presence/absence and include plausible partial-cue conditions. |
| Forced-choice discrimination | Ability to distinguish specified alternatives | Tests available information, not consciousness exclusively; task demands can miss consciously perceived fragments | Match the alternatives to the claimed transmitted information and verify task competence. |
| Identification or open report | Specific content accessible for explicit use | Harder than coarse categorization; language, memory, and response burden can create false negatives | Useful for message claims, accompanied by easier matched feature tests. |
| Signal detection d′ and criterion | Separation of response distributions under an explicit model | Distributional assumptions, lapses, unequal variances, and sparse extreme cells matter | Report sensitivity and bias separately, with intervals and a stated estimator. |
| Meta-d′ and metacognitive efficiency | Confidence discrimination between correct and incorrect judgments, adjusted for first-order performance | Model-dependent; ratios become unstable when first-order sensitivity is near zero | Estimate hierarchically with uncertainty; do not treat low meta-d′ as proof of no experience. |
| No-report physiological proxies | Reduces neural activity caused by overt reporting and response preparation | Proxy validity is task-specific; no report does not mean no awareness | Calibrate against reports in matched conditions; treat proxies as converging evidence. |
| Sleep staging plus experience sampling | Physiological state and sampled reports of experience | Scalp staging can miss local/transient events; awakening alters the state; later reports can forget content | Combine blinded staging, event-level arousal analysis, and prompt dream/content questions. |

PAS originated as an introspective tool; it is not an objective detector of unconsciousness. Confidence and wagering comparisons show that the choice of report changes conclusions. Meta-d′ was developed to quantify metacognitive sensitivity, while hierarchical extensions improve estimation without removing construct assumptions. [Ramsøy & Overgaard, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:PHEN.0000041900.30172.e8); [Sandberg et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2009.12.013); [Maniscalco & Lau, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021); [Fleming, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/nix007).

For equal-variance yes/no signal detection, d′ = Φ⁻¹(hit rate) − Φ⁻¹(false-alarm rate); criterion should also be reported. Two-alternative forced-choice accuracy uses a different conversion under its corresponding model. A sensitivity statistic does not acquire a consciousness-specific interpretation merely by being criterion-adjusted.

No-report studies help separate perceptual activity from reporting and action. They do not make a neural marker an infallible measure of experience. In particular, absence or presence of P3b, a pupil response, or a frequency-locked response should not stand alone as a test of semantic awareness. [Frässle et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4403-13.2014); [Cohen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0120-20.2020).

### How closely must the awareness task match?

It must be sensitive to **every consciously available cue sufficient to explain the main effect**, without imposing an extra bottleneck. Literal identity of two tasks is often impossible: a priming task and a direct report ask for different responses. The burden is therefore to demonstrate comparability and test the likely asymmetries.

Match stimulus energy and contrast, masking, adaptation, eye position, attention, code alternatives, relevant feature, temporal context, training, and response delay as closely as practicable. Changing a visible target into a blank screen, asking a harder semantic question, or delaying the awareness response can change what is measurable. A whole-word identification failure does not rule out a consciously perceived initial letter or category cue.

Concurrent reports reduce memory delay but can divide attention or change strategy. Separate blocks avoid that interference but introduce state and practice drift. Randomized interleaving, alternated blocks, easy positive controls, and analyses of time-on-task can measure this tradeoff. There is no universally superior timing schedule. [Reingold & Merikle, 1988](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207490); [Stockart et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X25101489); [Sánchez-Fuenzalida et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag052).

For an optical code, test **modulation detection, code discrimination, and meaning separately**. Failure to notice flicker cannot establish that a target or message was unseen. Conversely, seeing a glow does not establish conscious access to its code.

There are two distinct evidentiary targets. **Behavioral access without reported subjective experience** combines successful content use with a carefully characterized experience report. **An indirect-task advantage** compares sensitivity in different tasks under explicit measurement assumptions. If the participant correctly chooses between two code meanings, that is already objective content access. A second, information-identical choice task cannot coherently be declared an independent proof of zero objective access; discrepancies demand an explanation involving task demands, timing, or measurement. Forced-choice success is not, by itself, a definitive consciousness test. [Reingold & Merikle, 1988](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207490); [Micher et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.402).

## 4. Statistical decisions

### A nonsignificant awareness test is inconclusive about exact absence

A p-value above a threshold means the specified null was not rejected. It neither estimates the probability of no awareness nor establishes that the experiment could detect a small residual cue. Likewise, significant priming plus nonsignificant awareness is not a significant difference between the two.

Zerweck et al. provide a concrete empirical example: a timing condition produced a significant reaction-time effect and a nonsignificant direct test, while the direct-versus-indirect sensitivity comparison was essentially null. The behavioral effect and its unconscious interpretation must therefore be audited separately. [Zerweck et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-021-02312-2).

| Decision approach | What a favorable result permits | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary null-hypothesis test | Evidence against a specified chance model when significant | A nonsignificant result does not demonstrate no sensitivity. |
| Confidence interval | A range of compatible effects under the sampling model | Inclusion of zero is not equivalence; a group interval does not bound each person. |
| Prespecified equivalence / smallest effect of interest | Exclusion of effects outside a defensible interval | Exactly zero awareness, or irrelevance of a smaller cue capable of explaining the main effect. |
| Bayes factor | Relative predictive support for explicitly specified models | Model-free absence; a posterior model probability without model odds; immunity to weak priors or task mismatch. |
| Bayesian interval / practical-null region | Posterior mass within a prespecified negligible range | Independence from prior, likelihood, pooling, and construct assumptions. |
| Latent or measurement-error model | Estimation separating modeled noise from latent relations | Identification of unmeasured conscious cues or an invalid report scale. |
| Direct sensitivity comparison | Evidence that an indirect measure outperforms a comparable direct measure | Unconsciousness unless relative sensitivity to conscious information is justified. |
| Functional dissociation | Evidence against a specified common-process model when manipulations affect measures differently | A logically assumption-free demonstration of absent phenomenal experience. |

For a standard two-one-sided equivalence test at α = .05, the corresponding 90% interval must lie inside the prespecified bounds; an ordinary 95% interval serves a different inferential role. One-sided upper bounds may be appropriate for a clearly directional sensitivity question, but stable below-chance performance can indicate reversed mappings or systematic information and must be investigated. [Lakens, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177).

**Illustrative reasoning, not study data:** an awareness estimate of 51% with a 95% interval of 47–55% leaves appreciable residual discrimination possible. It cannot establish equivalence to 50% within ±2 percentage points. A much narrower interval could exclude that range, but the ±2-point bound still needs scientific justification. Small awareness is not automatically too small to explain a small priming effect.

In a deliberately simplified mixture model, if a fraction q of trials are consciously answered perfectly and all other trials are guesses, two-choice accuracy is .5 + .5q. A modest behavioral advantage can therefore come from rare informative trials. This example does not estimate q in any reviewed study; it explains why trial mixtures deserve explicit sensitivity analysis.

### Bayesian and latent-variable approaches improve questions, not construct validity

Specify a plausible range of residual sensitivity under the alternative, and repeat the analysis across narrower/wider, directional, and heterogeneous alternatives. A very broad alternative can favor a point-null model over small nonzero effects. Posterior practical-equivalence estimates can be easier to interpret than a single Bayes-factor threshold, but also require justified bounds.

Measurement-error regression can reduce attenuation that otherwise creates a spurious positive intercept at estimated zero awareness. It cannot repair an awareness task measuring the wrong feature. Check nonlinear relations, lapses, reporting criteria, participant mixtures, and whether the estimated zero-awareness intercept is an extrapolation. [Goldstein et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000316); [Vadillo et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-021-01923-y).

Yaron et al.'s nonparametric procedure tests whether a group effect is compatible with a modeled regression-to-the-mean explanation. It does not classify each participant as unconscious, and the authors acknowledge sensitivity to awareness-task validity and extreme awareness–effect relations. Different correction methods can disagree. [Yaron et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-023-02326-x).

The July 2026 group-binomial work targets whether sampled individuals depart from chance, rather than only testing a population mean. Its simulations and reanalysis show that the choice of test matters, including for Bayesian procedures. That is a useful diagnostic advance, not a universal validator of unconsciousness; binomial and independence assumptions still require scrutiny. [Lublinsky et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag039).

### Selection, heterogeneity, and missing data

Selecting people because their observed awareness score is low, then reusing that score to certify unawareness, enriches negative measurement errors. Their independent behavioral score can remain positive. Preregistering that selection makes the plan transparent but does not remove the statistical problem. Independent calibration and held-out awareness measurements reduce data reuse; they do not remove systematic response bias. [Shanks, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1170-y).

Selecting lowest-PAS trials raises a related but not identical issue: conditioning on a report changes the sampled evidence distribution. The 2026 critique of the 2025 consensus recommendations argues that such bins need not isolate zero sensitivity. This dispute should remain explicit, rather than treating “unseen” as either worthless or definitive. [Schmidt et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02926-6); [Stockart et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X25101489).

Plan both participant numbers and trials per person. Report individual/condition intervals, learning or fatigue drift, and the contribution of high performers. A group average can hide mixtures or systematic below-chance responses. Low split-half correlation can reflect noise, but also restricted true variation near chance; reliability and precision are not interchangeable. [Yaron et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-023-02326-x).

Keep every randomized participant in the accounting. Explain losses from weak SSVEPs, insufficient sleep, artifacts, reported awareness, or failed calibration; each can change the population represented. A missing report, ambiguous eye movement, or absent response is not a “no awareness” observation. Report all-trial success and responder-conditioned accuracy separately. Prespecify multiplicity across frequencies, timings, sleep stages, sensors, classifiers, endpoints, and delays; distinguish confirmatory from exploratory analyses. The paired significance of two outcomes is not a substitute for their direct comparison.

## 5. Paradigms do not automatically transfer

### Masking and semantic priming

Masked shape, arrow, number, and word tasks ask different questions. Response congruency may reflect learned motor associations; a familiar lexical-category effect can involve previously learned meaning without demonstrating sentence comprehension. The primary syntactic experiments and their later reanalysis illustrate how small behavioral effects coexist with difficult visibility measurement. The 2025 review is **secondary evidence plus a reanalysis**, not a second independent cohort. [Berkovitch & Dehaene, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2018.12.001); [Hernández-Gutiérrez et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.419).

Two 2026 developments prevent a uniformly negative verdict: intensive individual testing found conditional arrow-priming dissociations, and a multisite Registered Report replicated delayed orientation performance under reported unawareness. Neither tested hidden optical messages during sleep. Their strongest alternative is a surviving conscious-information or measurement model, not the assertion that their positive behavioral observations never occurred. [Sánchez-Fuenzalida et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag052); [Franco-Martínez et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf046).

The supplied Jimenez DOI compares different constructs as well as different mathematics: subjective awareness/detection versus objective discrimination/priming. Their disagreement at one timing is therefore not merely a contest between statistical software. Detailed sample and effect extraction remained access-limited. [Jimenez et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.104018).

A paper published two days before the cutoff adds restricted masked-word decision effects, but its lexical awareness task used different words and its complete text was inaccessible. It is a current lead, not decisive evidence for arbitrary unconscious inference. [Remiszewski et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag068).

### Flicker, spectral modulation, and closed eyes

Rapid flicker interfaces often implement an **outgoing** communication channel: a participant intentionally attends to a visible option and a computer decodes the brain response. “Bits per minute” belongs to that defined equipment-mediated task. It is not a measured rate of incoming semantic information. A carrier frequency is likewise not a semantic bit rate. [Brickwedde et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2022.109726); [Ming et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/acb50e).

“Invisible spectral flicker” is a technique name. The inspected 2024 study found greater perceived flicker than steady light despite lower visibility than conventional alternatives. Its neural effect should be retained, and its invisibility claim narrowed. An invisible-flicker attentional-capture null further shows that a neural response need not yield the proposed behavioral effect. [Hansen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-75448-4); [Alais et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep29296).

Eyelid transmission varies with wavelength and person. Transmission measurements do not establish preservation of spatial detail; separate retinal-pattern or behavioral evidence is needed before claiming that letters or words survive the route. Spectrally matched open-eye stimuli may cease to be matched after eyelid filtering—an optical inference requiring direct validation. [Bierman et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.3593151).

An eyes-closed waking BCI can read intentional attention without testing unconscious reception. Upconversion contact lenses change the sensory interface by converting near-infrared into visible light; aided discrimination must remain separate from unaided vision and sleep. [Lim et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2560/10/2/026021); [Ma et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.04.019).

### Sleep, auditory comparisons, and physiological effects

Sleep SSVEP results support sensory entry, while the 2026 EEG-fMRI study demonstrates state-dependent cortical changes. Negative cortical BOLD does not by itself prove information processing impossible, just as positive EEG does not establish comprehension. These measurements sample different signals and need not contradict one another. [Cicero et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73997-y).

Auditory work includes trained semantic response preparation, novel associations, and delayed tests. Different stages and tasks yield different outcomes. A word learned through sound and later tested with an image does not constitute optical input during sleep. New code-to-meaning acquisition, reactivation of an existing association, and consolidation of waking learning require separate controls. [Kouider et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.016); [Koroma et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.801666); [Schmidig et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.89601.3).

The 2025 social-evaluation result is narrow and admits a consolidation/reactivation interpretation. A different bias-reactivation protocol failed direct replication; that failure cannot be assigned to the 2025 protocol, but it cautions against general claims of reliable belief control. [Zhao et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9); [Humiston & Wamsley, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0211416).

Circadian shifts are another distinct achievement. Light can alter clock timing without representing a proposition. Equalizing physically relevant stimulation and counterbalancing message meanings are necessary to separate a content effect from alertness or circadian effects. [Lok et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41742-w).

## 6. Achievement levels and neural decoding

| Level | Evidence required | What remains unproved |
|---|---|---|
| Physical delivery | Intended optical differences reach the relevant receiving surface | Retinal/neural encoding or perception |
| Evoked response | Reproducible stimulus-linked physiology, with artifact controls | Discrimination between messages |
| Neural discrimination | Held-out decoding of randomized stimulus identity | Participant access or semantic understanding |
| Participant discrimination | Participant distinguishes alternatives beyond a prespecified baseline | Meaning, learning, or absent awareness |
| Access to learned meaning | Responses track learned meaning across changed exemplars or motor mappings | Acquisition of new meaning |
| New learning | Previously unavailable associations/content influence prespecified tests | Durable retention or generalized use |
| Delayed retention | Content-specific effects persist at planned delays | Transfer outside the trained test |
| Transfer | Appropriate performance on new tasks/items | Arbitrary message comprehension or general behavioral control |

This hierarchy is a conceptual audit, not a necessary serial biological model. A computer may extract a weak stimulus signature that the participant cannot use. A neural-versus-behavioral sensitivity advantage still does not show participant comprehension. The fMRI reanalysis by Streicher et al. also shows why neural significance and an awareness null require a direct statistical comparison. [Streicher et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaf042).

## 7. Competing explanations and tests that could reject them

The alternatives must be specified narrowly enough to make predictions. No single control falsifies every conceivable residual-awareness account.

| Testable explanation | Predicted signature | Observation that would count against that explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Residual visible cue drives behavior | Main performance tracks cue visibility; matched weak-visible controls reproduce the effect | Held-out, feature-matched tests and calibrated models bound the cue contribution below the observed content-specific effect; result survives alternative criteria and task timings |
| Guessing or response bias | Responses follow base rates or favored options, not randomized content | Balanced blinded labels, unseen item combinations, appropriate signal-detection analysis, and replication show content-specific accuracy beyond the chance model |
| Expectancy or demand | Guessed condition, experimenter knowledge, or instruction wording predicts success | Blinded delivery/scoring, randomized meanings, balanced expectancy, and concealed test content preserve the effect |
| Attention differs across tasks | Direct-task impairment increases with divided attention, delay, or unfamiliar response requirements | Matching or experimentally manipulating those factors leaves the proposed dissociation; positive controls establish direct-task competence |
| Awakening or dream awareness accounts for sleep success | Success clusters near arousals or reported content experience | Prespecified event-level staging and prompt probes find a bounded effect away from identified episodes; latent missed-event sensitivity analysis cannot account for its size |
| Equipment leakage carries the answer | Stimulus identity appears in audible/mechanical/electrical channels or control recordings | Powered optical-block controls and independent leakage channels cannot decode the answer, while neural/behavioral effects require the optical path |
| Classifier contamination creates accuracy | Performance depends on neighboring windows, label channels, test-set tuning, or shared preprocessing | Locked pipelines generalize to held-out sessions and independently generated payloads; grouped permutations and equipment-only classifiers fail |
| Global alertness/circadian change | Broad response-speed or state effects without item specificity | Matched physical exposure and counterbalanced code meanings produce selective semantic transfer without a corresponding global explanation |
| Waking learning or consolidation explains “new learning” | Target answers were available before sleep or encountered during later testing | Sleep-only randomized associations, no-answer exposure controls, first-test analyses, and delayed novel-item transfer retain the effect |

Prompt probes can reject a **specified** prevalence or contribution of detected experiences. They cannot retrospectively prove that every unprobed interval contained no experience. A genuinely unconscious account is also falsifiable at the operational level: discovery of sufficient matched conscious access, disappearance under leakage control, or failure of the prespecified behavioral endpoint defeats that study's claim.

## 8. Prioritized gaps

| Priority | Gap type | What is missing or unresolved | Most useful next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Missing evidence | End-to-end, unaided, content-specific optical communication in non-lucid sleep with credible awareness constraints | Replicated behavioral learned-code test, followed separately by novel-association learning and delayed transfer |
| 2 | Measurement problem | Flicker visibility is frequently substituted for content awareness; later recall substitutes for experience | Matched modulation/code/meaning tests and prompt experience sampling, with explicit uncertainty |
| 3 | Measurement/model dispute | Whether selected “unseen” trials isolate unconscious processing | All-condition functional dissociations, criterion manipulations, independent calibration, and competing latent models |
| 4 | Missing evidence | Direct comparison of open-eye, closed-eye waking, and sleep reception of the same code | State-specific validation with identical payload definitions and controls |
| 5 | Contradictory or restricted evidence | Priming, attentional capture, sleep learning, and social-bias effects do not generalize uniformly | Independent preregistered replications of narrowly defined effects, including informative nulls |
| 6 | Measurement/generalization problem | Rare responders, poor neural responders, insufficient sleepers, and rejected trials change denominators | Full attrition accounting; all-assigned and eligible-state estimates; participant-level uncertainty |
| 7 | Search/access limit | Incomplete full-text/supplement access, registry timing, sample-overlap audit, and exhaustive correction indexing | Retrieve specified missing originals/supplements and registration histories before quantitative pooling |

The recent positive replications make “all apparent unconscious processing is measurement error” too strong. The remaining construct and transfer gaps make “unconscious optical semantic communication is established” too strong. Both positions exceed the evidence reviewed.

## 9. Conceptual preregistered study outline

**Status:** proposed design only; not an executed or registered study. No equipment construction or exposure parameters are specified.

### Claim and estimands

Start with access to **two previously learned optical-code meanings**, not arbitrary language. Define separate primary estimates for (a) participant content-specific performance, (b) neural code discrimination, and (c) each awareness dimension. Define whether the target population is all enrolled participants or a specified eligible-sleep population; report both recruitment flow and delivery success.

The direct behavioral arm tests content access under absent reported subjective experience; successful choices must be acknowledged as objective sensitivity. A separate indirect arm can test priming or response facilitation against a matched direct task. These are different claims and should not share an undifferentiated “unconscious” endpoint.

Use waking open-eye and closed-eye conditions to establish the actual information available through the route. Test N2, N3, and non-lucid REM separately. Treat lucid dreaming as a distinct positive-control state for experienced communication. Do not pool these states to create a general “sleep success” endpoint.

### Randomization and controls

Counterbalance arbitrary code-to-meaning assignments across participants and generate novel message instances independently of response recording. Match physical payload statistics across meanings and include sham, scrambled/untrained mapping, and clearly perceptible positive controls. Include a powered-device condition with the optical path blocked and independent monitoring for unintended acoustic, mechanical, and electrical cues.

Demonstrate meaning beyond a memorized button mapping: change the response effector or mapping, use new exemplars, and test a different retrieval task. Include unrepeated held-out instances, since weak cues may accumulate across repetitions. Add a separate acquisition cohort only after the learned-code test is interpretable. That cohort may know the code system, but must not have encountered the target association or answer while awake. Learning a new association to a familiar word is narrower than acquiring a new concept; neither should be labeled arbitrary-message capacity.

### Awareness measurement

During waking validation, collect feature-specific PAS, confidence, modulation detection, code discrimination, and meaning judgments under matched conditions. Predefine which measure addresses which claim. Randomize or counterbalance reporting schedules to estimate report interference and delay costs. Use independently held-out calibration data and catch/easy trials; do not select an “unaware” group and certify it with the selection score.

During sleep, use blinded physiological staging and event-level arousal scoring, plus randomly sampled prompt awakenings asking separately about sensation, code, meaning, dream content, and lucidity. Record no recall as a memory outcome. Probes and no-probe periods should be analyzed separately because probing changes sleep.

### Behavioral and neural outcomes

The primary behavioral endpoint should depend on randomized content, be scored blind, and have an explicit chance model. Report correct, incorrect, ambiguous, and absent responses. Do not replace all-trial success with accuracy among responders.

Freeze the neural classifier before test-data access. Hold out entire runs or sessions; avoid overlapping windows across folds. Separate stimulus/trigger logs from predictive inputs, fit preprocessing inside training folds, and use permutations that preserve temporal/participant structure. Neural success is reported independently of participant success.

### Uncertainty and decision criteria

Power the study jointly for the smallest meaningful behavioral effect and informative constraints on reported experience and candidate residual cues. There is no universal awareness threshold below which conscious mediation is irrelevant. Choose bounds by asking how much weak or intermittent conscious information could account for the behavioral effect under calibrated plausible models, not by adopting a conventional small d′; vary those bounds in sensitivity analyses.

Prespecify joint criteria: a positive content-specific behavioral effect, precise reporting/awareness estimates, and comparison against calibrated weak-conscious and rare-aware-trial models. Do not demand zero objective performance on the same task that defines behavioral success. In the indirect arm, compare direct/indirect sensitivity where commensurable and examine full functional curves across stimulus conditions. Independently manipulate signal strength and reporting criteria to distinguish changes in processing from relabeling of experience. Use hierarchical models for people, items, sessions, and sleep stages, with explicit lapse, criterion, and measurement-error components. Report prior and model sensitivity. These analyses license bounded conclusions, never proof of exactly zero experience.

### Exclusions, missingness, and replication

Predefine artifact, stage, arousal, calibration, and missing-response rules before unblinding. Define sleep eligibility from the pre-stimulation state, and treat subsequent arousal as an outcome. Report all randomized opportunities and a secondary stable-state analysis: excluding arousal after stimulation can select on an effect of treatment. Quantify sensitivity to excluded and missing observations rather than coding them as unawareness.

Correct for planned multiple endpoints and state comparisons; specify stopping rules. Reserve new participants or an independent laboratory for confirmation. Registration and public analysis plans make decisions inspectable; they do not validate a poor measurement construct.

### Learning and transfer

Use separate prespecified tests for immediate access, delayed retention, and transfer to new items/tasks. Delay tests without supplying correct answers at earlier tests, or counterbalance separate item sets to avoid test-induced learning. If a novel meaning is claimed, rule out prior learning, familiar sensory-category associations, waking leakage, and simple motor conditioning.

**Decision outcomes:** a neural-only positive supports encoding; behavior with demonstrable awareness supports conscious perception or dream communication; behavior plus well-bounded relevant awareness supports a qualified unconscious-processing inference; a precise behavioral null bounds that specific task/state; imprecise results remain inconclusive.

### Search strategy

Searches were performed on 28 October 2026, covering foundational sources through that date; the newest included primary lead was published 26 October 2026. This is a targeted critical review with citation chasing, not a registered systematic review or exhaustive database export. No PRISMA counts or pooled global sample total are asserted.

Two web search engines were used to locate primary publisher pages, PubMed/PMC records, author manuscripts, institutional PDFs, and registration links. Queries included:

- `unconscious perception subjective objective awareness residual awareness perceptual awareness scale confidence signal detection meta-d`
- `unconscious priming regression to the mean measurement error equivalence Bayesian indirect task advantage`
- `SSVEP imperceptible flicker rapid invisible frequency tagging invisible spectral flicker`
- `closed eyelids spectral transmission eyes closed BCI sleep NREM REM optical`
- `sleep learning vocabulary new associations replication null preregistered lucid dream communication`
- Exact supplied DOIs/titles and combinations with `correction erratum retraction`, plus 2025–2026 recency searches.

Backward and forward chains included Vorberg → recent individual priming work; awareness consensus → its 2026 critique; syntactic originals → the 2025 reanalysis; Norton → later sleep SSVEP work; Konkoly → later sleep responsiveness; and Züst → subsequent auditory-learning studies. Reviews were used for discovery and disagreements, with their secondary role labeled.

**Inclusion:** human primary studies testing relevant optical delivery, neural/behavioral discrimination, awareness, learning, or sleep state; methodological papers addressing inference; important replications, nulls, corrections, and selected systematic reviews. **Exclusion from direct support:** media narratives, commercial announcements, prior assistant answers, animal-only findings, engineering receivers alone, and unrelated therapeutic efficacy. Ordinary presleep learning was included only when explicitly identified as consolidation/reactivation.

### Access and deduplication

The evidence and bibliography distinguish relevant full-text inspection, partial/indexed full text, and abstract/metadata-only access. Publisher errors and intermittent PMC CAPTCHA impeded some retrievals. Full original methods/supplements for Jimenez 2026, Norton 2017, and the human component of Ma 2025 were not completely inspected; exact missing sample/effect details are marked unverified. The October 2026 inference paper was inspected through indexed primary excerpts. No raw datasets were reanalyzed.

Published versions and preprints, PubMed and PMC mirrors, online-first and issue dates, and corrections were deduplicated. Reanalyses are not new experiments. Some investigator/sample overlap remains unaudited, especially related spectral-flicker studies; no quantitative meta-analysis across these heterogeneous paradigms is justified here.
