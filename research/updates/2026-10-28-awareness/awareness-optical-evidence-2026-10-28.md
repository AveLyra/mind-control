# Awareness and optical information: evidence appendix

**New literature update — search cutoff: 28 October 2026.** This appendix accompanies the critical review and annotated bibliography. It does not identify the unresolved citation `turn3file2`, recover an original upload, or replace the collection's provenance record. No experiments or publication of repository changes were undertaken.

The 30 selected rows compare what was measured with what the result supports. They are not a census of all relevant experiments or a pooled meta-analysis. Unless expressly identified as secondary, entries are primary experiments. Numbers come from inspected source material; missing fields are marked rather than inferred.

**Reading the table.** **NV** = not verified because access or inspection was insufficient; **NR** = not reported in the inspected material; **NL** = not located by this search; **NA** = not applicable. **FT** = relevant full-text sections inspected; **PFT** = partial primary text or indexed primary excerpts inspected; **A** = abstract/metadata only. FT does not imply that every supplement, registry version, or raw dataset was audited. These labels apply to this review's access, not to the paper's inherent availability.

CI means confidence interval; quoted intervals are 95% unless stated otherwise. SD and SE are distinguished where verified; unspecified ± values retain the source's uncertainty limitation. PSG means polysomnography; SSVEP means steady-state visual evoked potential; RIFT means rapid invisible frequency tagging; PAS means perceptual awareness scale; RT means response time; SWS means slow-wave sleep; LRP means lateralized readiness potential; BOLD is the fMRI blood-oxygenation signal. A null significance test is not an equivalence test. “Registration NL” is not a claim that no registration exists.

## Cross-study safeguards for interpretation

- **Count samples, not URLs or versions.** Publisher, PubMed, PMC, author-copy and preprint records can describe the same observations. Reanalysis is not replication. Online and issue years identify one study.
- **Keep the channel explicit.** A computer's decoded class, a participant's intentional choice, content perceived in a dream, and later forced-choice memory are different endpoints. Neural “information transfer” does not establish that a person understood an incoming payload.
- **Keep the awareness target explicit.** Detection of modulation, identification of a shape/word, recognition of a learned code, awareness of meaning, and later recall are not interchangeable measures.
- **Do not upgrade missing information.** NV and NR do not prove that a safeguard was absent. NL is a search outcome. Registration links require a timing/version audit before claiming complete adherence.
- **Interpret sleep findings at their measured level.** Conventional staging and arousal exclusion strengthen sleep-state attribution; they do not independently distinguish unconscious processing from dream awareness or experience forgotten before a later test.
