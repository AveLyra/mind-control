## Discovery and inspection scope

The 66-result reconsolidation/sleep query was paginated to its returned end. A source-memory/sleep continuation duplicated a PMID; a single-page retrieval then supplied all 31 distinct results. This correction is retained privately. Search results themselves were not treated as complete selected-abstract inspection. Targeted primary-site web searches supplemented PubMed for AI work. The zero-result title/abstract AI query does not establish field-wide absence: relevant HCI experiments and the preprint below are outside that narrow indexed result.

All 33 selected PMIDs supplied complete abstracts and citation metadata through targeted retrieval. Nine rights-eligible PMC bodies were retrieved in contiguous complete coverage: M06, M07, M10, M19, M22, M23, M24, M27, and M32. Selected methods/results/limitations and replication relationships were inspected, not every numerical cell or participant record. M03 remained abstract-level after two connector internal errors and a web browser challenge. M02 remained abstract-level after an unavailable publisher route. Selected design/results/limitations from the AI preprint's primary HTML were checked. No participant data, code, or original analysis was rerun; no intervention was performed. Raw receipts remain private.

Publication issue years follow PubMed; earlier online dates and DOI-year differences are explicit where relevant. A same-paper replication, a same-team extension, a direct new-team replication, and a different-protocol conceptual test do not supply interchangeable evidence.

## Selected sources and roles

<a id="m01"></a>
### M01 — Source monitoring

M K Johnson, S Hashtroudi, D S Lindsay (1993 Jul). [Source monitoring](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8346328/). DOI: `10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3`. **Framework, not a new experiment.** Foundational source-monitoring theory; perceptual/contextual/reflective evidence and flexible criteria. Does not directly test AI, sleep, or hypnosis combinations.

<a id="m02"></a>
### M02 — Large language models (LLMs) and the institutionalization of misinformation

Maryanne Garry et al. (2024 Dec). [Large language models (LLMs) and the institutionalization of misinformation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39393958/). DOI: `10.1016/j.tics.2024.08.007`. **Theoretical analysis; abstract/metadata checked.** Explicitly connects LLM misinformation with reality monitoring. No new participant experiment or demonstrated mediator. Publisher full-text route was unavailable through the web tool.

<a id="m03"></a>
### M03 — Reconsolidation of episodic memories: a subtle reminder triggers integration of new information

Almut Hupbach et al. (2007). [Reconsolidation of episodic memories: a subtle reminder triggers integration of new information](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17202429/). DOI: `10.1101/lm.365707`. **Original primary object-list experiments; abstract checked.** Reminder-dependent asymmetric intrusions and a delayed/immediate contrast. Two PMC connector attempts returned internal errors; the web route returned a browser challenge. No new complete-body inspection is claimed.

<a id="m04"></a>
### M04 — Episodic memory reconsolidation: updating or source confusion?

Almut Hupbach et al. (2009 Jul). [Episodic memory reconsolidation: updating or source confusion?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19468955/). DOI: `10.1080/09658210902882399`. **Primary source-test follow-up; abstract checked.** Same research line as M03, with direct source judgments and asymmetric misattribution. The authors interpret this as updating; the behavioral pattern alone does not uniquely establish a biological restabilization mechanism.

<a id="m05"></a>
### M05 — Does reactivation trigger episodic memory change? A meta-analysis

Iiona D Scully et al. (2017 Jul). [Does reactivation trigger episodic memory change? A meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28025069/). DOI: `10.1016/j.nlm.2016.12.012`. **Meta-analysis; abstract checked.** Supports reactivation-related susceptibility to interference and intrusions in the literature then available. Publication date is not evidence that later failed replications were included. Not an independent experimental cohort.

<a id="m06"></a>
### M06 — The time window of reconsolidation: A replication

Colleen M Parks et al. (2022 Oct). [The time window of reconsolidation: A replication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35428932/). DOI: `10.3758/s13423-022-02102-3`. **Primary direct replication; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** A different team replicated the critical time-dependent intrusion pattern from M03, with some object materials adapted. Positive replication of that endpoint, not proof of unrestricted memory rewriting.

<a id="m07"></a>
### M07 — Intrusions in episodic memory: reconsolidation or interference?

Angela Klingmüller et al. (2017 May). [Intrusions in episodic memory: reconsolidation or interference?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28416633/). DOI: `10.1101/lm.045047.117`. **Three primary boundary/replication experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Ordinary-context replication failed; salient-context manipulation produced a small qualitative replication. A further test challenged the assumed target-memory reactivation and favored interference/context binding. Separate from M06's positive time-dependency test.

<a id="m08"></a>
### M08 — Postretrieval new learning does not reliably induce human memory updating via reconsolidation

Tom E Hardwicke et al. (2016 May 10). [Postretrieval new learning does not reliably induce human memory updating via reconsolidation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27114514/). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1601440113`. **Primary replication program; abstract checked.** Four direct motor-sequence replications (n = 64) and three conceptual declarative-sequence experiments (n = 48). Expected impairment was absent; retrieval sometimes protected knowledge. These do not directly replicate M03's object-list protocol.

<a id="m09"></a>
### M09 — Modification of episodic memories by novel learning: a failed replication study

Kevin van Schie et al. (2017). [Modification of episodic memories by novel learning: a failed replication study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29038684/). DOI: `10.1080/20008198.2017.1315291`. **Primary picture-memory replication; abstract checked.** N = 96; some original differences appeared, but the critical reactivation-plus-learning versus learning-only difference lacked substantial support. Targets Wichert's picture paradigm, not M03's object-list study.

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10 — Surprise and destabilize: prediction error influences episodic memory reconsolidation

Alyssa H Sinclair et al. (2018 Aug). [Surprise and destabilize: prediction error influences episodic memory reconsolidation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30012882/). DOI: `10.1101/lm.046912.117`. **Primary behavioral prediction-error experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Memory samples N = 48 and N = 24; a separate N = 408 sample rated stimulus properties. Surprise and time-course results support an updating interpretation while leaving broader mechanistic questions open. The stimulus-rating sample is not a large memory-intervention replication.

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11 — Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories

Alyssa H Sinclair et al. (2021 Dec 21). [Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34911768/). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.2117625118`. **Primary fMRI extension; abstract checked.** Same research line as M10; disrupted hippocampal representations predicted episodic updating. Neural associations are not direct assays of trace destruction or an AI/hypnosis replication.

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12 — Recalling a witnessed event increases eyewitness suggestibility: the reversed testing effect

Jason C K Chan et al. (2009 Jan). [Recalling a witnessed event increases eyewitness suggestibility: the reversed testing effect](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19037905/). DOI: `10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02245.x`. **Primary eyewitness experiments; abstract checked.** Testing before misinformation increased later suggestibility. The interpretation includes potentiated new learning and interference; the report alone cannot distinguish all reconsolidation/source accounts. PubMed journal issue is January 2009, despite the DOI's 2008 component.

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13 — Does reactivating a witnessed memory increase its susceptibility to impairment by subsequent misinformation?

Eric J Rindal et al. (2016 Oct). [Does reactivating a witnessed memory increase its susceptibility to impairment by subsequent misinformation?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26913933/). DOI: `10.1037/xlm0000265`. **Primary new-team methods/replication comparison; abstract checked.** Replicated increased reporting under some tests without finding original-memory impairment under modified recognition. Different materials/measures make this a methodological/conceptual test, not an unchanged exact replication. Matching a behavioral result need not validate its original mechanistic interpretation.

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14 — The effect of testing can increase or decrease misinformation susceptibility depending on the retention interval

Ayanna K Thomas et al. (2017). [The effect of testing can increase or decrease misinformation susceptibility depending on the retention interval](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29214206/). DOI: `10.1186/s41235-017-0081-4`. **Two primary retention-interval experiments; abstract checked.** Testing increased immediate misinformation susceptibility but reduced it at the longer tested interval. Delay changes the result; the design does not isolate sleep as the causal factor.

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15 — Labile or stable: opposing consequences for memory when reactivated during waking and sleep

Susanne Diekelmann et al. (2011 Mar). [Labile or stable: opposing consequences for memory when reactivated during waking and sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21258327/). DOI: `10.1038/nn.2744`. **Primary state comparison; abstract checked.** Associated odor cues destabilized memories during waking but stabilized them during slow-wave sleep in the tested task. State-dependent counterexample to an invariant open rewriting window.

<a id="m16"></a>
### M16 — Sleep's role in the reconsolidation of declarative memories

Jens G Klinzing et al. (2016 Dec). [Sleep's role in the reconsolidation of declarative memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27720856/). DOI: `10.1016/j.nlm.2016.10.004`. **Primary sleep/reactivation comparison; abstract checked.** A brief sleep interval preferentially benefited reactivated remote visuospatial memories compared with the tested nonreactivated/recent-memory conditions. Shared investigators with M15; a related extension, not independent exact replication.

<a id="m17"></a>
### M17 — Sleep accelerates re-stabilization of human declarative memories

Malen D Moyano et al. (2019 Jul). [Sleep accelerates re-stabilization of human declarative memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31029768/). DOI: `10.1016/j.nlm.2019.04.012`. **Primary sleep/restabilization study; abstract checked.** The sleep result used retrieval-induced forgetting of an interfering list as an indirect measure of original-memory stability. Supports faster protection against interference, not direct evidence of false-content incorporation.

<a id="m18"></a>
### M18 — Associations between sleep and episodic memory updating

Natalie B Bryant et al. (2020 Aug). [Associations between sleep and episodic memory updating](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31743543/). DOI: `10.1002/hipo.23178`. **Two primary observational sleep/updating experiments; abstract checked.** Actigraphy and polysomnography associations linked sleep after successive learning sessions with updating. Spindle associations depended on earlier-night conditions; no causal spindle manipulation. PubMed issue is 2020, with earlier online publication in 2019.

<a id="m19"></a>
### M19 — Sleep enhances reconsolidation-based strengthening of visuospatial memories

Bethany J Jones et al. (2022 May 04). [Sleep enhances reconsolidation-based strengthening of visuospatial memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35508568/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-022-11135-6`. **Two primary sleep/reactivation experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Sleep was associated with preservation/strengthening, not arbitrary content substitution. The paper notes time-of-day/retention-interval differences, differing reactivation locations, and small sleep-physiology subsets. These constrain mechanistic certainty.

<a id="m20"></a>
### M20 — Reactivation during sleep with incomplete reminder cues rather than complete ones stabilizes long-term memory in humans

Cecilia Forcato et al. (2020 Dec 04). [Reactivation during sleep with incomplete reminder cues rather than complete ones stabilizes long-term memory in humans](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33277601/). DOI: `10.1038/s42003-020-01457-4`. **Primary cue-completeness/state experiments; abstract checked.** Incomplete waking reminders produced lability; both sleep cue types supported shorter-term stabilization, while incomplete cues supported the longer-delay result. Endpoint is existing-memory stabilization, not delivery of a new narrative. Overlaps investigators with M15-M16.

<a id="m21"></a>
### M21 — Does recall after sleep-dependent memory consolidation reinstate sensitivity to retroactive interference?

Gaétane Deliens et al. (2013). [Does recall after sleep-dependent memory consolidation reinstate sensitivity to retroactive interference?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23874738/). DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0068727`. **Primary contrary interference result; abstract checked.** Interference appeared specifically in the evening-learning/sleep condition of the tested word-pair design. Authors tentatively invoke reconsolidation. This narrows a universal sleep-protection claim without identifying an AI mechanism.

<a id="m22"></a>
### M22 — Sleep's role in updating aversive autobiographical memories

Yasmine Azza et al. (2022 Mar 24). [Sleep's role in updating aversive autobiographical memories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35332136/). DOI: `10.1038/s41398-022-01878-1`. **Primary rescripting/nap study; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** N = 44 healthy students. Selected immediate cognition/heart-rate advantages were absent one week later. Rescripting changes interpretation and emotional response; factual replacement and a causal spindle mechanism were not established.

<a id="m23"></a>
### M23 — Does sleep benefit source memory? Investigating 12-h retention intervals with a multinomial modeling approach

Sabrina Berres et al. (2025 Feb). [Does sleep benefit source memory? Investigating 12-h retention intervals with a multinomial modeling approach](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38831160/). DOI: `10.3758/s13421-024-01579-8`. **Two primary source-memory experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Spatial source-memory benefit replicated within the paper. No benefit for incidental frame color or bound spatial/color memory, with possible floor effects. Multinomial estimates depend on model constraints; night/day comparisons and online sleep measures limit causal specificity. PubMed issue is February 2025; DOI/online date is 2024.

<a id="m24"></a>
### M24 — A registered report testing the effect of sleep on Deese-Roediger-McDermott false memory: greater lure and veridical recall but fewer intrusions after sleep

Matthew H C Mak et al. (2023 Dec). [A registered report testing the effect of sleep on Deese-Roediger-McDermott false memory: greater lure and veridical recall but fewer intrusions after sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38077219/). DOI: `10.1098/rsos.220595`. **Primary registered report; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** N = 488. Better studied-word recall, fewer unrelated intrusions, and more critical lures in the specified adjusted analysis. Preplanned intrusion adjustment and time-of-day analysis matter. These DRM/gist outcomes are not interchangeable with misinformation or autobiographical invention.

<a id="m25"></a>
### M25 — Reality monitoring in hypnosis: a pilot investigation

Richard A Bryant et al. (2004 Apr). [Reality monitoring in hypnosis: a pilot investigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15115061/). DOI: `10.1076/iceh.52.2.188.28095`. **Primary hypnotic-reality pilot; abstract checked.** N = 20, high/low hypnotizable groups; ratings during suggested perception with/without a projected image. Perceived reality, not delayed autobiographical-memory source accuracy.

<a id="m26"></a>
### M26 — Reality monitoring in hypnosis: a real-simulating analysis

Richard A Bryant et al. (2005 Jan). [Reality monitoring in hypnosis: a real-simulating analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15788241/). DOI: `10.1080/00207140490914216`. **Primary same-team real-simulator extension; abstract checked.** N = 40, 20 real and 20 simulating participants. Results challenge a simple simulation account. Changed comparator and shared researchers prevent calling this independent exact replication; all social/expectancy explanations are not thereby excluded.

<a id="m27"></a>
### M27 — Recognition in Posthypnotic Amnesia, Revisited

John F Kihlstrom (2021). [Recognition in Posthypnotic Amnesia, Revisited](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33905302/). DOI: `10.1080/00207144.2021.1910827`. **Three primary posthypnotic-amnesia experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Conceptual extensions of earlier recognition/amnesia work. Familiarity supported some recognition despite poor recall, list differentiation, and temporal ordering. Relaxed criteria increased false alarms as well as hits. Selected responsive participants; no AI/sleep manipulation or trace-erasure assay.

<a id="m28"></a>
### M28 — The eyewitness suggestibility effect and memory for source

D S Lindsay et al. (1989 May). [The eyewitness suggestibility effect and memory for source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2725271/). DOI: `10.3758/bf03198473`. **Primary source-orientation experiment; abstract checked.** A source-monitoring test eliminated the suggestibility effect seen under yes/no recognition in this paradigm. Genuine source confusion versus conscious misattribution remained unresolved in the authors' interpretation. No universal protection claim.

<a id="m29"></a>
### M29 — Features and feedback: enhancing metamnemonic knowledge at retrieval reduces source-monitoring errors

Sean M Lane et al. (2007 Nov). [Features and feedback: enhancing metamnemonic knowledge at retrieval reduces source-monitoring errors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17983318/). DOI: `10.1037/0278-7393.33.6.1131`. **Three primary feature/feedback experiments; abstract checked.** Information about diagnostic memorial features and feedback reduced later source errors. Supports flexible judgment/monitoring contributions; not proof of complete reversibility in all tasks.

<a id="m30"></a>
### M30 — Why misinformation is reported: evidence from a warning and a source-monitoring task

Helen Wyler et al. (2016 Nov). [Why misinformation is reported: evidence from a warning and a source-monitoring task](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26754184/). DOI: `10.1080/09658211.2015.1117641`. **Primary warning/source experiments; abstract checked.** N = 429 across experiments. Warning/source-task effects differed for central and peripheral misinformation. Reporting misinformation is not a single uniform cognitive outcome.

<a id="m31"></a>
### M31 — Undoing suggestive influence on memory: the reversibility of the eyewitness misinformation effect

Aileen Oeberst et al. (2012 Nov). [Undoing suggestive influence on memory: the reversibility of the eyewitness misinformation effect](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22883683/). DOI: `10.1016/j.cognition.2012.07.009`. **Three primary recoverability experiments; abstract checked.** Separating competing items and source memory restored original-event performance, including after five weeks in one experiment. Challenges universal irreversible erasure; not an independent exact replication or a general reversal guarantee.

<a id="m32"></a>
### M32 — Timing of pre-retrieval warnings matters in reducing memory errors in a repeated testing misinformation study

Alia N Wulff et al. (2025 Jan 06). [Timing of pre-retrieval warnings matters in reducing memory errors in a repeated testing misinformation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39762387/). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-024-84154-0`. **Two primary warning-timing experiments; complete PMC body, selected sections checked.** Warnings reduced retrieval-enhanced suggestibility nearer misinformation exposure but not after an extra delay. Original details can remain accessible in one condition. The delay comparison does not isolate sleep or prove trace loss when a warning fails.

<a id="m33"></a>
### M33 — How self-reference enhances susceptibility to misinformation: An event-related potentials study

Mingyao Sun et al. (2026 Jul). [How self-reference enhances susceptibility to misinformation: An event-related potentials study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42061550/). DOI: `10.1016/j.biopsycho.2026.109277`. **Primary ERP experiment; abstract/publisher summary checked.** N = 28; self-reference increased misinformation acceptance with reactivation/source-monitoring-related ERP differences. Component labels are not unique biological reconsolidation markers. No generated-content, hypnosis, or sleep intervention.

<a id="m34"></a>
### M34 — Can AI Help You Get Over Your Breakup? One Session with a Belief-Reframing Chatbot Shows Sustained Distress Reduction

Thomas Menzel et al. (2026-05-05). [Can AI Help You Get Over Your Breakup? One Session with a Belief-Reframing Chatbot Shows Sustained Distress Reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03261). DOI: `10.48550/arXiv.2605.03261`. **Primary preprint v1; selected HTML sections checked.** Randomized chatbot versus assessment-only comparison, 254 baseline participants and 171 seven-day completers. Reduced self-reported breakup distress; exploratory later follow-up selected post-session completers. Feedback confounding and no active control limit specificity. Authors state reconsolidation was not experimentally demonstrated; no misinformation test. [Inspected primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03261v1).

## Interpretation boundary

The strongest supported cross-field observation is that remembered content, source/context, familiarity, reporting criteria, and emotional response can dissociate. Reconsolidation offers a testable account of a subset of changes, with supportive time-dependent replications and serious competing explanations. Direct source-memory/sleep and hypnotic-context experiments establish restricted components. They do not establish the AI misinformation–hypnosis–sleep combination, permanent personal-memory replacement, or a general behavioral override. The linked synthesis names the missing comparisons and contrary predictions.
