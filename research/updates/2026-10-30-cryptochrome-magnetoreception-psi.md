## Scope and access

32 selected entries have matching PMID/title/DOI metadata; 31 provide abstracts. Ten complete supplied PMC bodies were retrieved with contiguous pagination and selected passages inspected. The Reppert response is abstractless but its complete body was available. The eLife physical critique was also accessible through its primary publisher. Other entries were assessed at metadata/abstract level unless explicitly stated. No figure extraction, full supplement audit, raw-data reanalysis, independent experiment, or human exposure was performed.

Positive and negative syntheses are retained. A failed fly assay does not refute bird magnetic navigation, and a positive molecular assay does not establish human perception. A registered retrospective meta-analysis is not a new prospective replication. The scientific assessment does not depend on Wikipedia or the secondary websites cited in the newly recovered telepathy answer.

## Checked evidence

<a id="m01"></a>
### M01 — Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird.

[PubMed 34163056](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34163056/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9)

**Evidence role:** In-vitro biophysics.

European robin CRY4 photochemistry responds to magnetic fields; mutations help locate the electron-transfer steps. This is protein chemistry, not an intact-human compass or proof of a specific downstream neural circuit.

**Inspection:** bibliographic metadata and provided abstract; no complete-body inspection claimed.

<a id="m02"></a>
### M02 — Human cryptochrome exhibits light-dependent magnetosensitivity.

[PubMed 21694704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694704/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1364)

**Evidence role:** Transgenic animal behavior.

Human CRY2 restored light-dependent magnetic behavior in a fly assay. The Methods give a 0.1–5 G field gradient (10–500 µT). The paper explicitly leaves downstream human capability unknown; a human protein in a fly is not a human demonstration.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

<a id="m03"></a>
### M03 — No evidence for magnetic field effects on the behaviour of Drosophila.

[PubMed 37558871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37558871/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06397-7)

**Evidence role:** Large negative replication attempt.

Tests of 97,658 maze flies and 10,960 negative-geotaxis flies found no magnetic behavior under the tested conditions. Batch structure, assay differences, and the unit of replication matter; the fly count is not a count of independent human or bird experiments.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

### M04 — Magnetic field effects on behaviour in Drosophila.

[PubMed 38693417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38693417/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07319-x)

**Evidence role:** Published methodological dispute.

Reppert disputes the conditioning controls at the actual test location and the statistical treatment of batch-level data. This is a defense/reanalysis of published results, not a new independent positive replication. The complete supplied body was retrieved; PubMed supplies no abstract.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

<a id="m05"></a>
### M05 — Essential elements of radical pair magnetosensitivity in Drosophila.

[PubMed 36813962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36813962/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05735-z)

**Evidence role:** Alternative cellular/animal mechanism.

A fly CRY C-terminal segment without the usual FAD-binding domain and high free FAD supported responses in the authors’ assays. This challenges a uniquely canonical CRY explanation. Some neuronal experiments used 100 mT; do not equate their endpoint with an Earth-field human threshold.

**Inspection:** metadata/abstract and the primary-publisher indexed Figure 2 extract (100 mT condition); no complete-body inspection claimed.

<a id="m06"></a>
### M06 — Vertebrate Cryptochromes are Vestigial Flavoproteins.

[PubMed 28317918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28317918/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep44906)

**Evidence role:** Counterevidence to a simple mammalian receptor model.

Structural and binding work finds weak FAD binding in type-II cryptochromes, including human CRY1/2. Reported dissociation constants are 16 and 68 µM; estimated native cofactor occupancy is limited. This qualifies the inference from fly rescue without proving every possible mammalian magnetic mechanism absent.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

<a id="m07"></a>
### M07 — Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain.

[PubMed 31028046](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31028046/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019)

**Evidence role:** Human physiological response.

In 36 volunteers, particular rotations near 35 µT elicited alpha-band desynchronization. Participants had eyes closed in darkness. The authors use polarity dependence to argue against the standard radical-pair-only mechanism for this response. It does not establish conscious direction finding, semantic reception, or a measured molecular receptor.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

### M08 — Human magnetic sense is mediated by a light and magnetic field resonance-dependent mechanism.

[PubMed 35637212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35637212/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-12460-6)

**Evidence role:** Human orientation result requiring independent replication.

The rotary-chair study reports blue-light-, food-context-, and radiofrequency-dependent directional choices in men. It is a positive behavioral claim from a research program, not a molecular identification of human cryptochrome, and not a replication of the dark-room alpha-EEG protocol.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

<a id="m09"></a>
### M09 — Magnetic sense-dependent probabilistic decision-making in humans.

[PubMed 40125477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40125477/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1497021)

**Evidence role:** Exploratory human decision-making claim.

The Go-stone study associates field manipulations with human binary selections. Choices and concealment involve other people and ordinary visual access; independent random future targets were not the defining task. The Methods call the work double-blind while saying task experimenters knew conditions but not the investigators’ intended interpretation. This is a narrower blinding arrangement than full condition blinding.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10 — Delayed Correlations Between Geomagnetic Activity and Human EEG Alpha and Theta Oscillations: Evidence from Archival and Experimental Data.

[PubMed 42352556](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42352556/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci16060547)

**Evidence role:** Archival correlation plus uncontrolled longitudinal exposure.

Archival EEG involved 238 people and a ±90-day lag search; the follow-up involved 22 people and a 19-day comparison. The body reports opposite directions between datasets. There is no contemporaneous sham-exposure group in the described follow-up. This is not an independent replication of the 2019 rotation response or evidence of precognition.

**Inspection:** complete supplied PMC body retrieved; selected sections inspected.

### M12 — Physical limits to magnetogenetics.

[PubMed 27529126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27529126/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.17210)

**Evidence role:** Quantitative physical critique.

Meister calculates that the proposed protein-compass and particular ferritin force/heating schemes fall short by orders of magnitude. This criticism does not apply indiscriminately to radical-pair chemistry, magnetite crystals, TMS, or every engineered magnetic device.

**Inspection:** metadata/abstract plus the primary eLife body and selected physical arguments; calculations were not independently reproduced.

### M13 — MagR Alone Is Insufficient to Confer Cellular Calcium Responses to Magnetic Stimulation.

[PubMed 28360843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28360843/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2017.00011)

**Evidence role:** Negative cellular replication.

MagR expression did not produce magnetic calcium responses across tested HEK cells and primary neurons; optical positive controls worked in channelrhodopsin-expressing neurons. This tests MagR-alone cellular activation, not all Cry–MagR assemblies or all forms of magnetoreception.

**Inspection:** bibliographic metadata and provided abstract; no complete-body inspection claimed.

[PubMed 26834996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26834996/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.7177.2)

**Evidence role:** Positive retrospective synthesis.

The 90-experiment meta-analysis reports Hedges’ g=0.09 overall and g=0.06 after excluding Bem’s original experiments. Those are different contrasts, not the same as the original d. [PubMed 41191630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41191630/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335330)

The AMP–TPP project reports 26,483 participants. ## What could change the conclusion

The strongest unresolved biological opportunities are receptor-specific causal tests, signal transduction in an intact animal, and independent controlled human replication. The best objection to dismissing psi statistically is that some original meta-analyses report positive results even after bias checks. That objection warrants direct prospective replication; it supplies neither a magnetic mechanism nor a reliable predictive channel.

A natural magnetic-communication claim would need both an identified receiver and a sender signal carrying held-out content, with information leakage and common-cause cues excluded. A precognition claim would need prospectively fixed scoring against independently generated future targets, reproducible across laboratories. Neither follows from a spin-sensitive reaction or from an EEG response.
