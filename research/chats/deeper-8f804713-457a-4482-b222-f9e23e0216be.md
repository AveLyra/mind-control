The first thing to know is that most popular body-language claims are exaggerated.

There are very few reliable one-to-one mappings:

- Hand over mouth ≠ lying

- Looking away ≠ deception

- Crossed arms ≠ defensiveness

The useful signals are clusters, especially when they represent attempts to regulate emotion, conceal information, or manage social status.

A useful framework:

| Domain | What leaks |
| --- | --- |
| Mouth | Suppression |
| Eyes | Attention |
| Hands | Anxiety / intention |
| Posture | Status |
| Feet | True commitment |
| Voice | Cognitive load |

Mouth Behaviors (suppression)

These are among the most revealing because humans instinctively inhibit speech.

- Covering mouth

- Touching lips repeatedly

- Pressing lips together

- Biting inside cheek

- Biting fingernails

- Sucking on lip

- Swallowing before speaking

Often indicates:

- withholding information

- uncertainty

- self-censorship

- anxiety

A particularly strong tell:

A person begins speaking, stops, swallows, then restarts.

This often marks a moment where two competing narratives are being evaluated internally.

Eyes (attention)

Eyes reveal what the brain is prioritizing.

Interesting tells:

- Rapid blinking → stress/arousal

- Reduced blinking → intense focus

- Looking at exits → desire to leave

- Looking at someone before laughing → seeking social approval

- Eye freeze → surprise/threat detection

One of the strongest:

Micro-glances.

People often glance toward:

- objects they want

- people they fear

- escape routes

- hidden information

before consciously controlling themselves.

Hands (cognition leaking into action)

Hands are "future simulators."

Before actions occur physically, they often appear in the hands.

Examples:

- Finger drumming → impatience

- Fidgeting → excess cognitive load

- Touching neck → self-soothing

- Touching face → uncertainty

- Rubbing palms → anticipation

- Steepling fingers → confidence/dominance

One surprisingly reliable tell:

People frequently touch the body part currently occupying their thoughts.

- Touch throat while discussing speaking

- Touch chest while discussing emotions

- Touch stomach while discussing anxiety

Posture (status)

Humans constantly negotiate status.

High-status signals:

- relaxed movements

- slow movements

- taking space

- exposing torso

Low-status signals:

- hunched shoulders

- compressed posture

- self-hugging

- making body smaller

The biggest signal:

Movement speed.

Powerful people move more slowly because they feel less urgency.

Feet (the hidden truth)

Many investigators and negotiators watch feet more than faces.

Faces are controlled.

Feet are not.

Examples:

- Feet toward exit → wants to leave

- Feet toward person → interest

- One foot already pointed away → partial disengagement

Feet often reveal intentions seconds before actions.

The Neck

The neck is surprisingly important.

Common stress behaviors:

- touching throat

- rubbing side of neck

- pulling shirt collar

- covering suprasternal notch (base of throat)

The neck protects the airway.

When threatened psychologically, people often guard it unconsciously.

The "Freeze"

Perhaps the most important tell.

People think anxiety looks like movement.

Often it looks like stillness.

Sudden reduction in:

- blinking

- movement

- speech

- gesture

can indicate:

- fear

- surprise

- threat assessment

Predators freeze.
Prey freeze.
Humans freeze.

Narrative Tells (useful for fiction)

For character writing, the most revealing signals are not generic body language but recurring self-regulation habits.

Examples:

- Character always licks lips before lying.

- Character always swallows before saying something vulnerable.

- Character touches watch when thinking about the future.

- Character rubs wedding ring when uncertain.

- Character straightens posture before challenging authority.

- Character looks at exits whenever trapped.

Readers perceive these as psychologically real because real people develop individualized regulatory rituals.

The deepest principle is that body language is often not communication. It is self-regulation. Most tells emerge when the body is unconsciously trying to manage fear, desire, uncertainty, shame, attraction, status, or cognitive overload. Those moments create the richest observational details.
