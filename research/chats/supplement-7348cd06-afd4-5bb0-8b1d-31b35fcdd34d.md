**The passage has the right central caution, but it is not the full story.** It compresses a broad set of experiments into three drug rows and draws the cue response distinction too sharply.

The most important correction is that **limited responses to later cues are real**. In controlled hypnosis research, selected, highly hypnotizable participants have performed instructed tasks after a posthypnotic cue. A small placebo controlled study also found that oxytocin increased some socially unusual responses, such as swearing or dancing, to suggestions; its participants were selected for high hypnotizability. These findings do **not** establish that someone can secretly drug an unwilling person, install an arbitrary instruction, and reliably activate it later. They do mean the cheat sheet should avoid implying that all later cue effects are fictional. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

The historical scope also needs expanding. Recovered MKULTRA records describe **149 subprojects**, including **eight concerning hypnosis, two of which combined hypnosis with drugs**. The earlier BLUEBIRD/ARTICHOKE work included overseas interrogations using sodium pentothal and hypnosis. The Senate record documents objectives, funding, and abuses more clearly than it documents reproducible outcomes. Many project files were destroyed in 1973, and a 1963 inspector general report said some test planning and approvals were not recorded at all. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

There is a sourcing trap in the original scopolamine row: the discussion of its failure as a “truth serum” appears in a **historical review reproduced in the Senate hearing**. Its presence in that volume does not, by itself, show that a particular MKULTRA subproject tested scopolamine. The review describes confusion, amnesia, hallucinations, and other effects that undermined interrogation. Modern human research likewise finds impaired memory and information processing. So I would revise the conclusion to: **MKULTRA and related programs genuinely investigated drugs, hypnosis, interrogation, and behavioral influence, sometimes through grave nonconsensual abuse. Human studies demonstrate impairment, altered suggestibility, and some narrow posthypnotic cue effects. The available record does not demonstrate a reliable method for covertly programming an unwilling person to carry out a specific later action.** Because the archive is incomplete, that last sentence is a limit on what the evidence establishes, not proof that every attempted experiment is known.
