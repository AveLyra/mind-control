**Layman Version**
We tested the “machine” using real public sleep records, but only as a simulation. No sound was played to anyone. We asked: if we wanted to time pink-noise sounds to deep sleep, how often would the right sleep state actually be available?

The answer: less often than you might think. The deep-sleep stage you would want for slow-wave pink-noise experiments, N3, is scarce and uneven. In 153 public sleep records, 39 had no usable N3 windows under our standard timing rule. There were far more possible sham/control windows than real deep-sleep target windows.

So the useful conclusion is not “pink noise influences people.” We did not test that. The useful conclusion is: a real experiment would need careful planning, because the target sleep state is limited, front-loaded early in the night, and uneven across people/nights.

**Psych Research Version**
We ran a dry-run scheduling analysis over 153 Sleep-EDF cassette hypnograms, using EDF+ annotations to identify N2, N3, and REM intervals. We simulated cue-opportunity selection for a slow-wave pink-noise condition, an N2 sham condition, and a REM off-target condition. Outcomes were synthetic, so no biological response inference is licensed.

Under the standard rule, 30 s edge buffer and 90 s spacing, we found:

- N2 sham candidate windows: `20,792`
- N3 slow-wave candidate windows: `2,659`
- REM off-target candidate windows: `8,083`
- N3/N2 candidate ratio: `0.1279`
- N3/REM candidate ratio: `0.3290`
- Recordings with zero eligible N3 windows: `39 / 153`
- Median N3 candidates per recording: `11`
- Median N2 candidates per recording: `135`
- N3 minutes in first sleep third: `53.21%`

Design implication: N3 availability is a major feasibility constraint. A balanced within-night closed-loop acoustic stimulation design may require discarding many N2/REM opportunities, stratifying by N3 availability, screening nights, using unequal condition sizes, or defining the estimand over eligible opportunity windows rather than clock time. Conservative buffers amplify the constraint: N3 candidates dropped from `4,936` under dense scheduling to `499` under very conservative scheduling.

Claim ceiling: this supports feasibility and design-risk conclusions only. It does not estimate pink-noise effects on sleep, memory, affect, dreams, or evaluation.
