**What is the context of this project?**

Sounds linked to a daytime activity can sometimes reactivate memories during sleep. In a recent study, researchers reported that dream reports after a sound cue during REM sleep contained more details from the cued task than from an uncued task—although details from both tasks appeared often. Other research has reported effects at different sleep stages and times, so the size and timing of these effects remain open questions. [Konkoly et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2025.109229); [Picard-Deland & Nielsen](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.13391)

**What is the significance of this project?**

Dreams are difficult to study because people remember them imperfectly, and expectations can shape what they report. A carefully controlled test can help show whether a quiet, previously learned sound cue changes which task details appear in dream reports—and whether any apparent effect comes with sleep disruption. The findings would clarify the limits of this specific technique; they would not establish broad or lasting control over dreams.

**What are the goals of the project?**

We will conduct a small feasibility pilot with consenting adults in a sleep research facility. Participants will learn two simple tasks, each paired with a different sound. During monitored sleep, we will randomly play a cue or use comparison periods, then collect dream reports before revealing the condition. Reviewers who do not know which cue was played will score the reports for task-related details. We will also track cue recognition and sleep disruption. We plan to share the protocol and results, including null or inconclusive findings.

**Additional Info (optional)**

This is an exploratory pilot, not a confirmatory test. Participants will be told that benign sounds may be played during sleep; cue identity and timing may be concealed until reports are collected if approved by the reviewing body. Not remembering a sound will not count as proof that it went undetected. Recruitment will begin only after required ethics review and sleep-facility access are secured.
