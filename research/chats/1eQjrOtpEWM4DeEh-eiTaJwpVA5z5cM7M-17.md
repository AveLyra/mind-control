Yes. I underplayed what we can do independently. **We can produce a substantive research result without first joining a university**, though a new sleep experiment with volunteers is a much bigger step.

| Route | What we could do | Main limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reanalyse existing human data** | Test a prespecified question using the [Siefert sleep-cueing dataset](https://www.nemar.org/dataset/on005121): EEG and cue events from 34 participants, with [behavioral data and analysis code](https://github.com/schapirolab/Siefert2024) released separately. | It tests memory cueing within that study’s design, not the implantation of new beliefs. |
| **Publish a methods result** | Extend our [Sleep-EDF dry run] (local source path omitted) into a reproducible study of when cue delivery is feasible and how scheduling rules change the available sleep windows. | The current run delivered no cues and used synthetic outcomes; it is not evidence of influence. |
| **Run an original, consented pilot** | Design a small study, seek review from an independent ethics board, then recruit participants. [HHS guidance](https://opa.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/2021-05/irb-tip-sheet-2020_0.pdf) explicitly describes commercial or independent IRBs as an option for independent researchers. | Sleep staging, arousal checks, participant protections, and review make this substantially more costly. |

We could [preregister a new analysis on OSF](https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations) before examining the relevant outcome data. For pilot funding, [Experiment.com](https://experiment.com/start) explicitly welcomes independent researchers and reviews projects on a rolling basis; it is crowdfunding, so approval to launch does not guarantee funding. By contrast, [NSF does not provide direct funding to unaffiliated individuals](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-1-pre-submission).

**My recommendation:** start with the open human dataset. It gives us a way to challenge the sleep-influence hypothesis using actual cue, EEG, and behavioral records while keeping the stronger “inception” question clearly identified as unanswered.
