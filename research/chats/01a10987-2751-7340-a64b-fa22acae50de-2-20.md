Yes—there’s evidence for semantic processing without being able to report the words, but that’s different from speech being physically inaudible.

- **Very quiet speech while awake:** One experiment found that quiet spoken words could prime responses to semantically related words even when participants couldn’t reliably identify the primes. But participants could still detect that a sound was present, and the priming was modest and strongest in slower responders. Other auditory masking work found repetition effects but no semantic priming under its conditions, so results depend on the method. [*Subliminal Semantic Priming in Speech*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3105000/), [Kouider & Dupoux, 2005](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01584.x)

- **Speech during sleep:** Small studies found EEG responses consistent with distinguishing related from unrelated words during sleep. In one study, people practiced a word-classification task while awake; during N2 sleep, their brain activity still showed response preparation appropriate to the meanings of new words, while their later recognition of the sleep-played words was at chance. That supports some processing without conscious access or later recall, but it doesn’t show that sleepers understood or accepted complex suggestions. [Kouider et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4175175/), [Brualla et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9607517/)

- **Physically below the hearing threshold:** I found evidence that near-threshold *tones* can produce neural responses during sleep, but that doesn’t establish that a sleeping brain extracts word meaning from speech below the person’s detection threshold. At least one sleep-word study set playback above each participant’s measured detection threshold, while using background noise to make the words less salient. [Near-threshold tone study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21886362/), [sleep-word study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4231834/)

So the evidence supports **limited semantic processing without reportable awareness**. It’s much weaker for **understanding genuinely undetectable speech**, and it doesn’t show that subliminal audio can reliably implant beliefs or direct someone’s behavior. EEG markers such as the N400 are indirect evidence of processing, not proof of full, conscious-like comprehension.
