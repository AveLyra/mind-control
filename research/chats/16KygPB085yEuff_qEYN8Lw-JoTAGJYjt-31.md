**I found documented covert US funding historically, and openly acknowledged US and Israeli funding for later research on drugs, learning, and memory.** For China and India, the evidence requires care: an internationally coauthored paper with government support does not necessarily mean that government financed the experiments conducted abroad.

This is a focused search of public records and funding disclosures, not an exhaustive audit of classified research.

## 2. Later research with disclosed government support

### United States: scopolamine and human memory

A **2005 human study** investigated scopolamine’s effects on brain activity associated with forming long-term memories. Its funding record identifies grants from **NIMH, NIDA, and NINDS**, all NIH institutes. This is a direct example involving a substance on your list and publicly documented US government support. [Original study and grant identifiers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16207870/)

The scopolamine depression studies discussed earlier also included research conducted at **NIMH**. Their disclosed purpose was antidepressant treatment. [NIMH’s account](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-updates/2010/rapid-antidepressant-action-of-common-medication-confirmed-by-repeat-trial)

### Israel: scopolamine and associative learning

**Barak and Weiner, 2010**, at Tel Aviv University, studied scopolamine-related changes in *latent inhibition*: how prior exposure to an irrelevant stimulus affects later learning about it.

The university’s publication record explicitly identifies **Israel Science Foundation grant 1234/07** and a university fellowship. This was animal research concerning attention and models of psychiatric impairment. [Funding disclosure](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/dissociating-scopolamine-induced-disrupted-and-persistent-latent--2/), [original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20179909/)

The ISF is predominantly financed through Israel’s public higher-education funding system. That supports describing this as publicly funded research; the cited records provide no evidence of intelligence-service sponsorship. [ISF funding description](https://www.ipmp.isf.org.il/partners.aspx?p=7)

## 3. Research involving China and India

### China–Israel: verified joint funding for human learning research

**Shi and colleagues, 2023**, studied how people learn relationships between sequences of spoken and written words. The work involved Beijing Normal University and an Israeli collaborator; MRI scanning occurred at Peking University.

The paper acknowledges an **NSFC–Israel Science Foundation joint project, grant 31861143040**. This is a verified example of research conducted in China within a jointly supported Chinese–Israeli program. It involved learning tasks and brain imaging, with no drug intervention. The acknowledgment does not establish how funding was divided between countries. [Original paper, including methods and funding](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10028649/)

### India–United States: relevant drug research, with an important funding distinction

A **2019 study by Konar and colleagues** examined scopolamine-related memory impairment and potential recovery mechanisms in mice and neuronal cultures. Its methods identify **Banaras Hindu University, India**, for animal research.

The acknowledgment distinguishes:

- **Indian government support** for several participating researchers.
- **US National Institute on Aging support** for US collaborator Debomoy Lahiri, including grants **R01AG051086, P30AG010133, and R41AG053117-01**.

This establishes an India-based experimental study with an NIH-supported US collaborator. **It does not establish that NIH directly paid for the Indian experiments.** [Original paper and funding disclosure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6769020/)

### A China–US lead that does not establish experiments in China

A **2020 scopolamine-related study** included Chinese institutional affiliations and disclosed NIH support. However, its animal ethics statement identifies the **University at Buffalo**. I therefore would not count the affiliations alone as evidence of US-funded animal experiments conducted in China. [Full paper, funding and ethics statements](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2020.599389/full)
