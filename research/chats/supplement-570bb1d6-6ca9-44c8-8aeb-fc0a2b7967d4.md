I conflated **receptivity with execution**. That was the wrong distinction.

## In layman’s terms

For execution, the proposed role of absorption is to **keep the suggested action or experience dominant, with less competition from other mental activity**. The brain remains engaged in producing the response rather than repeatedly switching away from it. Supporting this, adding a competing mental task experimentally disrupted some hypnotic motor and cognitive responses. That supports an attentional contribution to execution, although it does not establish that absorption alone causes greater responsiveness. This is what “disrupting engagement” should have referred to.  [source 0](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207149908410031)

The color experiment demonstrates why this matters: a suggestion can affect **processes involved in constructing an experience**, not merely what someone says about it afterward. In eight highly hypnotizable participants, instructions to experience color or grayscale changed activity in color-processing regions despite the presented image remaining unchanged. The small, selected sample limits generalization, but the finding is more substantial than verbal compliance alone.  [source 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910791/)

**Placebo effects overlap with this principle: expectations can influence the neural machinery producing symptoms.** In some placebo-pain experiments, expecting relief recruits the body’s own opioid-based pain regulation. Researchers have also measured reduced pain-related activity in the spinal cord. The nervous system is changing its processing of the painful stimulus—not merely attaching a more positive description to unchanged processing.  [source 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.07.014)  [source 3](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1180142)

Religious faith can provide a framework for engaging these regulatory processes. In one experiment, contemplating religious imagery reduced experimental pain in practicing Catholics and engaged a prefrontal region associated with emotional regulation. That offers a plausible route from personally meaningful belief to genuine relief. It does **not** establish that all faith-healing experiences have this explanation, or that relief demonstrates repair of the underlying disease.  [source 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pain.2008.07.030)

## In technical terms

**Execution and experienced authorship are separable.** A motor suggestion must recruit systems capable of producing movement; absorption may help sustain the relevant representation and attentional configuration. In a 30-person experiment, motor imagery during hypnosis produced greater corticospinal activation in highly than minimally hypnotizable participants. This shows modulation of motor-system excitability, not proof that absorption automatically triggers movement. Separately, *cold-control theory* proposes that an implemented intention can be inaccurately represented as unintentional—explaining “it happened by itself” without requiring a separate controller.  [source 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7547693/)  [source 6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2020.11.006)

**For perception, the proposed mechanism is top-down modulation.** Activity associated with an instructed or imagined experience influences sensory processing, rather than sensory processing being determined solely by incoming stimulation. The color study found suggestion-dependent blood-flow changes in fusiform/lingual color-processing regions. Those findings support altered perceptual processing; they do not, by themselves, establish the exact feedback pathway or prove that suggested and ordinary perception are neurologically identical.  [source 7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910791/)

**For some placebo analgesia, the mechanism is more causally established.** A relevant descending pathway involves the rostral anterior cingulate cortex, periaqueductal gray, and rostral ventromedial medulla, with downstream regulation of spinal pain processing. Naloxone, which blocks opioid receptors, reduced placebo analgesia and abolished placebo-associated coupling between anterior cingulate cortex and periaqueductal gray. This pharmacological intervention provides stronger mechanistic evidence than a brain-activation correlation alone.  [source 8](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.07.014)  [source 9](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1180142)

However, **hypnosis, placebo, and religious coping are not one interchangeable mechanism**. In a study of 28 devout Protestants, religious prayer reduced experimental pain, but naloxone did not significantly remove the effect. Attention and interpretation of pain may therefore contribute through routes other than opioid-mediated descending inhibition.  [source 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5487465/)

Finally, these effects are not necessarily limited to subjective experience: after a flavored drink had been paired with an immunosuppressive drug, the drink subsequently elicited measurable immune suppression when paired with placebo instead. That was **learned physiological conditioning**, not evidence that belief alone can cure disease. Conversely, an asthma experiment found substantial reported relief with placebo interventions but no improvement in measured airflow beyond the no-treatment condition. **A real change in experience, a measurable physiological change, and correction of a disease are three different outcomes.**  [source 11](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.02-0389com)  [source 12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3154208/)
