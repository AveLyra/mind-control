**Yes—there is additional research directly relevant to suggestibility and hypnosis. For scopolamine specifically, I found historical research proposals and controlled memory experiments, but no convincing modern trial establishing reliable enhancement of hypnotic responsiveness.**

These are the most relevant additional records:

### 1. CIA research explicitly proposed testing scopolamine and hypnosis

**MKULTRA Subproject 73, established in 1957**, explicitly proposed investigating whether drugs—including scopolamine—could increase susceptibility to hypnosis, and whether drug-induced states resembled hypnosis.

This directly documents government research interest in your question. **The proposal does not establish which experiments were completed or what their results were.** The CIA’s indexed document text was accessible, but its PDF link redirected during retrieval, limiting verification of the complete file. [CIA proposal](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/MKULTRA%20SUBPROJECT%2073%20%20%285%5B8145533%5D.pdf), [archival catalog describing the funding approval](https://nl.sub.uni-goettingen.de/collection/nlh-ddr?filter%5B0%5D%5Byear_publish%5D=1957&filter%5B1%5D%5Bfacet_publisher%5D=CENTRAL+INTELLIGENCE+AGENCY&filter%5B2%5D%5Blang%5D=eng)

### 2. Scopolamine and false recognition: a useful counterexample

**Mintzer and Griffiths, 2001**, conducted a placebo-controlled study of recognition memory. Scopolamine reduced both correct recognition and false recognition of associated words. Participants also became more conservative in some recollection judgments.

This challenges the assumption that impaired memory automatically makes someone more likely to accept false information. However, the experiment tested word recognition, **not interpersonal persuasion or hypnosis**.

The funding record lists **US NIDA grants DA-03889 and DA-11936**. [Original study and funding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11243489/)

### 3. LSD and measured suggestibility

The **2015 study of ten volunteers** discussed earlier directly measured suggestibility and found an increase on the Creative Imagination Scale, but no significant change on a separate imagery test.

It provides limited experimental support for drug-enhanced responsiveness to suggestions. Its small sample and narrow outcomes do not establish general obedience or lasting influence. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/)

### 4. LSD and social influence: a later, more specific experiment

**Duerler and colleagues, 2020**, studied **24 volunteers** using a randomized, double-blind crossover design and an aesthetic-judgment task.

Participants showed greater adjustment toward other people’s ratings **when those ratings were relatively similar to their own**. The effect did not amount to indiscriminate conformity to strongly conflicting opinions. This is evidence of a specific change in social influence processing, with clear limits. [Original study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-68899-y)

### 5. Current research directly combining medication and hypnosis

The **Hypnodelic project at Leiden University**, scheduled for **2024–2026**, proposes a controlled study of drug-assisted hypnosis in **36 healthy volunteers**, measuring suggestibility and subjective experiences.

It is publicly funded through the **EU’s Marie Skłodowska-Curie programme**. The official record describes a research plan; it does **not** provide completed efficacy results. [Official project record](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101151810)

### What the evidence supports

**Some drugs can alter responsiveness to suggestions or social feedback.** That possibility should not be dismissed. But the evidence also shows that effects depend on the task and individual, and memory impairment can occur without increased acceptance of false information.
