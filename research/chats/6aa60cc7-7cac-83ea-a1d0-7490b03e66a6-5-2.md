## Bibliography

**[1] Zhao, H., Lin, X., Yuan, K., et al. (2025).** “Updating social evaluation during sleep.” *npj Science of Learning*, 10, 65. doi:10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9.
Key source for the finding that positive information presented during slow-wave sleep could alter subsequent social evaluation and representations. [DOI, editorial resolution](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00356-9)

**[2] Carbone, J., & Diekelmann, S. (2024).** “An update on recent advances in targeted memory reactivation during sleep.” *npj Science of Learning*, 9, 31. doi:10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8.
Review of TMR across declarative, procedural, and emotional memory; forgetting; REM sleep; dream content; cue timing; and potential applications. [DOI, editorial resolution](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8)

**[3] Xia, T., & Hu, X. (2026).** “Memory editing during sleep: mechanisms, clinical applications, and technological innovations.” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 30(4), 335–349. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2025.07.010.
Introduces an integrated framework for sleep-based “memory editing,” including extinction, interference, new information during reactivation, closed-loop neuromodulation, wearables, and neural decoding. [DOI, editorial resolution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.07.010)

**[4] Haar Horowitz, A., Cunningham, T. J., Maes, P., & Stickgold, R. (2020).** “Dormio: A targeted dream incubation device.” *Consciousness and Cognition*, 83, 102938. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2020.102938.
Introduces Dormio and targeted dream incubation: detecting sleep onset and repeatedly presenting selected information during hypnagogia to influence subsequent dream content. [DOI, editorial resolution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2020.102938)

**[5] Salvesen, L., Capriglia, E., Dresler, M., & Bernardi, G. (2024).** “Influencing dreams through sensory stimulation: A systematic review.” *Sleep Medicine Reviews*, 74, 101908. doi:10.1016/j.smrv.2024.101908.
Systematic review of 51 publications involving auditory, somatosensory, olfactory, visual, vestibular, and multimodal stimulation. Reported stimulus-dependent dream changes ranged from 0 to about 80%, illustrating both the reality and substantial variability of dream influence. [DOI, editorial resolution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2024.101908)
