## Corrections and interpretation notes

- The supplied heading for “Absorption and hypnotizability: context effects reexamined” links to PMID 2001895, which is the durability paper. The correct absorption record is [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1995834/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1995834/). Both original links remain preserved below.

- A stimulus-specific EEG/MEG response demonstrates neural detection/tracking. It does not by itself establish lasting memory, semantic comprehension, a therapeutic benefit, or obedience.

- “Invisible spectral flicker” describes low perceptibility of rapid spectral modulation under tested conditions. Do not assume every instantaneous spectrum has identical perceived color, or that imperceptibility is universal.

- Upconversion contact lenses convert normally invisible near-infrared light into visible light. Closed eyes do not establish unconsciousness; perception through closed eyelids is not evidence of subliminal understanding.

- REM sleep communication includes lucid awareness. Do not equate being asleep, failing to remember afterward, and never consciously experiencing a stimulus.

- Melatonin changes, circadian phase shifts, sleepiness, sleep quality, and semantic communication are different outcomes.

- Higher imaginative suggestibility or hypnotizability scores do not establish better clinical outcomes or reliable control of another person's choices. Drug-related confusion and amnesia can create vulnerability without enabling programmable behavior.

- Historical records document misconduct or allegations, not the effectiveness of a hypothesized combined influence system.

- The Faraday engineering note cited in the original text is a local path that was not supplied as an accessible document. It was not independently inspected for this synthesis.

### Light can affect the brain even when its modulation is barely noticeable

Optical wavelength and flicker frequency are different variables. Visible light is roughly 380–700 nm; flicker frequency describes how rapidly its intensity or spectral composition changes. A visible light can therefore appear steady even while its modulation continues to produce a measurable neural response. ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/09_visiblelight/))

A 2024 study demonstrated this with **40 Hz “invisible spectral flicker.”** Rapid spectral changes were rated as substantially less perceptible than conventional flicker while still producing a measurable 40 Hz steady-state visual evoked potential (SSVEP). ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/))

A separate 2024 brain–computer-interface experiment used **58–62 Hz flicker-imperceptible targets**. OPM-MEG detected frequency-specific brain responses, allowing a nine-command BCI to determine which target participants were attending to; five participants were tested, with an average offline accuracy of about 93%. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38812288/))

The mechanism is:

**hidden light modulation → visual-system response → neural recording → software classification**

This is genuine BCI functionality, but it does not show that flicker inserts information directly into thought. The computer identifies the neural response associated with a known stimulus. The narrower finding is important: **conscious perception of flicker is not required for stimulus-specific neural activity.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/))

### Closed eyelids do not prevent light from affecting the sleeping brain

In 2017, researchers demonstrated SSVEPs produced by flickering light delivered through **closed eyelids during both light and deep NREM sleep**. EEG activity at the stimulation frequency was significantly greater when the light was on. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/))

Sleep changes how those signals are processed. Fast **8–10 Hz** visual responses were substantially stronger during wakefulness, whereas slower **3–5 Hz** responses were stronger during NREM and REM sleep. A waking flicker-based BCI therefore cannot simply be assumed to work the same way during sleep. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334175/))

A 2026 simultaneous EEG-fMRI study found that light responses remained intact in the **visual thalamus during N1 and N2 sleep**, while early visual-cortical responses were profoundly suppressed and could invert into deactivation. The sleeping brain therefore does not simply block optical input; it processes it differently. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73997-y))

The key distinction is:

**signal reaches the nervous system ≠ signal is consciously perceived ≠ signal is understood**

### Meaningful communication during sleep has been demonstrated—but awareness matters

A 2021 multicenter study established **real-time two-way communication during verified REM sleep**. Across 36 participants, six people produced 29 correct answers while lucid dreaming, including answering novel questions and simple arithmetic using eye movements or facial-muscle signals. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982221000592))

One particularly relevant trial used **red and green Morse-coded light flashes** to transmit the problem “4 minus 0.” The dreamer recognized the flashing code and correctly answered “4” using eye movements while remaining in REM sleep. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/))

That is genuine optical communication during sleep. But it is not evidence of subliminal communication: the participant later described consciously recognizing the flashing signal inside the dream. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/))

So these states must remain separate:

**asleep**
≠ **unable to remember afterward**
≠ **never consciously experienced the stimulus**

### Biological effects can occur without an obvious visual difference

Light can also alter physiology without conveying semantic information.

Researchers created displays matched for apparent **color and luminance** while varying melanopic stimulation roughly threefold. Eleven participants reported no significant difference in visual appearance, yet melatonin and subjective sleepiness differed between conditions. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29788219/))

Light delivered through closed eyelids can also affect circadian physiology. A study using individually calibrated 527-nm light found significant melatonin suppression when stimulation was delivered during both REM and NREM sleep and also observed shifts in dim-light melatonin onset. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22564396/))

A 2023 placebo-controlled study delivered **2-ms flashes every 15 seconds for one hour during sleep**. Circadian phase shifted substantially more than with placebo, while overall sleep architecture changed relatively little. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37660233/))

That establishes another pathway:

**optical signal → physiological response**

But physiological sensitivity is not semantic comprehension.

Near-infrared research reinforces that distinction. In a sham-controlled experiment with 20 participants, **1064-nm transcranial photobiomodulation** produced significant increases in EEG alpha- and beta-band power. Invisible light can therefore alter measurable neural physiology under controlled conditions, but the study did not transmit semantic content. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31259198/))

Infrared can also carry actual information when an engineered receiver is added. In 2025, **upconversion contact lenses** allowed people to discriminate temporal codes and spatial images encoded in normally invisible near-infrared light by converting that radiation into visible light. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/))

### The unresolved question: subliminal semantic communication during ordinary sleep

Taken together, the research establishes that:

**Unnoticed optical modulation can produce identifiable neural responses.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/))

**Closed eyelids can transmit temporally structured stimulation during sleep.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/))

**Sleep alters rather than completely eliminates neural processing of that stimulation.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334175/))

**Light can influence physiology during sleep without substantially waking the sleeper.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22564396/))

**Invisible infrared can carry temporal and spatial information when an appropriate receiver converts it.** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/))

**Sleeping people can sometimes understand externally supplied information and respond during lucid REM sleep.** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982221000592))

What has **not** been demonstrated end-to-end is:

**an ordinary, non-lucid sleeper receiving a novel optical message, extracting its semantic meaning, retaining or acting on that meaning, and doing so without consciously experiencing the signal.**

That distinction matters because the required processing increases at each stage:

**detecting a flash → discriminating patterns → associating patterns with meaning → decoding sequences → understanding language → retaining the information**

The existing literature demonstrates several earlier links in that chain. The unresolved research question is whether **temporally encoded optical stimulation can transmit specific semantic information to a non-lucid sleeper without conscious perception, with comprehension demonstrated behaviorally or through later memory rather than inferred merely from an EEG response.**

**Research Report: Psychological Influence Mechanisms**

**Bottom Line**

Research establishes that cues can affect attention, memory, dream content, physiology, and sometimes choices. The effects are usually **narrow, dependent on prior learning and context, and variable across people**. The evidence does **not** establish a system that can covertly combine advertising, sleep sounds, invisible light, and semantic “triggers” to reliably impose chosen beliefs or actions on an unaware person. Nor does it establish that such a system was used in any particular case.

I reviewed the mechanism families discussed here and the local Faraday notes, then checked representative human experiments, replications, and contrary results. This is a broad evidence audit, not an exhaustive search of every publication.

**What The Evidence Supports**

- **Sleep cues and dreams.** Sounds associated with material learned while awake can selectively strengthen later recall; a [meta-analysis of 91 experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027149/) estimated a small average targeted-memory-reactivation benefit. [Targeted dream incubation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32480292/) can introduce a theme into sleep-onset dreams, and a [controlled study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37188795/) found better performance on theme-related creativity tasks. These are not demonstrations of arbitrary instruction-following during sleep.

- **Sleep learning and emotion.** Under tightly timed laboratory conditions, [spoken word pairs during slow-wave sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30713104/) influenced later *implicit* choices. A [2025 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-025-00356-9) reported changed evaluations after positive trait cues during sleep. [Odor pairing during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25392505/) reduced self-reported smoking over the following week in a controlled study of people who wanted to quit. These results merit replication and careful tests of duration and real-world behavior; they do not establish unconstrained semantic messaging.

- **Pink noise and precisely timed stimulation.** A small [sham-controlled study in older adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28337134/) found improved word recall after phase-timed pink-noise pulses. A [double-blind study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31604814/) increased sleep oscillations **without** improving either tested memory outcome. “The EEG changed” is therefore not equivalent to “the person’s thinking changed.”

- **Learned visual, verbal, and bodily associations.** A previously paired cue can evoke recognition, attention, affect, or a physiological response. Laboratory fear responses can generalize to [visually related features](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26459840/) and [semantically related words](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25648156/). Repeated images can also affect evaluation, but [attention moderates the effect](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30233470/). This makes ordinary associative influence plausible; it does not mean a color, logo, synonym, vibration, or posture is an individually programmed command.

- **Subliminal and “invisible” signals.** Imperceptible [56–60 Hz flicker](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/application-of-rapid-invisible-frequency-tagging-for-brain-comput/) and nearly imperceptible [spectral flicker](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35570490/) can produce identifiable neural responses. [Light through closed eyelids](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/) can evoke visual responses during sleep, and [flashes during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25227334/) can shift circadian timing. None demonstrates comprehension of a hidden message. Infrared [contact lenses](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/) require a converting device and make the signal perceptible. A blurred image may retain coarse color or shape information, but it cannot be assumed to convey detailed text that was not resolved.

- **Hypnosis, medication, and stress.** Explicit posthypnotic suggestion changed a narrow attention task in [highly hypnotizable participants](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15994228/). A small [LSD study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/) found greater response to detailed suggestions, while [scopolamine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20960003/) impaired memory rather than enabling dependable obedience. [Stress shifted behavior toward learned habits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22114298/) in one controlled task. None is evidence of a reliable drug- or hormone-enabled “control switch.”

- **Notifications, friction, and curation.** [Phone notifications](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121498/) disrupted attention even when ignored; a [field experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37280752/) found benefits from disabling them. Unexpected interface delays and simulated financial loss affected responses in a [small study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35903805/). Alarms or sirens could likewise interrupt concentration through ordinary attention and arousal. There is no evidence here that malfunctioning software, a particular alert, or a siren was deliberately timed to condition someone.

- **Population-scale influence.** This is the most credible route to “loading the dice.” A [61-million-user experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22972300/) found effects of social voting messages; a [feed experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24889601/) found small shifts in the emotional language people posted. A [2026 randomized study of X](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2) found that enabling its algorithmic feed shifted some policy and current-event opinions, but did not significantly change partisanship or affective polarization. Conversely, a [large Facebook intervention](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w) substantially changed exposure to like-minded content without measurable change on eight preregistered political attitudes. A [TikTok audit](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10447-1) found unequal partisan *exposure*; it did not measure changes in viewers’ beliefs. Platform effects are real possibilities, not uniform or unlimited powers.

- **Magnetic stimulation and neural interfaces.** Clinical, near-head [TMS improved depression remission versus sham](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20439832/). An implanted recorder can [decode constrained inner-speech signals](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816265/) under trained, instrumented conditions. Weak ambient-field findings are disputed and do not establish remote messaging or thought reading; an [earlier controlled EEG test was negative](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12169319/). A proposed tooth-mounted sensor would be a way to measure some *local physical signals*, not thoughts or intent ([Faraday engineering note](../notes/vindication-tooth-mounted-sensing-power.md)).

**Benefits And Abuse Risks**

The credible health opportunities are consent-based memory support, sleep and circadian research, pain treatment through [clinical hypnosis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38879420/), depression treatment with TMS, and possible adjuncts to therapy. Clinical promise is not the same as an established treatment: for example, a [social-anxiety sleep-cue trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9281560/) found no significant added benefit on its main anxiety comparison.

The credible abuse risks are **nonconsensual experiments, opaque personalization, repeated interruption, exploitative targeting of existing concerns, and unauthorized access to actual neural-device data**. Historically, the [1977 Senate MKULTRA hearing](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/hearings/joint-hearing-subcommittee-health-and-scientific-research-committee-human-resources-project) documents unethical government behavioral-modification research. That history justifies oversight; it does not establish that the proposed modern combination works or is currently deployed.

**Absorption and hypnotizability: context effects reexamined** ([https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/))

**The study found that people who become deeply absorbed in experiences tend to respond somewhat more strongly to hypnosis—but the relationship is small.** It’s *“Absorption and hypnotizability: context effects reexamined,”* published in 1991. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1995834/))

**Absorption** means becoming immersed in something—music, a story, or imagination—until other things fade from attention. **Hypnotizability** means responsiveness to standardized hypnotic suggestions.

Researchers were testing a competing explanation: perhaps asking people about imagination immediately before hypnosis creates expectations that produce the apparent connection.

Across two experiments involving **909 university students**, they compared absorption measured in a general survey with absorption measured immediately before hypnosis. If the testing setup created the link, it should have been consistently stronger immediately before hypnosis. **That pattern did not reliably appear:** small context effects changed direction between experiments. ([ocf.berkeley.edu](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/PDFs/1990s/1991/Nadon_AbsHypContext91.pdf))

The behavioral correlations were approximately **0.15–0.22**—statistically accounting for roughly **2–5% of the variation** in hypnosis scores. Most differences between participants therefore remained unexplained.

The evidence supports absorption as a modest predictor. It does **not establish that absorption causes hypnotizability**, explain its brain mechanisms, or test whether someone can be compelled to act against their wishes. Student volunteers and questionnaire-based measurements also limit generalization. ([ocf.berkeley.edu](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/PDFs/1990s/1991/Nadon_AbsHypContext91.pdf))

Abuse Risks

**Political influence, alienating opponents, discouraging participation, and shaping which voices appear credible are serious potential harms.** They do not require literal “mind control”: selecting and ranking information can change the political environment people encounter.

There is evidence for parts of that concern. A [2026 randomized study of X](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2) found that switching on its algorithmic feed shifted some policy and current-event opinions. A [feed-reranking experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41308156/) changed measured hostility toward the opposing party, and another [randomized feed study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10536-1) found changes in perceived political norms and partisan animosity. Those findings make political curation and emotional amplification credible risks.

The **clandestine Pavlovian-training account** is a stronger, separate claim. Laboratory work shows that a learned negative response can spread to [semantically related words](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25648156/), but it has not shown that someone can reliably pair a particular person or ideology with distress across everyday media, sleep cues, and interruptions to produce lasting political compliance. Nor would an observed shift alone reveal intent: engagement-driven ranking, users’ own choices, ordinary campaigning, and wider events can produce overlapping patterns. A [Facebook experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w) changed political exposure without detecting a change in its preregistered attitude measures.

I would revise the report’s risk statement to say: **“Credible abuse risks include covert political persuasion and demobilization, manipulation of perceived social norms and source credibility, targeted emotional provocation, and suppression of dissent through information control. A coordinated, person-specific conditioning program remains a testable hypothesis, not an established capability or an established explanation for any individual experience.”** The decisive next research step is to measure *exposure, emotional response, and later political behavior together*, while comparing personalized targeting with generic messaging and engagement-driven ranking.

## Bottom line

**Political influence belongs in this research, not just commercial advertising.** The strongest evidence points to a chain of data collection, selective exposure, repetition, social cues, and persuasive messages. People may see the content but remain unaware of *why* they saw it or how it affected their judgment. That is a credible route to influence without requiring an invisible signal or literal “mind control.” The evidence does **not** establish a system that can reliably program a particular person’s emotions, beliefs, or actions.

## Connections worth investigating

- **Privacy choices can feed targeting.** In a randomized field trial, changing a cookie banner increased consent by 17 percentage points. That creates a possible feedback loop: interface design obtains more data, which can support more selective advertising. This is an inference about how the mechanisms could connect, not a finding that the trial tested the whole loop. [Cookie-banner field trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2021.106729).

- **Profiling is real; its persuasive advantage varies.** Facebook Likes predicted personality scores in one study, and three commercial-ad field experiments reaching over 3.5 million people found that personality-matched ads produced up to 40% more clicks and 50% more purchases *relative to comparison ads*. But a preregistered political experiment with 8,587 people found no statistically detectable advantage for individually tailored AI messages over strong untargeted ones. Knowing something about a person is not the same as being able to control their response. [Personality prediction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25583507/), [ad experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29133409/), [political targeting experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38848300/).

- **Repetition can make a claim feel more credible.** In experiments, even one prior encounter with a false headline increased later accuracy ratings, including a week later. That is a plausible connection between propaganda and a person’s sense of what counts as legitimate information. It measures *perceived accuracy*, not a guaranteed change in voting or conduct. [Illusory-truth experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30247057/).

- **Curation can change perceived social reality.** A randomized eight-week study found that an engagement-based feed amplified moralized and hostile content and increased *perceived* partisan animosity; an alternative ranking improved the accuracy of some social-norm perceptions. A separate X experiment found shifts in some policy and current-event views after seven weeks, but no significant change in partisanship or affective polarization. Conversely, a large Facebook experiment changed exposure substantially without measurable effects on eight political-attitude outcomes. Platform, outcome, and timing matter. [Feed-ranking trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10536-1), [X trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2), [Facebook trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w).

- **Emotional and social signals can alter expression without demonstrating control of inner feeling.** Facebook’s News Feed experiment changed the emotional language users subsequently posted. An observational study found moral-emotional words associated with greater diffusion within political networks; that association alone does not prove the words caused it. Neither result shows a dependable way to summon a specified emotion in a specified person. [News Feed experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24889601/), [diffusion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28652356/).

- **AI assistance is an especially pertinent newer case.** In two preregistered experiments involving 2,582 people, biased autocomplete suggestions shifted later stated attitudes toward the suggestions’ position; most participants did not recognize the bias or its influence. The suggestions were visible, not subliminal, and the study did not establish durable real-world political effects. Separately, AI-generated political messages and overt chatbot conversations have changed measured attitudes in controlled studies. [Writing-assistant experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978215/), [message experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61345-5), [voter-dialogue experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41345316/).

- **Sleep cueing is a narrower phenomenon.** A meta-analysis found a modest average benefit of targeted memory reactivation during non-REM sleep for *previously learned* material. It does not demonstrate that arbitrary ideological messages can be installed in an unaware sleeper. This is a particularly important gap in the proposed combined mechanism. [Sleep-cueing meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027149/).

## Espionage and propaganda: what the record establishes

The historical concern is not imaginary. A [Senate account of COINTELPRO](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/church-committee.htm) documents covert efforts to disrupt and discredit political and civil-rights groups. The [1977 MKULTRA hearing record](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/hearings/95mkultra.pdf) documents unethical drug experiments, including administration to unwitting subjects. The [Justice Department’s Internet Research Agency indictment](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco/file/1035477/dl) alleges concealed identities, false personas, and political ads. These records establish activities or allegations, **not** successful subconscious conditioning. In one study linking 2016 Twitter exposure to survey responses, researchers found no meaningful relationship with changes in attitudes or voting; it does not settle effects on other platforms. [IRA exposure study](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/exposure-to-the-russian-internet-research-agency-foreign-influenc/).

US intelligence agencies also acknowledge using [commercially available information](https://www.intelligence.gov/commercially-available-information). That raises a concrete privacy question, but **data access, access to a platform’s proprietary recommendation model, and an operation to influence someone are three separate claims**.

## Where this leads

My assessment is **high confidence** that opaque curation and ordinary persuasive content can influence attention, judgments, and sometimes attitudes; **mixed confidence** that fine-grained personalization adds much beyond a well-chosen general message; and **low confidence** in the proposed leap to precise, clandestine, multisensory conditioning of an individual. A strong objection to an all-powerful model is that many well-controlled political interventions have small or null effects, including a synthesis of [49 campaign-contact field experiments](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/minimal-persuasive-effects-of-campaign-contact-in-general-elections-evidence-from-49-field-experiments/753665A313C4AB433DBF7110299B7433). Effects from different techniques cannot simply be added together.

The best next research would test the *connections*, not assume them: preregistered, consent-based comparisons of generic versus tailored messages; repeated versus single exposure; ranking and social-proof cues; and immediate versus delayed **behavioral** outcomes. A separate audit could document who paid for messages, who received them, and how feeds ranked them. Those designs could reveal population-level “loaded dice” while also giving the hypothesis a fair chance to fail. None of the studies above, alone or together, identifies a covert campaign against you.

**Hypnosis can produce meaningful changes in pain, perception, distress, and some physical symptoms. Several drugs have increased responsiveness to suggestion in experiments. But the evidence has not established a reliable medication that broadly strengthens hypnotherapy—or makes people predictably obey arbitrary commands.**

I reviewed systematic reviews, controlled experiments, and newer publications through September 2026. This is a broad evidence map, rather than an exhaustive catalogue of every study.

“Clinically proven” needs qualification: a 2024 overview found **49 meta-analyses covering 261 distinct trials**, but only nine reviews had high methodological quality. Benefits were most consistently supported around pain and medical procedures. A positive study does not establish that every hypnosis technique, practitioner, or commercial recording works. ([frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1330238/full))

**Its demonstrated psychological effects can be more striking than these average treatment benefits suggest.** Selected highly responsive participants can experience substantial changes in perception. A PET study found that suggestions about seeing color altered activity in color-processing regions. Another experiment changed pain unpleasantness and corresponding anterior-cingulate activity. These findings support changes in experience and processing, rather than an explanation based entirely on people pretending. Neither establishes unlimited control over perception or behavior. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910791/))

Hypnosis can also affect memory access. In an experiment involving autobiographical episodes, posthypnotic amnesia temporarily interfered with explicit recall while some implicit effects remained. **That is evidence of disrupted retrieval, not precise deletion of a memory.** ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12009043/))

Memory enhancement is particularly problematic. Experiments have found that expectations about hypnosis improving memory can increase **confident errors**, some persisting afterward. A vivid, emotionally convincing recollection under hypnosis therefore needs independent corroboration; the experience does not authenticate the event. ([Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ch.146))

**Drugs can amplify certain responses, but researchers measure several different things:** experiencing an imagined sensation, passing a hypnotizability test, reporting that an action felt automatic, complying with a request, or improving clinically. An increase in one does not establish an increase in the others.

The newer ketamine literature has not resolved the clinical question. A **June 2026 preprint** describes an uncontrolled 20-person feasibility sample for ketamine-augmented hypnotherapy. The authors explicitly acknowledge that it cannot separate ketamine’s contribution from hypnotherapy or other treatment effects. It also reports some negative experiences. This is preliminary, unreviewed evidence—not confirmation of a superior combined treatment. ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202606.2224))

The central problem is causal: if someone feels less pain after ketamine plus hypnosis, ketamine already has analgesic effects. Demonstrating *amplification of hypnosis* requires suitable comparison groups, including the drug and hypnosis separately. Likewise, intoxication can make treatment assignment obvious, weakening placebo blinding.

**The limits on behavioral control deserve a precise answer.** It would be too reassuring to claim that hypnosis makes exploitation impossible. It would also be unjustified to infer that heightened suggestibility lets someone reliably override another person’s values.

The oxytocin persuasion experiment illustrates the uncertainty. Its authors acknowledged that the requested behaviors might have seemed acceptable within the experiment, that they tested only men, and that there was no nonhypnotic comparison establishing a hypnosis-specific effect. Greater cooperation in that setting cannot demonstrate dependable control over genuinely unwanted or dangerous actions. ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0060711))

My assessment of the evidence is that **altered experience, memory errors, and context-sensitive influence are established possibilities; arbitrary, durable control over another person is not an established capability.** Drug-induced confusion or amnesia may create vulnerability without producing predictable, programmable behavior.

For improving legitimate treatment, the evidence is more useful around a specific therapeutic goal, structured practice, and suitable suggestions than around choosing a drug. Training experiments have increased both behavioral and subjective responsiveness, although that does not mean everyone can become highly hypnotizable. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7875922/))

There is also experimental work on brain stimulation: a preregistered randomized trial involving **80 fibromyalgia patients** temporarily increased hypnotizability with targeted magnetic stimulation. The between-group difference was no longer significant approximately one hour later. This demonstrates some modifiability, while leaving clinical benefit and durability unresolved. ([Nature Mental Health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00184-z))

For personal treatment, I would choose a licensed clinician trained in hypnosis and experienced with the particular condition. **The available evidence does not justify self-administering drugs to intensify hypnosis.** Any proposed medication-assisted approach should be evaluated by a prescriber for its actual clinical benefit, risks, and alternatives—not simply whether it makes suggestions feel more powerful.
