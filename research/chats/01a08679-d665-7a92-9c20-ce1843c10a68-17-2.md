Yes. That’s the right place to back up to.

I’d restate the hypothesis more cleanly as:

**Pink noise may not be the “message.” It may be a carrier, gate, stabilizer, or state-shaping input. The actual emotional or semantic influence may come from paired associations: ads, phone cues, names, images, notification sounds, social media reward loops, or subliminal/low-awareness material.**

That gives us a much better research frame than “pink noise = mind control.”

The mechanism space to explore is:

1. **State steering**
Pink noise, rhythmic sound, vibration, light, or stimulation may alter sleep depth, arousal threshold, awakenings, hypnagogia, or slow-wave timing.

2. **Timing gate**
The same cue may matter more if delivered at sleep onset, during NREM, REM, post-awakening, deep focus, meditation, or emotional vulnerability.

3. **Associative payload**
The content may come from waking-life material: commercials, social feeds, people’s names, brands, threats, rewards, songs, alerts, or imagery.

4. **Reactivation**
A later sound, vibration, smell, phrase, or visual cue may reactivate already encoded associations rather than implant new ones from scratch.

5. **Emotional tagging**
Influence may work by shifting salience, familiarity, liking, anxiety, urgency, or disgust, not by producing explicit beliefs.

6. **Feedback loop**
If the person notices patterns, the system may become self-reinforcing through attention, expectation, sleep loss, stress, and hypervigilance. That has to be modeled as a competing mechanism, not dismissed.

So I’d change the research question from:

“Can pink noise control the mind?”

to:

**“Can state-shaping stimuli plus paired associative inputs measurably shift attention, dream content, memory salience, emotional valence, or behavior under low-awareness conditions?”**

And we should explicitly compare models:

- **Model A:** pink noise directly causes influence
- **Model B:** pink noise changes sleep/arousal state, making other cues more effective
- **Model C:** waking media/phone cues create associations, and sleep cues reactivate them
- **Model D:** ordinary conditioning plus notification salience explains most effects
- **Model E:** expectation, pattern detection, stress, and sleep disruption explain perceived coordination
- **Model F:** some combination of the above

The next experiments should not start with Sleep-EDF alone. Sleep-EDF helps with sleep-stage feasibility, but not semantic influence. We need a broader scaffold:

- waking conditioning task
- notification/phone cue task
- media priming task
- sleep/rest reactivation task
- pink-noise/state-modulation task
- combined “state + association” task

The key test is interaction:

**Does pink noise or state-timed sound amplify the effect of previously paired associations compared with the same associations without pink noise?**

That’s the bridge. If there is no interaction, then pink noise may just be sleep ambience or disruption. If there is an interaction, then we have something much closer to your imagined mechanism, still without needing to assume intent or attribution.
