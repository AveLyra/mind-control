Totally. That’s a cleaner and much more scientifically tractable question:

**“How far can subconscious or low-awareness influence get using known mechanisms, under ethical conditions?”**

I’d frame it as a capability-boundary project, not a personal attribution project.

The main move is to break “mind control” into measurable layers:

1. **Attention Capture**
Can cues pull attention away without conscious choice?

Test: notification-like sounds, vibrations, abrupt tones, or visual flashes during focus tasks.  
Outcome: reaction time, errors, recovery time, urge to check device.

2. **Affective Priming**
Can low-awareness cues shift mood, threat perception, liking, or aversion?

Test: subliminal or briefly presented words/images/audio textures paired with neutral targets.  
Outcome: target ratings, implicit association tasks, choice bias, physiological arousal.

3. **Conditioned Cue Response**
Can a neutral cue become disruptive or motivational after repeated pairing?

Test: pair one sound with reward/checking/social feedback; pair another with neutral activity. Later test both during unrelated work.  
Outcome: checking urge, distraction, preference, arousal.

4. **Memory Weighting**
Can cues bias what is remembered or forgotten?

Test: learn material with cue associations, replay cues during rest or sleep, compare recall.  
This is close to targeted memory reactivation. Sources: [TMR review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38622159/), [npj Science of Learning review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00244-8).

5. **Dream/Sleep Influence**
Can timed cues affect dream content, next-day memory, or emotional evaluation?

Test: benign prompts around sleep onset or slow-wave sleep versus random-time/no-cue controls.  
Sources: [Dormio/TDI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32480292/), [sensory dream influence review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38417380/).

6. **Evaluation Updating**
Can information delivered during sleep alter later attitudes?

This is the spicy frontier. The 2025 “Updating social evaluation during sleep” paper suggests sleeping semantic input can shift later evaluation of learned identities, though that does not equal arbitrary belief implantation. Source: [PMC paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12398527/).

7. **Accumulated Influence**
Can small effects compound across days?

Test: repeated cue/media/sleep exposures over multiple sessions.  
Outcome: persistence, habituation, sensitization, decay, individual susceptibility.

The “mind control” scale could look like this:

- **Level 0:** no measurable effect
- **Level 1:** attention disruption
- **Level 2:** mood/arousal shift
- **Level 3:** recall or salience bias
- **Level 4:** preference/evaluation shift
- **Level 5:** short-term behavioral bias
- **Level 6:** durable belief/identity/action change

My expectation: current evidence probably supports Levels 1-4 under controlled conditions, sometimes Level 5 for narrow behaviors, and does **not** support Level 6 in the strong sci-fi sense.

For Faraday, each level becomes a separate falsifiable protocol. For Sherlock, each mechanism gets a source dossier: sleep TMR, dream incubation, notification distraction, subliminal priming, conditioning, ad/reward modeling, and neuroethics. Then the project can say, with discipline: “Here is what is achievable, here is what is weakly supported, here is what fails replication, and here is where speculation begins.”
