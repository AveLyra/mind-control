**Yes, in two separate senses—but the records and the modern experiments do not establish one combined technique.**

The [1977 Senate hearing](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf) identifies MKULTRA subprojects involving **drugs, hypnosis, and sleep research**. The [National Research Council’s review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK217781/?report=printable) documents Army Edgewood studies of several antimuscarinic agents on your list. I did **not** find evidence in those records of the specific modern method you describe: measuring a person’s ongoing EEG and timing sounds to the phase of alpha, theta, or slow oscillations.

The modern findings are real, with narrower outcomes than “suggestibility”:

| Finding | What the study supports |
|---|---|
| **Slow waves and spindles** | Timed sounds can change sleep rhythms, but a study that changed slow oscillations and spindles **did not improve the memory task** it measured. [Human study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11009689/) |
| **REM alpha and theta** | Phase-locked sounds changed measured power and frequency in **18 healthy adults**. The study tested brain rhythms, not compliance with suggestions. [Human study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/47/12/zsae193/7745355) |
| **Spindle-triggered sounds** | Sounds delivered during detected spindles changed subsequent activity in **10 adults**; stimulation could also end a spindle early. [Human study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40626105/) |
| **Sleep onset** | In a **21-person** crossover insomnia trial, average sleep-onset time fell by **10.5 minutes** during the stimulation week. Individual results varied considerably, and the comparison was a *silent* sham, so this alone does not isolate the benefit of precise phase timing from hearing sounds. [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38844793/) |

For the **drug list**, the historical match is also selective. The [VA](https://www.publichealth.va.gov/exposures/edgewood-aberdeen/index.asp) confirms Edgewood testing of **atropine and scopolamine**; the [National Research Council](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK217781/?report=printable) discusses **Ditran, benactyzine, EA-3167, EA-3443, and EA-3580**. A researcher’s [sworn testimony in a DEA proceeding](https://maps.org/wp-content/uploads/1988/11/0023.pdf) documents Army-funded **MDA and MDMA animal studies**—that particular evidence does not show MDMA testing in humans. The Senate record documents ARTICHOKE drug and hypnosis work, but some operational drugs were left **unnamed** in the surviving account. [CIA ARTICHOKE memorandum](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/PROJECT%20ARTICHOKE%5B12888369%5D.pdf)

**The gap:** Changing an EEG rhythm, shifting sleep onset, or strengthening a *previously learned* association is not evidence that an unheard verbal command can be understood and obeyed, or that a person can be conditioned into a Manchurian Candidate state. In a sleep-cue experiment, the sound’s effect depended on learning the association **before sleep**. [Human study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25515103/) The strongest objection to a categorical historical claim is that some MKULTRA records were lost or destroyed; the accurate conclusion is that the **records reviewed do not document this specific EEG-timed technique**, not that every historical activity is known.
