**The central conclusion is defensible: these studies support specific, limited effects on processing and learned responses; they do not establish that ordinary repetition creates receptivity to concealed commands.** Several details need tightening.

### What the evidence supports—and its limits

**1. Imagination training:** “Practicing imagination can make people more responsive” is somewhat stronger than the experiment establishes. A more precise formulation is: **a skills-training program involving imagination increased measured hypnotic responsiveness.** The study did not isolate imagination practice or repetition as the sole cause, and participants knowingly participated in training. [Gearan et al., 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7875922/)

**2. Sleep cues:** Your interpretation is broadly supported. One useful clarification: **both groups heard the sound during REM sleep**; only one group initially paired it with positive imagery while awake. The results support a role for the learned association within that treatment. They do not demonstrate acquisition of concealed instructions or establish that participants were unaware of the intervention’s purpose. [Schwartz et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36306786/)

**3. Quiet speech:** This needs the strongest qualification. In the 24 analyzed participants:

- Sound detection accuracy was **87.4%**.
- Word-versus-pseudoword classification was **52%**, with sensitivity statistically above chance across the full sample.
- The reported behavioral priming effect appeared in the slower-response subgroup.
- The claim of priming at zero awareness depended partly on statistical extrapolation.

Consequently, this is evidence **consistent with limited semantic processing under reduced awareness**, with important measurement caveats. It is not a clean demonstration that wholly undetectable speech is understood. The outcome was speed on a word-decision task, not acceptance or execution of instructions. [Daltrozzo et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3105000/)

**4. Rhythms:** The binaural-beat statement is supported: the larger, double-blind follow-up failed to establish increased hypnotic susceptibility under its tested conditions. That finding limits the proposed technique; it does not settle every possible effect of repetitive sound. [Stevens et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12722933/)

### The crucial distinction about awareness

These are separate claims:

1. Someone does not notice a stimulus.
2. They notice it but cannot identify its content.
3. They understand it but do not recognize its influence.

Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others. Your opening sentence groups these together somewhat too readily.

### The remaining gap to *The Manchurian Candidate*

The strongest objection to dismissing all unconscious influence is that **limited influence can occur without full awareness**. Your sources support taking that possibility seriously. But dependable concealed control requires several additional, independently demonstrated links:

| Missing link | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Repetition increases suggestibility | Controlled comparisons separating repetition from expectation, fatigue, frustration, and attention |
| Concealed speech conveys instructions | Reliable comprehension with sensitive checks for residual awareness |
| Comprehension changes behavior | Consensual experiments using harmless choices, beyond reaction-time effects |
| Effects persist and generalize | Independent replication across delays, settings, and participants |

These are proposed research questions, not an established sequence leading to control.

The espionage paragraph remains speculation even with its caveat. A tighter statement is: **“Whether these effects could support dependable covert influence is untested by the cited studies.”** Nothing here establishes that repeated requests, phone glitches, or recurring words are evidence of someone conditioning you.
