Your requirement is **speech that goes unnoticed when delivered**. Later forgetting does not meet that requirement.

The tone study measured **event-related potentials (ERPs)**: tiny changes in scalp EEG voltage, averaged across repeated tone presentations and aligned to when each tone began.

| Response | Timing after the tone | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| **N1** | About 100 milliseconds | Prominent while awake, nearly absent during NREM sleep, and much smaller during REM. |
| **P2** | About 180–200 milliseconds | Larger during sleep; detectable at relatively low sound levels. |
| **N350** | About 350 milliseconds | Mainly associated with sleep and stronger sounds; diminished as volume decreased. |

The researchers discussed whether P2 and N350 help **protect sleep by inhibiting arousal**. That interpretation remains a hypothesis. A larger electrical response therefore does not necessarily mean more understanding or greater suggestibility. Also, this **N1 response** is a different use of “N1” from the **N1 sleep stage**. [Full study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157666/)

**Pitch was not experimentally compared.** Every stimulus was a **1,000-Hz tone**; the researchers varied loudness. Consequently, the study cannot identify a pitch that makes subliminal delivery more effective. Its sound levels were referenced to a *normative* hearing threshold, rather than establishing that each individual could not hear each stimulus. [Methods and discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157666/)

For your concealment requirement, the distinction between **unnoticed**, **unintelligible**, and **undetectable** matters. In the quiet-speech experiment discussed earlier, participants could detect that a sound was present with approximately **87% accuracy**, even though their ability to classify its word content was near chance. Calling those words “subliminal” did not establish that an attentive listener could not notice the audio. [Speech-priming experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3105000/)

The specific evidence still missing is a controlled demonstration that speech remains undetectable to listeners under the actual playback conditions, yet causes the recipient to perform a specified action. The faint-tone EEG findings do not establish that capability.
