**Yes. Actual combination studies exist—including a human study of scopolamine with LSD. My earlier answer was incomplete because it discussed interaction risks without identifying that experiment.** The records also establish concealed CIA funding and continuation beyond MKULTRA. They provide much weaker evidence for successful, predictable control of someone’s behavior.

Below are the strongest findings from the original studies, declassified records, and congressional testimony.

## 1. Research that actually combined drugs

### Scopolamine + LSD: a human experiment published in 1959

Harris Isbell, C. R. Logan, and E. J. Miner published *Studies on Lysergic Acid Diethylamide (LSD-25): III. Attempts to Attenuate the LSD-Reaction in Man by Pretreatment with Neurohumoral Blocking Agents*. This included administering scopolamine and LSD together. [Original journal record](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/archneurpsyc/article-abstract/652816)

Isbell’s contemporaneous report describes comparisons involving the individual drugs, their combination, and placebos. Outcomes included physiological measurements, symptom questionnaires, and clinical ratings of mental effects. For scopolamine with LSD, it reported:

> “No significant diminution or accentuation of the intensity of the LSD reaction could be demonstrated.”

This establishes a **completed human combination experiment with a reported result**. It did not measure hypnotic responsiveness, obedience, or acceptance of suggested beliefs. A statistically nonsignificant result also does not establish that the combination has no interactions or is safe. [Isbell’s original report, “Effects of Various Drugs on the LSD Reaction,” printed p. 363](https://bibliography.maps.org/resources/download/7935)

**Funding limitation:** I have not established a specific CIA funding authorization for this particular published experiment. A researcher’s participation in CIA-connected work does not automatically establish the funding of every paper.

### A modern combination study directly relevant to social influence

The **2020 Duerler study** discussed earlier also included a combination: LSD with **ketanserin**, a serotonin-receptor antagonist.

In 24 participants, researchers compared placebo, LSD, and the combination. LSD increased adjustment toward other people’s aesthetic judgments when those judgments were relatively similar to the participant’s own. Ketanserin blocked the associated LSD-induced changes in brain activity during social-feedback processing.

This is a controlled combination experiment investigating the mechanism of social influence. It does **not** demonstrate that combining drugs increases influence: the added drug blocked a measured effect. The study also lacked a ketanserin-only condition, limiting interpretation. I found no demonstrated connection between this experiment and the historical covert programs. [Original 2020 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-68899-y)

## 2. Human Ecology: concealed funding and combined interventions

### What the funding record establishes

In the 1977 Senate hearing, John Gittinger testified that the **Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology**, subsequently associated with the Human Ecology Fund, distributed CIA money to researchers, including recipients unaware of its origin.

He described a broad portfolio of psychology, sociology, and anthropology, with drug research a relatively small component. That characterization is **his testimony**, not an independent audit of every grant. [1977 hearing, printed pp. 60–63](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf#page=62)

### A specific funded project: Ewen Cameron, Subproject 68

A CIA retrospective cable identifies:

- **Researcher:** D. Ewen Cameron.
- **Institution:** Allan Memorial Institute, McGill University, Montreal.
- **Funding intermediary:** Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology.
- **Recorded objective:** studying behavioral effects of repeated verbal signals.

The cable describes a program involving drugs and other interventions, and explicitly identifies covert funding of research at a Canadian university. It contains favorable claims about results, but does not supply the controls or outcome data needed to establish reliable behavioral control. I verified the CIA’s indexed document text; direct retrieval of the complete record was unreliable. [CIA record, identifier CIA-RDP80M00165A000700140066-1](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp80m00165a000700140066-1)

**Relevance to your question:** This documents combining pharmacological and psychological interventions. It does not isolate the effects of a particular drug mixture.

### What subsequent clinical follow-up found

Schwartzman and Termansen’s **1967 “Intensive Electroconvulsive Therapy: A Follow-Up Study”** examined former Allan Memorial patients who had undergone Cameron’s “depatterning” treatment.

The original sample comprised 79 patients, with smaller subsets available for different assessments:

- Current-status information was obtained for **47**.
- A detailed memory questionnaire was completed by **27**.
- **60% of those questionnaire respondents** reported persistent loss of memories covering periods before treatment.
- Objective testing in another assessed subset showed little evidence of general intellectual or memory impairment—a discrepancy the authors explicitly discussed.
- The authors found no clear long-term clinical advantage over the comparison treatment literature and argued against using intensive ECT as a standard procedure.

This was an incomplete observational follow-up, not a randomized experiment. It cannot separate drug effects from ECT, underlying illness, or other treatment components. It documents concerning outcomes without demonstrating successful implantation of beliefs or dependable obedience. [Original 1967 paper, pp. 217–218](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/070674376701200219)

## 3. MKSEARCH: a documented continuation

The Senate record explicitly identifies **MKSEARCH as the continuation of MKULTRA**, with funding from **fiscal years 1966–1972**. Its stated purpose included developing and evaluating materials and techniques for predictable behavioral or physiological changes in covert operations. [1977 hearing, printed p. 169](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf#page=171)

A September 20, 1977 Defense Department memorandum instead gives an apparent **1965–1973** program span. The documents therefore distinguish imperfectly between program activity, funding, and termination. [DoD General Counsel memorandum, p. 3](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/NCB/02-A-0846_RELEASE.pdf#page=3)

These records establish institutional continuation and intent. **Neither summary establishes a successful drug combination for hypnosis or coercive control.** An objective written into a program authorization is evidence of what researchers sought to achieve.

## 4. OFTEN / CHICKWIT: distinct activities, including concealed sponsorship

The Defense Department’s 1977 review separates several activities:

| Component | What the review describes |
|---|---|
| **CHICKWIT** | Identifying foreign drug developments and collecting information; the reviewed component conducted no human testing. |
| **OFTEN database work** | Converting pharmacological information, including existing clinical data, into computer-accessible records; no new human testing in that component. |
| **OFTEN experimental work** | Animal research and disputed attribution of later human testing. |
| **OFTEN contractor research** | Chemical synthesis research funded through the Navy to conceal CIA sponsorship; the reviewed Navy records did not indicate human testing. |

An authorization quoted in the review states:

> “The work will be unclassified, but Agency association will be confidential.”

That is direct documentary evidence of **concealed sponsorship of otherwise unclassified research**. It does not establish that every resulting publication was secret or that a successful influence technique resulted. [DoD memorandum, pp. 4–7](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/NCB/02-A-0846_RELEASE.pdf#page=4)

### The 1973 human-testing dispute is real

The September 1977 Senate proceedings preserve conflicting accounts:

- **CIA witnesses** said the relevant human tests were Army-funded and outside CIA sponsorship.
- **DoD General Counsel Deanne Siemer** concluded that the June 1973 testing belonged to the Agency’s project.
- Siemer acknowledged limited documentation and explained that her conclusion rested partly on the sequence of CIA funding and renewed Army work.
- Senator Kennedy explicitly challenged the conflicting testimony.

It would be inaccurate to flatten this into either “no human testing occurred” or “CIA sponsorship of every test was conclusively established.” **Human testing was acknowledged; responsibility for the particular tests was disputed.** These passages also do not establish a multiple-drug experiment. [*Human Drug Testing by the CIA*, 1977, printed pp. 127–130 and 149–150; hearing reproduced by Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/human-drug-testing-by-the-cia-1977-hearings-before-us-senate/)

## 5. What remains unresolved

The strongest objection to a sweeping negative conclusion is that **the archive is incomplete**: records were destroyed, and much of the material recovered in 1977 consisted of financial documentation. That limits reconstruction of experiments and outcomes. [1977 MKULTRA hearing](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf)

However, missing records cannot establish what their results were.

For the OFTEN dispute, the hearing identifies particularly useful underlying records: a **May 29, 1973 memorandum to the Director of Research and Development**, an undated document titled **“Influencing Human Behavior,”** and a **February 12, 1975 Edgewood interview/trip report**. Those are specific documentary leads for resolving sponsorship and experimental scope; I have not independently verified their complete contents. [Siemer’s testimony](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/publications/human-drug-testing-by-the-cia-1977-hearings-before-us-senate/)

### Overall assessment

- **Established:** combination experiments occurred; CIA sponsorship was sometimes concealed; behavioral research continued under successor programs.
- **Established for particular studies:** drugs can alter measured psychological responses, and another drug can modify or block some effects.
- **Not demonstrated by the records reviewed:** a reproducible combination producing dependable hypnosis, obedience, or lasting control over someone’s decisions.

The key unanswered scientific question is **whether a combination changes a defined behavioral outcome beyond either drug alone, independently of confusion, memory impairment, expectations, and the surrounding situation**. The historical program summaries do not answer that question.
