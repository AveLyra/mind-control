Yes. That’s fair. I’d revise the interpretation downward and sideways.

What we “squeezed out” of Sleep-EDF is not really science about your core hypothesis. It is **protocol logistics science**: if we ever want to test sleep-state timing, N3 is scarce, uneven, and front-loaded. That matters, but it only answers a narrow setup question.

It does **not** meaningfully test:

- whether pink noise steers consciousness
- whether pink noise increases suggestibility
- whether paired commercial/phone cues create emotional reactions
- whether sleep cues reactivate those associations
- whether subliminal/low-awareness media inputs matter
- whether state steering plus associative payload has an interaction effect

So the corrected conclusion is:

**The Sleep-EDF run tells us whether a slow-wave cueing experiment is feasible to schedule. It does not tell us whether the hypothesized influence mechanism exists.**

The most important thing it revealed is a design constraint: if we focus only on N3 slow-wave pink-noise timing, we may be over-narrowing the hypothesis. Your actual mechanism may involve sleep onset, microarousals, REM, transitions, awakenings, attention states, or waking media/notification loops. Sleep-EDF’s N3 bottleneck may be a warning that “deep sleep pink noise” is only one possible gate, not the whole machine.

So I’d reframe the evidence ladder:

1. **State gate feasibility**
Can we detect or approximate relevant states?
Sleep-EDF partially helps here.

2. **State perturbation**
Can pink noise or rhythmic sound change arousal, sleep depth, awakenings, hypnagogia, or transitions?
Sleep-EDF does not answer this without real stimulation data.

3. **Associative payload**
Can media/phone/subliminal cues create measurable emotional or attentional associations?
Sleep-EDF does not answer this.

4. **Reactivation**
Can later cues reactivate those associations during sleep/rest/focus?
Needs TMR-style data or new experiment.

5. **Interaction**
Does state steering amplify associative reactivation?
This is the real bridge to your theory.

The best next move is not more Sleep-EDF squeezing. It is to design a **2x2 interaction experiment**:

- paired association vs unpaired/control association
- pink-noise/state cue vs sham/no state cue

Primary question:

**Does the combination produce more emotional salience, recall bias, urge, or dream incorporation than either component alone?**

That would directly test the mechanism you’re describing.
