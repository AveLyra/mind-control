**“Highly hypnotizable” primarily describes a high score on standardized tests.** Researchers give suggestions involving movement, sensation, perception, or memory, then assess the responses—for example, an arm feeling difficult to bend or a suggested taste seeming present. In the oxytocin study, participants had to score highly on two separate assessments. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**Why some people respond strongly remains only partly understood.** Several factors are associated with it:

- **Absorption:** becoming deeply immersed in an experience or imagination, with surrounding distractions fading from attention. This is a recurring predictor, but it does not identify everyone who is highly hypnotizable. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
- **Vivid imagined experiences:** one experiment found that highly hypnotizable participants experienced imagery as more vivid and absorbing, without evidence that they were simply trying harder. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
- **Expectations and the situation:** expecting to respond is associated with stronger responses, although findings vary. A positive attitude alone is a weak predictor. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

There also appears to be a **relatively stable individual tendency**: a small longitudinal study found substantial consistency over 25 years. That does not mean responsiveness is fixed or identical in every situation. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

For our earlier discussion, the critical limit is this: **a high hypnotizability score measures responsiveness to particular suggestions under test conditions. It does not establish that someone will obey arbitrary commands against their wishes.** Even the oxytocin researchers acknowledged that their participants might have considered the requested behaviors acceptable within the experiment.
