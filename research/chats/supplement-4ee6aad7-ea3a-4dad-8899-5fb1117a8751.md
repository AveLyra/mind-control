## Findings to emphasize for documented Israeli connections

### Defense-funded hypnosis combined with sensory conditioning

**Abramowitz and Lichtenberg (2010), “A new hypnotic technique for treating combat-related posttraumatic stress disorder,”** has an explicit defense connection. Abramowitz’s listed affiliation was the IDF Mental Health Division, and the paper acknowledges research funding from the **IDF Medical Corps and Israeli Ministry of Defense**, alongside a charitable contribution. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

The intervention combined hypnosis with learning an association between a pleasant smell and feelings of safety and self-control. The smell could subsequently serve as a reminder of that internal state. The study involved **36 patients with combat-related PTSD** and reported improvement, but it was an uncontrolled prospective study—not a randomized demonstration of efficacy. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**Why emphasize it:** it directly connects Israeli defense sponsorship with the relationship you have been examining: **imagery/suggestion → learned association → an external sensory cue influencing an internal experience**.

Its documented purpose was treatment. It did not test unnoticed verbal messages, subliminal semantic comprehension, or intelligence operations. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

### Joint IDF–U.S. Army research on repeated attention training

**Wald and colleagues (2016), “Selective prevention of combat-related post-traumatic stress disorder using attention bias modification training,”** is a particularly clear institutional connection. The author affiliations include both the **IDF Medical Corps’ Mental Health Division** and the **Walter Reed Army Institute of Research, U.S. Army Medical Research and Materiel Command**, alongside university and NIMH researchers. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

The randomized trial involved **719 infantry soldiers** and repeated computerized training intended to change attention to threat. Four training sessions reduced subsequent PTSD risk relative to no training; eight sessions did not produce the same significant comparison. Thus, it does not support a simple “more repetition produces more influence” rule. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**Why emphasize it:** this is direct military collaboration on changing cognitive processing through task experience. A related 2015 study explicitly described the training in terms of **implicitly learned associations** and tested interventions in both Israeli and U.S. combat veterans. Participants consented to treatment; implicit learning did not mean that stimuli or participation were concealed. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

## What is documented on the intelligence side?

The clearest verified intelligence connection is **historical U.S. research**, rather than an Israeli intelligence attribution for these papers.

The **1977 Senate MKULTRA hearing** identifies **eight hypnosis subprojects, including two combining hypnosis and drugs**, plus projects involving sleep and psychotherapy-related behavioral research. Its reproduced historical material also discusses BLUEBIRD/ARTICHOKE objectives involving memory, interrogation, and protection against hostile influence. These were documented intelligence interests and activities—not merely hypothetical applications invented by later commentators. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

The hearing also cites **Martin Orne’s 1958 evaluation, “The potential uses of hypnosis in interrogation,” under an Air Force contract**. That provides a specific historical U.S. defense connection to evaluating hypnosis for intelligence purposes. It does not demonstrate continuity with the modern cold-control or subliminal-speech experiments. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
