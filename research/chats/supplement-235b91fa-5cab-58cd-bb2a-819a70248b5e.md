**This study found that responses to hypnotic suggestions could persist for weeks, but their durability depended on the task.** Unlike the previous paper, which examined who responds to hypnosis, this 1991 study examined how long responses last. [source 0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/)

Researchers compared **36 highly hypnotizable participants** with **18 low-hypnotizable participants instructed to pretend to be hypnotized**. After hypnosis, a later cue was supposed to make participants reenter hypnosis and perform their assigned task. Researchers tested responses six times over eight weeks, using both observer ratings and participants’ reports. [source 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/)

The three suggestions showed different patterns, described in a later paper by two of the same researchers: [source 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11923423_Efficacy_of_Induction_and_Difficulty_Level_in_Durability_of_Posthypnotic_Suggestions)

| Suggested response | What happened over time |
|---|---|
| An arm lowering | Declined least |
| Smelling ammonia that was not present | Declined more |
| Being unable to say their hometown’s name | Declined fastest |

**The interesting finding:** the highly hypnotizable participants showed these differing rates of decline; the people pretending did not show the same statistically significant pattern. That supports a difference between the groups, although it does not establish the mechanism. Participants reporting deeper hypnosis also responded more strongly—but this association appeared **in both groups**, so reported “depth” alone cannot distinguish hypnosis from simulation. [source 3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/)

The main limitations are small groups, repeated testing, and potentially confusing **suggestion type with task difficulty**. A later follow-up found inconsistent durability patterns and no measurable benefit from explicitly instructing participants to reenter hypnosis. These experiments do not establish that suggestions remain continuously active between tests or override someone’s wishes. [source 4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11923423_Efficacy_of_Induction_and_Difficulty_Level_in_Durability_of_Posthypnotic_Suggestions)
