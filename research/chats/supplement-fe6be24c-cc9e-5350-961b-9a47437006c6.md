**Executive summary, in plain language.** The brain can respond to signals that a person does not consciously notice. Those responses can affect body-clock timing, attention, memory, or experience. But **detecting a signal, understanding its meaning, and following an instruction are separate abilities**. The studies reviewed do not establish a reliable method for sending arbitrary instructions to an unaware sleeper.

“Invisible” also describes different things. A lamp can look steady while the brain tracks its rapid flicker. Light can look almost unchanged while affecting melatonin differently. Experimental infrared contact lenses convert otherwise invisible light into something the wearer can see. These are demonstrated effects, but they involve different mechanisms. See the [flicker experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/), [lighting-and-melatonin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29788219/), and [infrared contact-lens study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/). [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Hypnosis likewise has real, bounded effects: it can help some people manage symptoms, and suggestions can change experience. Small experiments suggest that certain drugs increase particular measures of suggestibility. They have **not established a dependable drug that broadly improves hypnotherapy or makes people obey arbitrary commands**. The distinction is visible in the [IBS treatment trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30473202/), [LSD experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/), and [ketamine feasibility study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29856288/). [citation marker unresolved in reader]

There are credible abuse risks without assuming unlimited control: misleading suggestions, memory distortion, nonconsensual experimentation, and concealed persuasion. Particularly relevant are [experiments with biased AI writing assistants](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978215/): participants’ stated attitudes shifted, while many did not recognize the bias or influence. The words were visible; the persuasive process was less apparent. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**The longer technical summary follows.** It integrates the optical research with the hypnosis, sleep-learning, and persuasion material in your attachments.

**1. “Invisible signals” require several separate definitions.** Optical wavelength determines which radiation reaches particular receptors; modulation frequency describes how rapidly the light changes. Neither alone establishes whether someone consciously perceives a pattern.

| Mechanism | What human research demonstrates | What that finding does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid, imperceptible flicker** | Apparently steady targets can produce distinguishable, frequency-specific neural responses. A [2024 brain–computer-interface study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38812288/) used targets at 58–62 Hz. | Understanding a hidden sentence, or reading thoughts without recording equipment. |
| **Nearly imperceptible spectral flicker** | Rapid alternation of spectral composition can evoke a 40 Hz visual response with less perceived flicker than conventional stimulation. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/). | A universal state of hypnosis, improved cognition, or clinical benefit merely because a 40 Hz response occurs. |
| **Invisible infrared carrier, visible output** | Experimental upconversion contact lenses transform near-infrared radiation into visible light, allowing perception of encoded patterns. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409271/). | Subconscious decoding: the device makes the signal perceptible. |
| **Similar appearance, different physiological stimulation** | Displays matched for apparent color and brightness can differ in their effects on melatonin and subjective sleepiness. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29788219/). | Conveying semantic information or determining someone’s beliefs. |

These findings come from particular apparatus, exposure conditions, and participants. “Imperceptible” should not be treated as a universal property independent of the observer and viewing conditions. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

The brain–computer-interface example is especially easy to overinterpret. The system records a response and classifies which **known target** someone attends to. That establishes a useful communication channel through instrumentation. It does not establish that the brain automatically translates arbitrary flicker into language.

**2. Physiological effects and comprehension follow different pathways.** Light can influence circadian physiology without carrying a meaningful message. A [placebo-controlled sleep study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37660233/) demonstrated circadian phase shifts from brief flashes delivered during sleep. However, melatonin suppression, body-clock shifts, alertness, and sleep quality are distinct outcomes. Another [controlled experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35998110/) found that melatonin suppression did not automatically translate into changes in sleepiness, vigilance, or sleep. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Near-infrared photobiomodulation belongs in another category again. A [controlled experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31259198/) reported changes in EEG activity following local transcranial irradiation. Its result concerns physiological modulation under specified exposure conditions; it does not demonstrate transmission of an encoded message.

**3. Sleep permits some processing, but its stage and the person’s awareness matter.** Closed eyelids do not completely block light. Researchers have recorded [stimulus-linked visual responses during sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/), with [different frequency responses from wakefulness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28334175/). A [2026 EEG–fMRI study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73997-y) found preserved light responses in the visual thalamus during N1 and N2 sleep, alongside marked suppression or inversion of responses in visual cortex. Preserved input therefore does not imply preserved waking-style interpretation. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Meaningful communication during sleep has nevertheless been demonstrated. In the [2021 multicenter REM study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/), six of 36 participants produced 29 correct answers during verified REM sleep. One participant interpreted a light-coded arithmetic question. Crucially, successful examples included **lucid dreamers consciously experiencing the incoming signal within a dream**. Most communication attempts did not yield a correct response. This establishes intermittent communication during sleep, not reliable subconscious instruction delivery. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Being asleep, having no later recollection, and never consciously experiencing something must therefore remain separate claims.

**4. Sleep learning is real but constrained.** Targeted memory reactivation generally presents cues associated with material learned beforehand. A [meta-analysis of 91 experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027149/) found a modest average benefit, particularly during non-REM sleep. That is evidence for selectively influencing memory consolidation. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

There is also evidence for limited new learning: [word-pair presentations during slow-wave sleep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30713104/) influenced later implicit choices under carefully timed conditions. Consequently, “nothing new can be learned during sleep” is too strong. The supported result is narrower than learning arbitrary instructions, acquiring an ideology, or reliably executing a later command. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**5. Hypnosis has clinical value, but response and durability vary.** Its strongest practical evidence concerns specific treatment outcomes. In the [IMAGINE IBS trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30473202/), adequate relief at three months occurred in approximately 41% receiving individual hypnotherapy, 33% receiving group hypnotherapy, and 17% receiving educational support. A [recent perioperative review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40991959/) also supported benefits for certain pain, anxiety, and nausea outcomes, while finding uncertainty elsewhere. These are meaningful treatment effects with substantial nonresponse and condition-specific limits. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Posthypnotic effects can outlast a session. A small [1991 durability experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/) found responses across repeated testing over eight weeks, with different rates of decline for different suggestions. That does not show continuous activation between tests, universal persistence, or control over unwilling behavior. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

Memory claims deserve particular scrutiny. In a [controlled hypnosis-and-memory experiment](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ch.146), expectations of memory enhancement increased confident errors among highly suggestible participants without improving accurate recall. A vivid recollection produced under suggestion therefore requires independent corroboration. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**6. Drug “amplification” depends on what researchers measure.** Imaginative suggestibility, hypnotizability scores, subjective automaticity, cooperation, and clinical improvement are different outcomes.

| Substance | Relevant finding | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **LSD** | A [10-person experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/) increased responsiveness to imaginative suggestions. | Does not establish improved treatment outcomes or compulsory obedience. |
| **Nitrous oxide** | A [30-person experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19057896/) increased imaginative suggestibility and imagery vividness. | The suggestion test did not require a conventional hypnotic induction. |
| **Ketamine** | An [11-person feasibility study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29856288/) reported increased hypnotizability particularly among initially low-scoring participants. | Too small to establish dependable clinical enhancement; responses differed across participants. |

These are experimental signals rather than established medication strategies for strengthening hypnotherapy. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

A combined treatment must be compared with both components separately: improvement after ketamine plus hypnosis could reflect ketamine’s own effects, hypnosis, expectations, or an interaction. Similarly, sedation or amnesia should not be relabeled as enhanced hypnosis. The evidence reviewed does not justify self-administering drugs to intensify suggestion.

**7. Abuse risks are credible, but their mechanisms should be stated precisely.**

- **Memory contamination and exploitation of trust:** leading suggestions can generate confidence in inaccurate recollections. This creates risks in therapy, interrogation, and interpersonal manipulation; confidence does not authenticate a memory. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
- **Nonconsensual exposure or experimentation:** a plausible misuse of sleep and lighting interventions is unwanted interference with sleep or circadian timing. That is a risk inferred from demonstrated physiological effects, not evidence that such interference necessarily conveys instructions. The [MKULTRA hearing record](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/hearings/95mkultra.pdf) separately documents historical unethical experimentation; it does not establish successful programmable control. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
- **Concealed persuasion through ordinary content:** biased AI suggestions shifted stated attitudes in [two preregistered experiments involving 2,582 participants](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978215/). A [randomized X-feed study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2) shifted some policy and current-event opinions without significantly changing partisanship or affective polarization. These support concerns about opaque political or commercial influence. [citation marker unresolved in reader]
- **Overclaiming effectiveness:** effects are not uniform. A large [Facebook experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06297-w) substantially changed exposure to like-minded sources without measurable changes on eight preregistered attitude outcomes. This limits claims that controlling exposure reliably controls beliefs. [citation marker unresolved in reader]

**8. The unresolved claim requires evidence across the entire causal sequence.** A convincing demonstration of unconscious optical instruction delivery would need to establish actual exposure, objective lack of awareness, discrimination of the encoded pattern, comprehension of novel meaning, and a subsequent behavioral effect—while excluding waking episodes, conscious dream perception, prior conditioning, and chance.

The existing studies demonstrate different portions of that sequence under different conditions. Combining their findings does not establish that the complete system works, that its effects add together, or that it has been deployed in a particular case.
