### 1. Can a sound cue steer what appears in a dream?


## Experiment project draft

**Project title**  
Can a Sound Cue Nudge What Appears in a Dream?

**Short summary**  
Can a sound linked to an experience influence whether that experience later appears in a dream? Participants will learn two simple tasks, each paired with a different sound. During monitored sleep, one sound will be played during a pre-approved sleep window; cue identity and timing will be concealed until the dream reports are collected. Independent reviewers, unaware of the cue condition, will score whether task details appear in the reports.

**Research question**  
Does replaying a sound associated with one task during sleep increase the appearance of that task’s details in later dream reports, compared with the uncued task and control periods?

**Hypothesis and alternatives**  
- **Hypothesis:** Dream reports will contain more details from the cued task than from the uncued task.
- **Alternatives:** Cueing will not change dream content; both tasks will appear at similar rates; or any apparent effect will be explained by report expectations, awakenings, or differences between the tasks.

**Study plan**  
Recruit consenting adults for an ethics-reviewed sleep study. Teach two benign tasks and pair each with a distinct sound. During monitored sleep, randomly present one task’s cue in the registered sleep window, with sham or uncued comparison periods. Collect reports before revealing conditions, then have masked reviewers score them using a frozen rubric. Record awakenings and sleep disruption.

**Primary outcome**  
Difference in blinded task-detail incorporation between cued and uncued conditions.

**Why this is worth studying**  
A recent laboratory study found more elements from a cued task than an uncued task in REM-sleep dream reports, while also noting that dream incorporation was high for both tasks. This project would test a bounded replication or extension; it would not establish broad control over dreams. [Konkoly et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2025.109229)

**Funding use**  
Sleep-lab or monitored-sleep access, participant compensation, cue-delivery equipment, and preparation of an open protocol and de-identified results.  
**Funding target:** Set after confirming site, equipment, and recruitment costs.

**Backer updates and results**  
Share the registered protocol, progress updates, de-identified scoring data, and a plain-language report including null or inconclusive results.

**Review status**  
Human-participant sleep study. Obtain the required independent ethics review before recruitment. Consent will disclose that benign sounds may be played during sleep; cue identity and timing may remain concealed until debriefing if approved.

### 2. Can sleep cues selectively affect what people remember the next day?


## Experiment project draft

**Project title**  
Can a Quiet Sound Cue Strengthen a Memory During Sleep?

**Short summary**  
People learn many details before bed. We will test whether softly replaying sounds linked to some of those details during monitored sleep changes next-day recall compared with uncued details. The sounds will be used as memory cues, not as messages. We will measure both memory and sleep disruption.

**Research question**  
Does replaying a learning-associated sound during a registered sleep stage improve next-day recall for the associated material?

**Hypothesis and alternatives**  
- **Hypothesis:** Recall will be better for items whose associated sound was replayed during the registered sleep window.
- **Alternatives:** Replaying cues will not change recall; effects will depend on how well an item was learned before sleep; or sound-related awakenings will explain the result.

**Study plan**  
Consenting adults learn a set of simple object-location pairs, each linked to a distinct sound. After a baseline memory test, a randomized subset of sounds is replayed quietly during monitored sleep. The remaining items serve as within-participant controls. Test recall the next morning without first revealing which sounds were played. Track sleep interruptions and report any device failures.

**Primary outcome**  
Within-participant difference in next-day recall accuracy for cued versus uncued items, accounting for baseline recall.

**Why this is worth studying**  
A controlled study with 20 participants reported improved recall for some object-location associations after associated sounds were replayed during slow-wave sleep; the effect depended on prior learning. This proposal would test a clearly bounded result and its limits. [Creery et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4670)

**Funding use**  
Monitored-sleep access, cue playback and logging equipment, participant compensation, and open materials and results.  
**Funding target:** Set after confirming actual study and recruitment costs.

**Backer updates and results**  
Share the task materials, registered analysis plan, de-identified results, and a plain-language summary of which items changed, if any.

**Review status**  
Human-participant sleep study. Obtain independent ethics review before recruitment. Participants will be told that some learning-associated sounds may be replayed during sleep, while item assignments remain concealed until testing is complete.

### 3. How low can a sleep cue be and still produce a measurable effect?


## Experiment project draft

**Project title**  
How Quiet Can a Sleep Cue Be Before Its Effect Disappears?

**Short summary**  
Some sleep studies replay learning-associated sounds quietly. This project will compare clearly audible and low-level cues to test whether cue intensity changes memory or dream-report outcomes—and whether any measured change occurs without detectable sleep disruption. Participants will consent to possible sounds during sleep; the study will conceal cue timing and condition assignments until debriefing if approved.

**Research question**  
Do low-level learning-associated sounds played during monitored sleep affect a registered memory or dream-report outcome, compared with clearly audible cues and sham periods?

**Hypothesis and alternatives**  
- **Hypothesis:** Low-level cues may produce a measurable outcome difference without a corresponding increase in awakenings.
- **Alternatives:** Low-level cues may have no measurable effect; any effect may occur only at more audible levels; or changes may track awakenings or cue detection rather than sleep-state processing.

**Study plan**  
Participants learn simple material linked to distinct sounds. During monitored sleep, randomly assigned cues are played at pre-approved, conservative sound levels; other items receive a sham or no-cue condition. The study records sleep-stage and arousal measures, tests memory after waking, and asks participants what they recall hearing. The analysis compares low-level and audible conditions while accounting for baseline learning and sleep disruption.

**Primary outcome**  
Difference in next-day recall between low-level-cued, audible-cued, and uncued items.

**Interpretation limit**  
If participants do not report hearing a cue, that alone does not establish that the cue was “invisible” or processed unconsciously. The result would be limited to the tested sounds, participants, sleep conditions, and measurement sensitivity.

**Why this is worth studying**  
Targeted memory reactivation studies provide a basis for testing whether learning-associated sounds replayed during sleep can affect later recall. This project would focus on cue intensity, awareness reports, and sleep disruption. [Creery et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4670)

**Funding use**  
Monitored-sleep access, calibrated audio and sleep measurement equipment, participant compensation, and open materials and results.  
**Funding target:** Set after confirming actual study and recruitment costs.

**Backer updates and results**  
Share the sound-level protocol, sleep-disruption measures, de-identified results, and a plain-language account of what the awareness checks can and cannot establish.

**Review status**  
Human-participant sleep study. Obtain independent ethics review before recruitment. Consent will disclose possible sleep sounds, their benign nature, withdrawal rights, and possible sleep disruption; specific timing and cue assignments may be concealed until debriefing if approved.
