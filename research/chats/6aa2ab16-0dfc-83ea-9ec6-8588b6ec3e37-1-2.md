The newer literature makes the general concern substantially more concrete than it was a few years ago. It still does not establish “mind control,” nor does it provide evidence that such techniques were covertly used on any particular person. But it now supports a narrower proposition that matters: information delivered during carefully selected sleep states can alter later memory, evaluation, dream content, and in some experiments implicit or explicit attitudes.

The most striking paper I found is from August 2025, “Updating social evaluation during sleep.” Participants first learned identities associated with faces. During slow-wave sleep, researchers played a person’s name paired with novel positive personality traits. After sleep, participants rated the cued person more positively, reconstructed their face as more attractive, remembered more positive and fewer negative traits, and showed a more favorable implicit-association score. Critically, the authors report that participants did not consciously remember the positive traits presented during sleep. The authors themselves explicitly raise the possibility of misuse for “political propaganda or commercial manipulation” and call for neuroethical safeguards. That is considerably closer to what you are describing than classic TMR.

The field has also moved conceptually. A 2026 Trends in Cognitive Sciences review now explicitly uses the term “memory editing during sleep.” It describes three mechanisms: reactivating an existing memory while extinction occurs, strengthening competing memories, and introducing interfering information during sleep-based reactivation. The authors identify closed-loop neuromodulation, wearable sensing and neural decoding as technologies that could increasingly target these processes. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

At the same time, there are important constraints. The effects are not arbitrary writing into the brain. TMR works best when the cue has some relationship to material already encoded, although the 2025 experiment is important precisely because it suggests genuinely new semantic information can sometimes become integrated during sleep. The 2024 TMR review describes effects on declarative, procedural and emotional memories, forgetting, REM processing and dream content, but repeatedly emphasizes context dependence and variable effect sizes. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

Dream manipulation is even less deterministic. A 2024 systematic review examined 51 studies of sensory influence on dreams. Reported stimulus-related dream changes ranged from essentially zero to roughly 80%, with extreme methodological heterogeneity. In other words, influencing dream content is real; reliably specifying what someone will dream or believe is not. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

Dormio/TDI establishes another component of your proposed architecture: state detection followed by timed semantic input. The system detects hypnagogia and injects auditory prompts while executive control is reduced but external auditory information can still enter ongoing mentation. Later studies found that incubated themes could affect subsequent creativity, and a 2026 pilot reports that targeted material introduced around sleep onset can subsequently appear in REM dreams. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

Closed-loop stimulation supplies a different component. Acoustic pulses synchronized to endogenous slow oscillations can modify slow-wave activity and spindle-related physiology. But the behavioral consequences are modest and inconsistent: a 2021 meta-analysis found an overall episodic-memory effect of only about Hedges' g = 0.25, with the overall result not conventionally significant; some phase-locked subgroups performed better. This distinction matters for your pink-noise hypothesis. Pink noise is better thought of as a physiological carrier/modulator than as a “hypnosis signal.” I found no good evidence that pink noise itself produces hypnotic suggestibility. Continuous open-loop pink noise can actually disturb normal architecture and impair some sleep-dependent cognitive processing. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat] Separately, hypnotic suggestion before sleep has altered subsequent slow-wave sleep in highly hypnotizable subjects, but that experiment used explicit hypnotic induction and suggestion, not pink noise. So I would reformulate your hypothesized mass-influence mechanism as something like:

\[
\Delta \mathbf A_i
=
\sum_t
G(S_{it})
\,R_{it}
\,E_{it}
\,C_{it}
\,\mathbf m_t
+\epsilon_i
\]

Here \(\mathbf A_i\) is person \(i\)'s latent associative/evaluative state; \(\mathbf m_t\) is the semantic/emotional direction of stimulus \(t\); \(G(S)\) is a sleep-state gate determining whether that stimulus arrives during a useful physiological state; \(R\) represents prior relevance/reactivation of related material; \(E\) represents emotional salience/arousal; and \(C\) represents effective processing/consolidation. Repeated exposures sum imperfectly because learning saturates, competes and sometimes interferes.

At population level:

\[
\Delta\bar{\mathbf A}
=
\frac1N\sum_i\Delta\mathbf A_i.
\]

That is a useful model of “subconscious propaganda,” but importantly it does not imply identical changes in everyone. Individual susceptibility, existing associations, sleep stage, stimulus perception, prior beliefs and subsequent waking experiences all introduce substantial variance.

The particularly plausible abuse architecture is therefore not:

> pink noise → hypnosis → arbitrary command → obedience.

It is closer to:

> daytime profiling/priming → existing emotionally meaningful associations → sleep-state inference → repeated low-salience semantic or associative cues → preferential reactivation/integration → small probabilistic changes in salience, familiarity, memory accessibility or evaluation → repetition across nights and reinforcement during waking life.

That is much more consistent with the evidence.

And this changes the significance of “mass” deployment. An influence operation does not require a large deterministic effect on each person. Advertising and recommendation systems already operate on tiny changes in probabilities. If sleep manipulation produced only a very small mean shift in preference, fear association, familiarity or recall, repeated exposure over millions of individuals could still have commercially or politically meaningful aggregate effects. The danger is therefore better modeled as statistical choice architecture than coercive control.

There are also several distinct abuse channels worth separating conceptually:

1. **Associative valence manipulation.** Pair a known person/topic with positive or negative semantic material. The 2025 social-evaluation experiment is direct proof-of-principle for the positive direction. A negative version was deliberately not tested partly because of ethical concerns. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

2. **Memory-selection manipulation.** Strengthen some pre-existing memories while allowing competing memories to decay or interfere. That could change what evidence feels most available when someone later forms a judgment. The 2024 TMR review explicitly discusses strengthening and forgetting. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

3. **Affective reconsolidation/interference.** Reactivating emotionally loaded memories while introducing new associations could alter subsequent emotional responses. This is exactly why the same mechanisms are being investigated therapeutically for aversive memories. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

4. **Dream-content steering.** Less useful for precise persuasion but potentially powerful for emotional salience: repeatedly making a person, threat, symbol or theme appear in hypnagogic or dream imagery. Current evidence supports influencing themes rather than constructing elaborate predetermined dreams. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

5. **Sleep disruption as influence.** This may actually be easier technologically. Poorly timed or deliberately disruptive stimulation can cause micro-arousals, fragmented sleep or changes in architecture. Cognitive impairment, emotional dysregulation and confusion arising from chronic sleep disruption would be nonspecific rather than programmable, but could increase vulnerability to ordinary waking persuasion. Pink-noise experiments themselves illustrate that manipulating sleep physiology can have unintended detrimental cognitive effects. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

There is one historical result that looks even more like political persuasion, but it illustrates why caution is essential. Hu et al. reported in Science in 2015 that replaying cues during slow-wave sleep strengthened prior counter-stereotype training against racial and gender biases. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat] A well-powered replication in 2019 failed to reproduce the result and explicitly reduced confidence in that effect. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat] The 2025 social-evaluation study is stronger evidence for the general possibility, but it is still one controlled laboratory paradigm and had no long-term follow-up or waking control. The authors explicitly acknowledge those limitations. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

The commercial concern is not hypothetical at the level of intent. Coors ran a 2021 campaign encouraging participants to watch branded material before sleep and then play an overnight soundtrack intended to influence dreams. That prompted dozens of sleep scientists to warn against commercial dream manipulation. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]

So I would update the conclusion we discussed previously:

**The scientifically defensible threat model is now “closed-loop, state-dependent covert associative influence,” not merely “dream advertising.”** The strongest foreseeable effects are changes in accessibility, emotional valence, familiarity, memory weighting, mental representation and perhaps implicit evaluation. Combining those effects with profiling and repeated exposure creates a plausible population-scale influence system even though no component currently supports reliable arbitrary belief implantation.

The important evidentiary boundary regarding what you previously experienced remains unchanged: these studies establish that mechanisms resembling parts of your description exist. They do **not** establish that those mechanisms were deployed against you, and phenomenological similarity alone cannot distinguish technologically induced experiences from ordinary sleep phenomena or other causes. But as a general technology-risk hypothesis, the proposition is substantially less speculative in 2026 than it would have been five or ten years ago. [citation marker unresolved in recovered chat]
