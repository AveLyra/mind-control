**The strongest qualification to an overly categorical dismissal is that some drugs can measurably change suggestibility and memory.** However, the leap from those findings to reliable covert control is unsupported by these studies. A laboratory task, a therapeutic outcome, and involuntary obedience are different outcomes requiring different evidence.

## Does this justify testing for additional substances?

**The historical list is not a sufficient basis for choosing a testing panel.** Additional testing might be appropriate after reviewing an actual episode, but these papers do not identify which substance—if any—would explain nonspecific fatigue or wooziness.

For a laboratory, the useful request is:

> “Please have a toxicologist review the symptoms, timing, current medications, and sample type, and recommend an appropriate screen and any targeted confirmation. Please specify which substances the method covers and what a negative result cannot exclude.”

That matters because even a “comprehensive” clinical screen can miss substances present below its detection thresholds, and a method intended for urine cannot automatically be assumed suitable for food. [University of Washington laboratory guidance](https://testguide.labmed.uw.edu/guideline/comp_drug_screen_detection_limits)
