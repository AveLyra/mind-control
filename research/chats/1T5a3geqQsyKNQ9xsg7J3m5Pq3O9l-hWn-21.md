Then the campaign should fund **outside sleep-lab access and EEG monitoring**. A timed speaker at home could play sounds after lights-out, but without sleep-stage data you couldn’t reliably claim the cues were delivered during sleep.

A public UMass sleep-lab rate sheet lists external rates of $43.75–$122.50 per PSG device use, $3.50 per room-hour, and $35–$260 per hour for technical support. Those are **benchmark rates from one facility, not a quote or a complete study cost**; contact the lab you’d actually use for an estimate. [UMass rates](https://www.umass.edu/ials/core-facilities/sleep-monitoring-lab/sleep-monitoring-lab-rates-services)

Funds will support a small, ethics-reviewed feasibility study at an external sleep research facility. The main costs are monitored overnight sessions with EEG sleep-stage recording, technical support and scoring, participant compensation, and a cue-playback and timestamp-logging setup. We will use the pilot to assess whether the cue procedure can be delivered during the intended sleep stage, whether it disrupts sleep, and whether blinded reviewers can score the resulting dream reports. The pilot is designed to assess feasibility and safety; it is not powered to establish a reliable effect on dream content. The funding target will be set from a written facility quote and the approved study budget, including Experiment’s platform and payment-processing fees.
