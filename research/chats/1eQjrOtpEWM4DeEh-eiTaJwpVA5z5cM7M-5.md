Yes—**“Inception” is the audacious version**: can a suggestion delivered during sleep shape a dream, then cause someone to adopt an idea or make a later choice *as if it were their own*?

Researchers have influenced **dream themes** with prompts during sleep onset. In one controlled study, participants were repeatedly prompted to think of a tree and reported more tree-related dream content; the study also examined immediate creative performance. That is a long way from reliably implanting a belief. A separate study reported immediate changes in social evaluations after sleep cues, but did not test whether they lasted. ([Dream-incubation study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31361-w), [social-evaluation study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-025-00356-9))

The sharp research question is: **Does a sleep-induced idea cause a later, unprompted choice about something new?** To answer it, a consented study would have to distinguish the effect of the dream from the sound itself, brief waking, expectation, and biased dream recall—and check whether any change persists. Right now, *Inception*-style belief implantation remains unproven.
