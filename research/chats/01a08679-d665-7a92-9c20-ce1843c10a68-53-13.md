**Cheat sheet: waking-life cue effects**

A learned cue can nudge attention, bodily arousal, or a familiar action. That is measurable conditioning, but it is **not evidence of reliable “mind control”** or that any particular cue was used on someone.

| Factor | What the evidence suggests |
|---|---|
| **Acute stress** | Can shift behavior toward habits in a laboratory task. Propranolol prevented that shift in one experiment, which points to a role for noradrenaline, not a general-purpose way to switch susceptibility on or off. [Human experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22114298/) |
| **Sleep loss** | May impair deliberate control, but it does **not consistently strengthen cue-driven behavior**. A randomized food-cue study found *less* cue-driven responding after total sleep deprivation, contrary to its hypothesis. [Sleep experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60223-2) |
| **Sedatives, including benzodiazepines** | Can affect alertness and memory; that does not imply stronger conditioning. In one small threat-learning study, lorazepam reduced immediate fear ratings, with no detected difference in skin-conductance responses or two-day persistence. [Human study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32259695/) |
| **Antidepressants** | Effects depend on the drug and stage of learning. Two weeks of escitalopram *facilitated extinction* of a learned fear response in a small trial, rather than making a cue universally more powerful. [Randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23776033/) |
| **Caffeine or other stimulants** | Can improve vigilance during sleep loss; that is not proof they block conditioned cues. [Randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35321359/) |

**Bottom line:** stress is the clearest candidate here for increasing reliance on an *existing habit* during waking life. “More habitual,” “more easily conditioned,” and “more likely to obey a cue” are different claims. The strongest objection to a broad drug-based theory is that effects change with the task, cue, and whether researchers measure learning, expression, or extinction. Medication changes would also confound a self-test, so this is something to study with consent and objective measures, not by altering treatment.
