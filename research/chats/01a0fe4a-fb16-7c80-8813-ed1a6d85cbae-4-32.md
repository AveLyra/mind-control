Yes. The decisive change is to make **hypnotic involuntariness an actual struggle over action**, not merely a feeling Daniel reports afterward.

Cold-control theory explains how an intended action might be experienced as unintended; dissociated-control theory proposes that suggestions can activate responses with reduced executive oversight. They are explanations of observed hypnosis, not proven limits on what a fictional discovery could do. Your extrapolation can go further: **a suggestion persists into wakefulness, competes in the formation of goals, and sometimes defeats a conscious attempt to veto it while the person remains alert and capable of reasoning**. That last sentence is the invented breakthrough. [Cold-control theory](https://academic.oup.com/book/53962/chapter-abstract/422199634) · [Dissociated-control account](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9470232/)

That makes Daniel more disturbing than an obedient sleeper. He recognizes the three notes. He knows why he wants to leave. He decides to stay—and finds himself constructing a sensible reason to go anyway. At the café he identifies the questioning pattern and withholds one answer, yet feels his next answer gathering before he has chosen to give it. Sometimes he stops himself. Sometimes he can only stop halfway through a sentence. His intelligence remains available to both sides of the struggle.

### The involuntariness on the page


The three notes sounded behind Daniel.

*Stay seated,* he told himself. He put both hands flat on the desk. Nothing pulled at his arms. No voice spoke in his head. He simply became certain that he ought to check the street before the afternoon briefing.

He could name the thought as an intrusion. He could explain, with perfect clarity, why leaving was a mistake. While he was still explaining it, he saved his work and stood.

At the café, the woman mentioned a name. Daniel said nothing. She mentioned another, and he watched himself decide that correcting her would be harmless. The first word was out before the decision felt complete.

He stopped. She waited.

What frightened him was the interval that followed. He still wanted to tell her.

For the wider political-economy scenario, the operatives would reserve this **fictional, powerful form of hypnosis** for a small number of consequential people. Population influence would remain weaker and statistical. The group’s aim would be to alter enough judgments about credibility, risk, and urgency that pivotal institutional choices favor its preferred systems. Those choices then create real investments and dependencies that persist without continued hypnosis. A 61-million-user experiment shows that a digital intervention can produce a small aggregate change in voting participation; it does not establish precise political control. [Mobilization experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11421)

The strongest scientific objection should remain visible in the fiction: **no cited experiment shows an alert person accurately identifying an unwanted suggestion and repeatedly failing to veto complex, context-sensitive actions because of hypnosis**. If your fictional researchers demonstrate that result under controlled conditions, they have discovered something genuinely beyond today’s theories. Daniel’s awareness and resistance then become evidence of the new phenomenon—and the source of the story’s tension.
