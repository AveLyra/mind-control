The extreme, research-grounded scenario is **probabilistic control of decision environments**. A small group uses ordinary institutional access to determine what people encounter and what choices reach them. Hypnosis and sleep influence add uncertain, person-specific effects on memory, familiarity, and emotion. The group does not need everyone to obey: it needs enough consequential decisions to fall its way that later decisions inherit the resulting economy.

### How the abuse would scale

Assume the operatives secretly control parts of three systems: a widely used information service, a sleep or mental-health service, and the forecasts or briefings used by major institutions. That access is the largest fictional assumption. A small group could set objectives for automated systems, but could not personally manage a global campaign.

The proposed causal chain is:

**Information encountered while awake → what feels memorable or credible → marginal changes in participation and institutional judgment → laws, investments, and standards → new constraints on future choices.**

The sleep service would have a specific, limited role. Sleep-onset prompts can influence **dream themes**; cues during later sleep have sometimes strengthened **previously learned memories** or simple associations. A hypnosis study found changes in a specific attention task among highly responsive participants; a recall study found increased errors rather than improved access to truth. These findings give the fictional system possible ways to affect *salience and confidence*. They do not give it a language for issuing reliable commands to sleepers. [Dream experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37188795/) · [Memory-cue experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17347444/) · [Hypnosis studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15994228/) · [Recall study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1402714/)

At population scale, a small effect can still matter near a threshold. A randomized Facebook experiment involving 61 million users estimated roughly **340,000 additional votes**, mainly through social influence among close friends. That was a voting encouragement intervention, not control over *which* candidate people chose. It establishes that a platform can measurably affect aggregate behavior in a particular setting. [Bond and colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11421)

The operatives’ ambition would be to turn occasional marginal effects into durable structural advantage. In the worst case, a close decision favors their preferred infrastructure standard, financing arrangement, or emergency authority. That decision directs real investment; firms and governments adapt; the new system becomes cheaper and more familiar than its rivals. Economic models show how early advantages **can** become locked in when adoption produces increasing returns. The model does not show that an intelligence group could reliably create those advantages worldwide. [Arthur’s original model](https://academic.oup.com/ej/article-abstract/99/394/116/5188212)

### Where the scenario crosses into science fiction

| Required capability | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Change a specific learned association or task response under laboratory conditions | **Demonstrated in some settings**, with variable results. |
| Reliably choose which memory or emotional judgment changes in a given person | **Unestablished.** A 2026 sleep-cue study found no overall selective memory benefit in its design. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41656173/) |
| Produce durable political attitudes through sleep cues | **Weak evidence.** A reported sleep effect on social evaluation is intriguing; a related social-bias result failed direct replication. [Social-evaluation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40885761/) · [Replication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30682167/) |
| Produce large, predictable political effects through digital curation | **Not established.** Changing feeds substantially altered exposure in a 2020 election experiment without significantly changing key attitudes over three months. Removing political ads in another large experiment produced no detectable effect on turnout or several other outcomes. [Feed experiment](https://www.networkscienceinstitute.org/publications/how-do-social-media-feed-algorithms-affect-attitudes-and-behavior-in-an-election-campaign) · [Advertising experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41772056/) |
| Sustain covert influence across rival states, markets, and institutions for decades | **Fictional premise.** It requires extraordinary access, prediction, secrecy, and coordination; none follows from the psychology studies. |

A more invasive version could give **a few** influential people implanted neural hardware. Researchers have demonstrated acute stimulation with an engineered cortical receiver and limited inner-speech decoding from four people with implanted arrays. Reading a constrained signal and producing a chosen belief are separate capabilities. These studies provide no route to decoding or controlling an uninstrumented population at a distance. [Stimulation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11014439/) · [Inner-speech study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816265/)

So the most severe outcome for your fictional world is not uniform obedience. It is a **long series of biased choices that creates genuine dependence on the group’s preferred systems**. By the time anyone investigates, much of the trajectory is maintained by contracts, installed technology, concentrated capital, and institutions defending their own prior decisions. The psychological influence started some of the movement; ordinary economic forces then carry it forward.

The strongest objection is substantial: controlled studies often find small, inconsistent, or null political effects, and the sleep findings are much narrower still. Your workspace’s [population influence agenda] (local source path omitted) correctly labels global drift as hypotheses; its [sleep dry run] (local source path omitted) measured scheduling feasibility with synthetic outcomes, not human influence. For the extreme scenario to work, the fiction must grant the operatives a breakthrough in **predicting consequential effects across people and institutions**, alongside extraordinary access to the systems making those decisions.
