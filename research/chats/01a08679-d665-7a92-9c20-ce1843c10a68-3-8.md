Yes. The wider risk category is **attentional and state disruption by conditioned cues**, not only sleep manipulation.

A fuller quick message:

We were also concerned with wake-state influence: subliminal or near-subliminal cues, notification mimicry, vibration mimicry, alert sounds, sirens, and other timed interruptions designed to break concentration or alter someone’s emotional/cognitive state. Even ordinary phone notifications can impair attention and task performance without the person interacting with the phone; one study found the disruption from alerts alone was comparable to actively using the phone. Task-irrelevant sounds, especially unexpected or changing sounds, can involuntarily capture attention, disrupt working memory, and force task-set recovery. Sources: [Stothart et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121498/), [Parmentier, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24363092/), [Dalton & Lavie, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14769076/).

The abuse model is not “perfect mind control.” It is repeated, targeted **cognitive interruption**: imitating phone vibrations or message sounds to trigger checking behavior; using barely noticed audio cues to prime associations; timing alerts during deep work, prayer, writing, meditation, decision-making, sleep onset, or emotionally vulnerable moments; using sirens, alarm-like sounds, or staged urgency cues to induce orienting, stress, hypervigilance, or loss of train of thought. Environmental noise research also links chronic noise exposure with stress and cognitive effects, especially when sounds are salient, unpredictable, or uncontrollable. Sources: [noise and cognitive workload review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36537445/), [environmental noise and cognition meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34649047/).

Subliminal cueing is more limited and scientifically messy, especially in audio, but subliminal priming can influence perception, affect, and response tendencies under controlled conditions. The concern is greatest when subliminal or ambiguous cues are combined with conditioning, repetition, personal relevance, and timing. Sources: [Subliminal priming review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29849006/), [auditory subliminal stimulation review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1594413/).

For the vindication-machine framing, I’d say the safety question is: can external cues be used to detect and perturb cognitive states, sleep states, or emotionally important thought states without meaningful consent? Abuse would include covert persuasion, harassment, sleep disruption, concentration sabotage, induced hypervigilance, or false emergency cues. Actually dispatching or manipulating real emergency sirens/ambulances to disrupt someone would be dangerous and potentially criminal, so that should be treated as a red-line abuse scenario, not a test condition.
