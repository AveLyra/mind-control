**Hypnosis can produce meaningful changes in pain, perception, distress, and some physical symptoms. Several drugs have increased responsiveness to suggestion in experiments. But the evidence has not established a reliable medication that broadly strengthens hypnotherapy—or makes people predictably obey arbitrary commands.**

I reviewed systematic reviews, controlled experiments, and newer publications through September 2026. This is a broad evidence map, rather than an exhaustive catalogue of every study.

“Clinically proven” needs qualification: a 2024 overview found **49 meta-analyses covering 261 distinct trials**, but only nine reviews had high methodological quality. Benefits were most consistently supported around pain and medical procedures. A positive study does not establish that every hypnosis technique, practitioner, or commercial recording works. [source 0](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1330238/full)

The major clinical applications break down as follows:

| Application | What the evidence supports | Important limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical procedures: pain, anxiety, nausea** | Among the best-supported applications. A review published online in 2025, in a 2026 journal issue, identified 142 studies involving 9,238 patients. Its randomized-trial analyses supported reductions in certain pain, anxiety, and postoperative nausea outcomes. | Average pain reductions in the relevant analyses were approximately **0.9–1.1 cm on a visual analogue scale**. Effects depended on when hypnosis was delivered; many other outcomes remained uncertain. [source 1](https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anae.70013) |
| **Chronic pain** | Can improve pain and its interference with daily life for some patients. | Superiority over other credible treatments is inconsistent. In a 328-person trial, hypnosis, mindfulness, and education all improved outcomes initially, without a significant immediate between-group difference in average pain; later differences favored hypnosis and mindfulness. [source 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35082248/), [source 2](https://www.iasp-pain.org/publications/pain-research-forum/papers-of-the-week/paper/188582-effects-hypnosis-vs-mindfulness-meditation-vs-education-chronic-pain-intensity-and/) |
| **Irritable bowel syndrome** | Gut-directed hypnotherapy has substantial clinical support. In the 354-person IMAGINE trial, adequate relief at three months occurred in approximately **41% with individual hypnosis, 33% with group hypnosis, and 17% with educational support**. | Many participants did not achieve adequate relief. This establishes a treatment option, not a universal cure. [source 3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30473202/) |
| **Menopausal hot flashes** | Supported by randomized trials. A 2025 trial of 250 women found self-administered hypnosis reduced hot-flash scores by **53.4%**, versus **40.9%** with a sham intervention at six weeks. | The additional benefit over the comparison group matters: attributing the entire 53% improvement specifically to hypnosis would exaggerate its effect. [source 4](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2841242) |
| **Cancer-related symptoms** | Evidence supports helping with some pain, anxiety, and nausea associated with cancer or treatment. | These are symptom-management findings; they do not demonstrate treatment of the cancer itself. [source 5](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1330238/full) |
| **Insomnia and sleep disturbance** | Promising, with positive and mixed findings across studies. | The 2023 systematic review identified substantial variation in methods and results. It does not establish dependable effectiveness for everyone with insomnia. [source 6](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207144.2023.2226177) |
| **Depression and PTSD** | Some encouraging trials and possible adjunctive benefits. | A 2024 depression review found insufficient evidence for clinical recommendation. A 2026 PTSD review reported promising findings but substantial heterogeneity, including uncontrolled studies. [source 7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388785/), [source 7](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.encep.2025.12.002) |
| **Childbirth** | May reduce overall use of pain medication. | Cochrane found no clear reduction in epidural use or improvement in normal vaginal delivery; evidence was low quality. [source 8](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009356_hypnosis-pain-management-during-labour-and-childbirth) |
| **Smoking cessation** | Some people quit following hypnotherapy. | Cochrane found insufficient evidence that hypnosis outperforms other behavioral support or unassisted quitting. Successful testimonials cannot establish the treatment’s added benefit. [source 9](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001008_does-hypnotherapy-help-people-who-are-trying-stop-smoking) |
| **Weight loss** | Some favorable results, including hypnosis added to behavioral treatment. | The evidence is too limited and methodologically variable to promise substantial, durable weight loss from hypnosis alone. [source 10](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1330238/full) |

**Its demonstrated psychological effects can be more striking than these average treatment benefits suggest.** Selected highly responsive participants can experience substantial changes in perception. A PET study found that suggestions about seeing color altered activity in color-processing regions. Another experiment changed pain unpleasantness and corresponding anterior-cingulate activity. These findings support changes in experience and processing, rather than an explanation based entirely on people pretending. Neither establishes unlimited control over perception or behavior. [source 11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10910791/), [source 11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252330/)

Hypnosis can also affect memory access. In an experiment involving autobiographical episodes, posthypnotic amnesia temporarily interfered with explicit recall while some implicit effects remained. **That is evidence of disrupted retrieval, not precise deletion of a memory.** [source 12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12009043/)

Memory enhancement is particularly problematic. Experiments have found that expectations about hypnosis improving memory can increase **confident errors**, some persisting afterward. A vivid, emotionally convincing recollection under hypnosis therefore needs independent corroboration; the experience does not authenticate the event. [source 13](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ch.146)

**Drugs can amplify certain responses, but researchers measure several different things:** experiencing an imagined sensation, passing a hypnotizability test, reporting that an action felt automatic, complying with a request, or improving clinically. An increase in one does not establish an increase in the others.

| Substance | Relevant human findings | What can reasonably be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| **LSD** | A placebo-controlled study of **10 volunteers** found increased responsiveness on the Creative Imagination Scale, but not a separate cued-imagery measure. | A small experiment supports increased imaginative suggestibility. It did not establish improved hypnotherapy outcomes, lasting hypnotizability changes, or compulsory obedience. [source 14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/) |
| **Nitrous oxide** | A crossover experiment involving **30 participants** found increased imaginative suggestibility and imagery vividness. The suggestion test omitted the usual hypnotic induction. | Suggestibility can increase during drug exposure without demonstrating a stronger, distinct “hypnotic state.” Clinical added benefit remains a separate question. [source 15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19057896/), [source 15](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23572806_Enhancement_of_suggestibility_and_imaginative_ability_with_nitrous_oxide) |
| **Ketamine** | An **11-person** placebo-controlled feasibility study reported a signal for increased hypnotizability, especially among initially low-scoring participants. | Preliminary evidence warrants research; it is far too small to establish a dependable clinical enhancement strategy. [source 16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6181123/), [source 16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29856288/) |
| **Oxytocin** | One study involved 40 initially low-hypnotizable men. Another, involving **28 highly hypnotizable men**, reported greater compliance with mildly socially unusual requests. A separate experiment found that oxytocin **weakened** one posthypnotic attention effect. | Effects are task- and context-dependent. Oxytocin is not a general-purpose “trust” or obedience switch. [source 17](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306453011001636), [source 17](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0060711), [source 17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4090953/) |
| **Alcohol** | A placebo comparison reported increased subjective hypnotic responsiveness, assessed through feelings of automaticity rather than conventional objective pass/fail scoring. | Evidence of a particular subjective effect; no demonstrated therapeutic advantage from adding alcohol. [source 18](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810013000937) |
| **Cannabis** | A 1978 study of **35 participants** found increased waking suggestibility during intoxication; the effect was absent when tested sober one week later. | An old, small finding about temporary responsiveness—not evidence of lasting enhancement or established clinical usefulness. [source 19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/417375/) |
| **Methylphenidate** | A naturalistic study reported higher hypnotizability scores during treatment in adults with ADHD. | Without randomized placebo control, medication effects cannot be cleanly separated from repeated testing, expectations, or improved ability to engage with the task. It cannot be generalized to everyone. [source 20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25978083/) |
| **Benzodiazepines and scopolamine** | Diazepam has been examined in older hypnotizability research. Controlled lorazepam/scopolamine research clearly demonstrates impaired memory and other cognitive functions. | Sedation, amnesia, and reduced resistance must not be interpreted as proof of enhanced therapeutic hypnosis or reliable truth-telling. [source 21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/856373/), [source 21](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/lorazepam-and-scopolamine-a-single-dose-comparison-of-effects-on--4/) |

The newer ketamine literature has not resolved the clinical question. A **June 2026 preprint** describes an uncontrolled 20-person feasibility sample for ketamine-augmented hypnotherapy. The authors explicitly acknowledge that it cannot separate ketamine’s contribution from hypnotherapy or other treatment effects. It also reports some negative experiences. This is preliminary, unreviewed evidence—not confirmation of a superior combined treatment. [source 22](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202606.2224)

The central problem is causal: if someone feels less pain after ketamine plus hypnosis, ketamine already has analgesic effects. Demonstrating *amplification of hypnosis* requires suitable comparison groups, including the drug and hypnosis separately. Likewise, intoxication can make treatment assignment obvious, weakening placebo blinding.

**The limits on behavioral control deserve a precise answer.** It would be too reassuring to claim that hypnosis makes exploitation impossible. It would also be unjustified to infer that heightened suggestibility lets someone reliably override another person’s values.

The oxytocin persuasion experiment illustrates the uncertainty. Its authors acknowledged that the requested behaviors might have seemed acceptable within the experiment, that they tested only men, and that there was no nonhypnotic comparison establishing a hypnosis-specific effect. Greater cooperation in that setting cannot demonstrate dependable control over genuinely unwanted or dangerous actions. [source 23](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0060711)

My assessment of the evidence is that **altered experience, memory errors, and context-sensitive influence are established possibilities; arbitrary, durable control over another person is not an established capability.** Drug-induced confusion or amnesia may create vulnerability without producing predictable, programmable behavior.

For improving legitimate treatment, the evidence is more useful around a specific therapeutic goal, structured practice, and suitable suggestions than around choosing a drug. Training experiments have increased both behavioral and subjective responsiveness, although that does not mean everyone can become highly hypnotizable. [source 24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7875922/)

There is also experimental work on brain stimulation: a preregistered randomized trial involving **80 fibromyalgia patients** temporarily increased hypnotizability with targeted magnetic stimulation. The between-group difference was no longer significant approximately one hour later. This demonstrates some modifiability, while leaving clinical benefit and durability unresolved. [source 25](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00184-z)

For personal treatment, I would choose a licensed clinician trained in hypnosis and experienced with the particular condition. **The available evidence does not justify self-administering drugs to intensify hypnosis.** Any proposed medication-assisted approach should be evaluated by a prescriber for its actual clinical benefit, risks, and alternatives—not simply whether it makes suggestions feel more powerful.
