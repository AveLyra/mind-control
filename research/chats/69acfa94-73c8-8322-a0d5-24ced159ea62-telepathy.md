Short answer: **No experiment has produced accepted scientific evidence that telepathy exists.**
However, there **have been legitimate scientific attempts to test it**, and a few experiments have reported statistical anomalies that proponents interpret as possible evidence. These findings remain **controversial and unconfirmed**.

Below is a structured review of the **main research programs and what the scientific consensus says**.

---

# 1. The Main Experimental Program: Ganzfeld Telepathy Experiments
The most serious and sustained attempt to test telepathy scientifically is the **Ganzfeld experiment**, developed in the 1970s.

### Basic setup
- A **“sender”** looks at an image or video in another room.
- A **“receiver”** sits in sensory deprivation (red light, white noise).
- The receiver describes mental impressions.
- Later they choose which of four targets was being sent.

If telepathy exists, the receiver should select the correct target **more often than the 25% expected by chance**. [original reference 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzfeld_experiment)

---

# 2. Some Meta-Analyses Found Small Statistical Effects
Some analyses of many experiments reported **slightly higher-than-chance success rates**.

Example findings:

- A meta-analysis of **29 studies (1997–2008)** reported a **32.2% hit rate**, statistically higher than the expected 25%. [original reference 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzfeld_experiment)
- Earlier automated experiments showed similar rates around **32% success** across hundreds of trials. [original reference 3](https://www.sheldrake.org/research/telepathy/testing-for-telepathy-in-connection-with-e-mails)
- Recent reviews still examine “anomalous perception” under Ganzfeld conditions using decades of data. [original reference 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11134153/)

These results are the **strongest statistical claims for telepathy in the literature**.

However, they remain disputed.

---

# 3. Major Problems Identified by Critics
When other scientists examined the same research, several issues appeared.

### 1. Lack of reliable replication
Independent laboratories often fail to reproduce the effect.

A meta-analysis of **30 experiments found no statistically significant telepathy effect**. [original reference 5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10414223/)

---

### 2. Experimental flaws
Investigations found multiple methodological weaknesses:

- imperfect randomization
- possible **sensory leakage** (subtle cues)
- inadequate documentation of procedures [original reference 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzfeld_experiment)

Some experiments even allowed **acoustic or visual hints** from the sender room.

---

### 3. Statistical artifacts
Later statistical reviews argued that the apparent effects could arise from:

- **selective publication**
- questionable research practices
- small sample sizes

A 2016 analysis showed how such practices could **create the illusion of significant results in random data**. [original reference 7](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0153049)

---

# 4. Other Experimental Claims
A few other lines of research have tried to detect telepathy-like phenomena:

### Dream telepathy
1960s–70s studies at the Maimonides Dream Lab attempted to send images to sleeping subjects.

Result: intriguing early reports but **failed replication later**.

---

### Phone-call or SMS telepathy
Rupert Sheldrake conducted tests where subjects guessed who was calling them.

Some experiments reported **above-chance guessing**, but critics argue they lack rigorous controls and independent replication. [original reference 8](https://journals.lub.lu.se/jaex/article/view/25934)

---

### Government-funded PSI research
The U.S. government funded psychic research during the Cold War (e.g., **Stargate Project**).

After decades of work, official reviews concluded the results were **not reliable enough for intelligence use**.

---

# 5. Current Scientific Consensus
Across psychology, neuroscience, and physics, the consensus is:

- **No reproducible evidence exists that telepathy occurs.**
- All claimed effects disappear with **tight experimental controls**.
- No plausible biological or physical mechanism has been demonstrated.

Many reviews conclude ESP **“has not been successfully demonstrated in experiments for over 150 years.”** [original reference 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzfeld_experiment)

---

# 6. Why the Question Still Persists
Three factors keep the topic alive:

1. **Statistical anomalies** occasionally appear in meta-analyses.
2. Human perception and coincidence are prone to **confirmation bias**.
3. The concept resonates with ideas from **quantum nonlocality**, though there is **no scientific evidence linking quantum effects to telepathy**.

---

# 7. Bottom Line
- Telepathy **has been seriously tested scientifically**, especially through Ganzfeld experiments.
- Some studies reported **weak statistical deviations from chance**.
- However, these findings **do not replicate reliably** and are widely attributed to methodological or statistical artifacts.

**Scientific consensus:**
> There is currently **no credible empirical evidence that telepathy exists.**
