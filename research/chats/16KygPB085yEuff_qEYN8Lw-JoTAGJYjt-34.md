**That paper supports an effect on memory-related brain activity. It does not demonstrate suppression of someone’s will or an ability to control their actions.** If my earlier wording suggested that scopolamine has been shown to “remove free will,” that was an overstatement.

### What the study actually found

The researchers compared **15 participants receiving scopolamine with 16 receiving no drug** while they performed picture-memory tasks.

“Persistent activity” means brain activity sustained during a brief delay between pictures. It does **not** refer to determination, resistance, or persistence of someone’s intentions.

Scopolamine reduced activity associated with later remembering. Participants responded more slowly and made somewhat more errors, but still performed the immediate tasks at approximately **94–95% accuracy**. Overall later recognition did **not** differ significantly between groups. The comparison used a previously studied no-drug group, rather than a randomized placebo-controlled design. [Full original paper](https://www.bu.edu/hasselmo/SchonJNeurosci2005.pdf)

### What that implies for hypnosis or “mind control”

There are several distinct claims:

| Claim | Evidence here |
|---|---|
| **Can affect memory formation and cognitive processing** | Supported by the study’s findings, with the limitations above. |
| **Can compromise clear thinking** | Scopolamine’s prescribing information documents confusion, disorientation, and drowsiness. [Official label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=b877a694-a1d0-4280-937a-a06820b12a88) |
| **Increases hypnotic responsiveness** | This study did not test hypnosis or suggestibility. |
| **Makes people reliably obey commands against their wishes** | This study provides no evidence for that claim. |
| **Produces lasting conditioned obedience** | Neither conditioning nor lasting behavioral control was tested. |

**The strongest reasonable concern is that cognitive impairment can undermine someone’s ability to understand a situation and make informed decisions.** That is a clinical inference about impaired capacity; it does not establish predictable compliance with another person’s instructions. The label also reports agitation and hallucinations, illustrating that impairment can take very different forms.

The suggestibility study I mentioned earlier concerned **LSD**, involved only ten volunteers, and measured responses to standardized suggestions. It cannot be transferred to scopolamine or treated as evidence of compelled obedience. [LSD study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/)

So the precise correction is: **scopolamine can impair cognitive capacities involved in exercising autonomy; “it disables the will” goes beyond what these sources establish.**
