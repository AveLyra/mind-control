Yes. My previous answer understated the historical record. There is unusually explicit documentation of attempts to take these techniques beyond laboratory psychology into intelligence and military field settings.

The strongest examples are:

- In 1951, CIA’s BLUEBIRD plan explicitly asked whether people could be “conditioned,” whether their future physical/mental activities could be controlled, and investigated **post-hypnotic control, amnesia, hypnosis combined with drugs, and covert drug delivery in food, cigarettes, candy, or beer**. [source 0](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/00146203)
- A 1953 ARTICHOKE directive called for **field teams** to test drugs, hypnosis, and other techniques on “indigenous personnel under field conditions,” including extracting information from unwilling people and developing means to control their “activities and mental capacities.” It also assigned responsibility for the **operational use** of ARTICHOKE techniques. [source 1](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/00190673)
- A later CIA internal reconstruction says actual drug-assisted interrogations had occurred as early as **1951**, and ARTICHOKE teams traveled to **Europe and East Asia** apparently to interrogate foreign agents, although surviving records do not reveal the results. [source 2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/PROJECT%20ARTICHOKE%5B12888369%5D.pdf)
- The Army went substantially further with drugs in actual field interrogations. In **Project THIRD CHANCE (Europe, 1961)** and **Project DERBY HAT (Far East, 1962)**, Army teams secretly administered LSD to nonvolunteer subjects—including suspected intelligence sources—and interrogated them to evaluate LSD as a field interrogation aid. The contemporary retrospective says the interrogations produced only moderate success. [source 3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/PROJECT%20MKULTRA%2C%20THE%20CIAS%5B12885086%5D.pdf)
- Separately, the Army tested psychochemical incapacitating agents on a large scale. DoD now acknowledges roughly **7,000 Edgewood Arsenal volunteers** exposed/tested with nerve agents, psychochemicals, irritants and other substances from 1955–75. [source 4](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2015/05/08/Edgewood-Arsenal-Chemical-Agent-Exposure-Studies) Field exercises tested whether LSD would disrupt functioning of military groups, and **Project DORK (1964)** dispersed BZ in the open to study effective dosage and BZ-induced delirium. [source 5](https://www.health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2015/05/08/Field-Testing-of-Hallucinogenic-Agents)
- CIA documents even discuss proposed hypnotized couriers, concealed hypnosis, drugs combined with hypnosis, amnesia and eventual field testing. Importantly, the same document admits major uncertainties and says some proposed effects had **not yet been demonstrated under actual field conditions**. [source 6](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0000140391.pdf)

So there are really three evidentiary levels:

**Documented intent:** extremely strong. Agencies explicitly wanted techniques for conditioning, amnesia, interrogation, covert drugging and behavioral control.

**Documented human/field experimentation:** also strong. Unwitting CIA drug experiments, Army field interrogations using LSD, ARTICHOKE field teams, and military BZ/LSD field testing are established historical facts. CIA later acknowledged behavioral-modification testing involving drugs and hypnosis on unwitting subjects. [source 7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp80m00165a000700140054-4)

**Documented success at durable covert behavioral “programming”:** much weaker. The surviving record does not demonstrate a reliable system whereby hypnosis + conditioning + drugs could secretly program an adult to execute complex behaviors over long periods. In fact, several internal records reveal uncertainty and disappointing operational results.

That last distinction matters, but it also means the historical baseline is substantially stronger than “they only studied this in laboratories.” They explicitly contemplated **field deployment**, created teams for it, and in some cases actually carried out covert field interventions.
