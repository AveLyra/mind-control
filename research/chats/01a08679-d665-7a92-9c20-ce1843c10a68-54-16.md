Yes, they belong on the cheat sheet, but **“was investigated” is not the same as “worked as mind control.”** The [1977 Senate MKULTRA hearing](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf) documents abusive, sometimes nonconsensual drug testing, especially with LSD. It does not establish a reliable way to program someone to respond to a later cue.

| Drug or class | What matters for your question |
|---|---|
| **Scopolamine** | Historically promoted as a “truth serum.” It can cause confusion, disorientation and amnesia, but the historical review included in the Senate hearing says its side effects made it unsuitable even for reliable interrogation. In a controlled study, it impaired memory and information processing. **Amnesia is not evidence that a new conditioned response was installed.** [Senate hearing](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf), [human study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22281851/) |
| **LSD** | A small modern placebo-controlled study found greater responsiveness to *explicit, detailed suggestions* in 10 volunteers, but not to a brief cued-imagery measure. That is a limited finding about suggestibility, not proof of covert or durable control. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242255/) |
| **Barbiturates and other sedatives** | They may impair judgment or memory, but drugged statements can be false as well as true. They are poor evidence of either truthful disclosure or precise cue-driven obedience. [Historical review in Senate record](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-hearings-95mkultra.pdf) |

The key research distinction is **temporary impairment or suggestibility versus learning and later expressing a specific response while sober**. The cited evidence supports the former in some circumstances; it does not demonstrate the latter. The Senate record is also incomplete because many program records were destroyed, so that conclusion is about what the available evidence establishes, not a claim to know every experiment that occurred.
