**Yes—N1, P2, and N350 change with wakefulness, drowsiness, and sleep, but they aren’t direct meters of arousal.** A larger response can occur while someone is *less* awake.

They are components of an **event-related potential (ERP)**: changes in scalp-recorded electrical voltage following a stimulus, usually made clearer by averaging many presentations. **N** means a negative deflection and **P** a positive one relative to the recording reference. Those signs do **not** directly mean neural inhibition or excitation.

| Response | Approximate timing after sound | Relationship to wakefulness and sleep |
|---|---|---|
| **N1** | Around **100 milliseconds** | An early auditory response, influenced by sound intensity and attention. Usually decreases during sleep onset and becomes very small or absent in established non-REM sleep. A smaller response can persist in REM. |
| **P2** | Around **180–200 milliseconds** | Present during wakefulness, but can become **larger during sleep**, even as awareness of the environment decreases. Its function depends on the experimental context. |
| **N350** | Around **350 milliseconds** | Emerges around sleep onset and is prominent in non-REM sleep. Closely associated with **vertex sharp waves**, characteristic electrical events during light sleep. It is generally smaller in REM. |

These patterns were observed in the [near-threshold tone study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157666/). Also, **the N1 response and the N1 sleep stage are different uses of “N1.”**

“Arousal” needs two distinctions here:

- **Arousal state:** how awake or asleep someone is. These responses do vary with that state.
- **An arousal event:** a brief shift toward wakefulness. An individual P2 or N350 does **not**, by itself, establish that such a shift occurred.

One interpretation is that enhanced P2 and N350 reflect processes that **help preserve sleep despite incoming sounds**. However, that is a functional hypothesis; researchers have also considered arousal-related interpretations of N350. The waveform alone cannot settle which process is occurring. [Study discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157666/)

The strongest limitation is that **the same response changes for reasons other than arousal**. In a separate experiment, P2 and N350 increased with sound intensity, and N350 was substantially larger when sounds were spaced farther apart. Consequently, a larger N350 could reflect the stimulus schedule rather than a different level of consciousness. [Muller-Gass & Campbell, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20472957/)

For your earlier question, none of these three responses alone establishes that someone understood words, accepted a suggestion, or became more susceptible to one.
