**I found a usable commercial route and a relevant public dataset, but no verified open model that meets all your requirements:** bedside phone audio, downloadable trained weights, and low-latency sleep staging.

| Candidate | What is available | Fit for your prototype |
|---|---|---|
| **Asleep SleepTrack** | Proprietary trained model through an API; iOS/Android SDKs and sample apps | Closest option for avoiding training. Vendor documents live staging at **five-minute intervals**, with server processing—not a verified 30-second on-device solution. |
| **SoundSleepNet / HomeSleepNet** | Published smartphone-audio research | Relevant science, but I could not verify downloadable checkpoints. HomeSleepNet’s dataset is explicitly private, and its published architecture uses future audio. |
| **Nidra** | Public code and demonstration weights using PSG-Audio | An exploratory candidate, but its README reports unresolved model-loading/compilation issues and uses surrounding epochs. I would not select it as the scientific baseline yet. |

Sources: [SleepTrack product](https://www.asleep.ai/en/product), [SDK quickstart](https://docs-en.asleep.ai/docs/quickstart), [HomeSleepNet methods and data availability](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e46216), [Nidra repository](https://github.com/devangk003/Nidra).

**The dataset I would start with is PSG-Audio.** Its original publication describes **212 participants**, synchronized ambient and tracheal microphone recordings, PSG signals, and expert sleep-stage annotations. For our intended setup, we would use the **ambient microphone**, keeping the contact microphone and other physiological signals out of the model inputs. [Dataset paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-021-00977-w) · [Official dataset](https://doi.org/10.11922/sciencedb.00345)

It has two material limitations:

- Recordings came from people referred for sleep-apnea assessment, using a laboratory microphone approximately one metre above the bed. Performance there would not establish accuracy on a bedside smartphone at home.
- I verified the publication and dataset reference, but **did not verify a successful official download or the current dataset licence**. Those remain checks before use.

There is also a route that avoids training **from scratch**, while still requiring some supervised training: use a pretrained audio encoder and train a small sleep-stage classifier on PSG-Audio. A 2025 study specifically explored **HuBERT speech-model features for sound-based sleep staging**, making this a research-backed direction. However, I did not find a verified released sleep-staging checkpoint for that work. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41336581/)

My recommendation depends on the latency requirement:

- **Five-minute updates and cloud processing are acceptable:** evaluate SleepTrack first. Confirm prediction timestamps, actual reporting delay, whether live estimates are later revised, and access to the four-stage plan.
- **On-device predictions every 30 seconds are required:** use PSG-Audio plus a pretrained encoder as the starting point, expecting to train and validate a classifier. I cannot currently promise a credible zero-training option.

I have checked documentation and availability claims; **I have not run these models or measured their accuracy or latency**. The strongest unresolved issue is whether any available model estimates the *current* stage promptly, rather than delivering a delayed label for an earlier interval.
