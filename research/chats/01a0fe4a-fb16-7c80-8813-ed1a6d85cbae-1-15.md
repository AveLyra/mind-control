## What the evidence supports

The more credible horror is **influencing what people trust and remember**, not turning them into remote-controlled agents.

- Hypnotic suggestions can affect some people’s experience or performance on a specific task, but effects vary. In one small experiment, posthypnotic suggestions improved performance on a working-memory task. Hypnosis did not reliably uncover accurate hidden memories in another study; it increased incorrect recollections among some participants. [Working-memory study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32976853/) · [Memory-recall study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1402714/)
- Sleep can strengthen memories learned while awake when a cue is linked to that learning. One study also found that sleeping participants acquired a simple tone-and-odor association. That’s a narrow conditioning result, not evidence that people can absorb detailed instructions or a new belief through sleep audio. [Memory-consolidation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17347444/) · [Sleep-conditioning study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22922782/)
- These effects aren’t uniform: a 2026 study found no overall memory benefit for its sleep cue, though it did find a more limited effect related to how strongly material had been learned beforehand. [2026 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41656173/)

So the strongest scientific objection to a “sleep command” plot is that real findings concern small, specific changes in memory or conditioning. Reliable control of complex behavior is a speculative leap.
