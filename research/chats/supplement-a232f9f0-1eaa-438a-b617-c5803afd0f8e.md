## 2. It Follows (2014)

[entity annotation omitted]

This film has an unusually well-documented independent production history.

According to contemporary industry reporting:

- Animal Kingdom and Northern Lights financed the film.
- David Robert Mitchell directed it.
- Rebecca Green, Laura D. Smith, and other independent producers participated.
- RADiUS acquired American distribution rights.

The producers also discussed the financing process in an interview with Film Independent. [source 5](https://www.filmindependent.org/blog/icymi-halloween-edition-the-making-of-indie-horror-classic-it-follows-with-director-david-robert-mitchell/)

**Assessment:** The available evidence supports an ordinary independent-film production. It does not establish intelligence financing or editorial involvement.

The film's themes of pursuit, fear, and an apparently inescapable threat are open to psychological and political interpretation, but those interpretations are not evidence of covert sponsorship.

## 3. American Ultra (2015)

[entity annotation omitted]

This is particularly relevant because its fictional premise explicitly involves CIA experimentation and sleeper-agent conditioning.

The film was written by Max Landis and directed by Nima Nourizadeh.

The American Film Institute identifies its production companies, including PalmStar Media, The Bridge Finance Company, Circle of Confusion, FilmNation Entertainment, and Likely Story. [source 7](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/70408-AMERICAN-ULTRA)

Its connection to MKULTRA is thematic and explicit, rather than evidence of secret participation.

I found no production documents establishing CIA sponsorship, script approval, or operational involvement.

That distinction is important because the CIA has historically participated in entertainment productions. An intelligence-themed film, however, is not necessarily an intelligence-supported film.

## 4. Unabomber (2026)

[entity annotation omitted]

This is the most interesting case from a historical-documentation perspective.

The film dramatizes Ted Kaczynski's experiences in psychological experiments conducted by Harvard psychologist Henry Murray.

Murray previously worked for the Office of Strategic Services, the CIA's predecessor.

However, **the claim that Kaczynski's Harvard experiments were part of MKULTRA has not been conclusively established.**

The film's writers, Sam Chalsen and Nelson Greaves, acknowledge dramatizing elements of Kaczynski's relationship with Murray.

The documented production arrangement identifies 2.0 Entertainment and MRC, with Sophie Cassidy producing. [source 9](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/unabomber-release-date-cast-news)

There is no identified evidence that the CIA commissioned or financed this film.

Interestingly, the filmmakers' public statements acknowledge the distinction between documented history and invented dramatic relationships.

## 5. What the broader historical record establishes

A peer-reviewed investigation by Tom Secker and Matthew Alford, *New Evidence for the Surprisingly Significant Propaganda Role of the Central Intelligence Agency and Department of Defense in the Screen Entertainment Industry*, examined government documents concerning cooperation with film and television productions.

The researchers describe government involvement extending beyond straightforward technical advice, including influence over how military and intelligence institutions are represented.

Their findings provide evidence that intelligence and defense organizations have sometimes influenced entertainment content through production relationships. They do not establish involvement in any of the specific works examined above. [source 10](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/pid8iyWP5JRFAZSujypQ/full)

## 6. What would constitute meaningful evidence?

For the particular artists and works you identified, a stronger investigation would need to examine:

| Evidence | What it could establish |
|---|---|
| Production financing agreements | Who actually paid for a work |
| Agency entertainment-liaison records | Whether government officials assisted production |
| Script correspondence | Whether officials requested narrative changes |
| Publishing contracts and grants | Whether organizations subsidized particular writing |
| FOIA disclosures | Whether federal agencies possess relevant correspondence |
| Credible firsthand testimony | Whether participants describe specific government involvement |

A government connection somewhere in a production company's corporate structure would not, by itself, establish intelligence direction.

Likewise, similarities between a fictional narrative and someone's personal experiences would not establish that the work was commissioned to influence that person.

## Conclusion

The historical existence of covert cultural propaganda is well established. American intelligence agencies have secretly supported publications, artists, and entertainment productions.

**For these six subjects, however, I found no substantiated connection to covert American or Israeli intelligence sponsorship.**

The most promising avenue for further documentary investigation is the film industry because production credits, financing arrangements, and government entertainment-liaison records can sometimes be independently verified.

The next useful step would be a targeted examination of **FOIA-released CIA and Department of Defense entertainment-liaison records**, cross-referenced against the production companies and personnel behind *American Ultra*, *It Follows*, and *Unabomber*. That would provide stronger evidence than thematic comparisons alone.
