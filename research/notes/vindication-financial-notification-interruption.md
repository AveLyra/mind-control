# Financial Notification Interruption Pathway

This note adds a candidate pathway for financial-stress notifications as
attention-disruption or state-reactivation cues. It is a hypothesis map, not
evidence that notifications have been deliberately timed against anyone.

## Mechanism Sketch

The proposed mechanism is that a notification with financial-threat salience
can interrupt concentration more strongly than a neutral notification,
especially when it arrives during a cognitively demanding or emotionally
vulnerable state.

Candidate components:

- notification onset: sound, vibration, banner, badge, email alert, SMS, app
  push, bank alert, bill reminder, market alert, or debt/overdraft prompt;
- financial-threat salience: loss, scarcity, late payment, overdraft, account
  balance, credit, taxes, employment income, investment movement, or surprise
  expense;
- task-state timing: arrival during focused work, decision-making, sleep
  transition, conflict, creative flow, or recovery from a prior disruption;
- interruption cost: attention shift, working-memory loss, error rate, slower
  task completion, perseveration, or rumination;
- reactivation: later financial cues become more distracting after repeated
  pairing with stress or task disruption.

This pathway can be ordinary and non-coordinated. Finance-related notifications
are naturally salient because they often imply risk, scarcity, obligation, or
loss. The research question is whether timing and financial salience interact
above ordinary notification distraction.

## Testable Claims

| Claim | Current level | Main test | Main falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Financial-stress notifications disrupt attention more than neutral notifications | Hypothesis | Randomize notification content during matched tasks | Neutral notifications with equal novelty and urgency disrupt equally |
| Timing during high-load states amplifies disruption | Hypothesis | Cross notification type with low-load versus high-load task windows | Financial content has the same effect across task states |
| Repeated financial notifications condition later distraction | Hypothesis | Compare later response to financial cues after paired versus unpaired exposure | Delayed response is explained by baseline financial stress or general anxiety |
| Perceived targeting is explainable by base rates and selection | Competing explanation | Compare observed timing with device/app notification logs and random-time controls | Apparent clustering disappears after accounting for notification frequency and task schedule |

## Minimal Experiment Shape

A first design should avoid real financial harm. Use simulated or fictional
notifications unless independent review approves using participant-owned
financial alerts.

Possible factorial design:

- content: neutral notification versus financial-threat notification versus
  unrelated negative notification;
- timing: low cognitive load versus high cognitive load versus recovery period
  after an interruption;
- delivery channel: silent banner versus vibration versus sound, if ethically
  and technically controlled;
- expectation: expected scheduled alert versus unexpected alert.

Primary outcomes:

- task error rate;
- reaction time or time-to-resume-task;
- working-memory loss or missed task steps;
- self-reported stress, rumination, and perceived intrusion;
- delayed salience of financial cues.

Key controls:

- matched novelty and urgency;
- matched emotional valence without financial content;
- sham notification windows;
- random-time notification schedule;
- baseline financial stress, debt load, income volatility, anxiety, sleep, and
  notification volume;
- awareness and expectancy checks.

## Data Requirements

Useful real-world data would need:

- notification logs with timestamps and channel type;
- task-state logs or independently timestamped work sessions;
- app category or message category labels, ideally redacted into semantic
  classes rather than raw private content;
- financial-stress baseline measures;
- phone settings, do-not-disturb state, and notification-permission history;
- predeclared random or pseudo-random comparison windows.

Without notification base rates and task-state timestamps, anecdotal clustering
is weak evidence because salient interruptions are easy to remember and neutral
non-events are easy to forget.

## Claim Ceiling

This pathway can support claims about distraction, salience, timing
interactions, and conditioning under a measured design.

It cannot by itself support claims that a person was targeted, gaslit,
coordinated against, or externally controlled. Those claims require separate
evidence about who controlled the notifications, whether timing was non-random
relative to a frozen comparator, and whether mundane notification schedules,
financial stress, app defaults, advertising, or memory-selection effects fail to
explain the pattern.
