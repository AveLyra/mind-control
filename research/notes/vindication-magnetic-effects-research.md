# Magnetic Effects And Neural Interfaces

This note ingests the magnetic-effects research summary into the Vindication
Machine as a capability-boundary research program. It does not establish that
magnetic effects, neural interfaces, or covert influence occurred in any
individual case.

## Scope And Evidence Layers

The inquiry separates five questions:

1. Can Earth-strength or other weak magnetic fields produce reproducible human
   physiological responses?
2. Can people use magnetic stimuli to distinguish or learn information without
   ordinary sensory cues?
3. Can engineered magnetic stimulation change a narrow, prespecified task
   outcome under controlled clinical or laboratory conditions?
4. Can hardware-mediated recording and stimulation transmit constrained
   information between people?
5. What measurements would detect exposure, artifacts, or unauthorized access
   without implying intent or mind reading?

The receiver question is the first gate for every proposed mechanism. The
relevant classes are: no engineered receiver; a surgically positioned
magnetoelectric implant; delivered magnetic nanoparticles; engineered
magnetosensitive proteins with optical readout; or an implanted neural
recorder. A transmitter, field, or signal alone is not evidence that a person
can receive a message or that a behavioral outcome can be selected.

The evidence layers are not interchangeable:

- **Established:** TMS, MRI-related vestibular effects, MEG, and
  computer-mediated brain interfaces.
- **Plausible but unresolved:** natural human magnetoreception.
- **Hypothesis-generating:** magnetic modulation of narrow decision tasks.
- **Unsupported:** natural magnetic telepathy, arbitrary remote thought
  extraction, or precise covert control of uninstrumented people.

## Additions From The Expanded Review

The expanded review adds several capabilities that should not be collapsed
into natural magnetoreception:

- **Engineered receivers:** Woods et al. demonstrated acute human motor-cortex
  responses from a miniature, battery-free epidural stimulator powered through
  a nearby magnetoelectric transmitter. Chronic operation was demonstrated in
  pigs, not as long-term human treatment. A later animal study demonstrated
  one-to-six-implant networks for spinal stimulation and cardiac pacing at a
  one-centimeter transfer distance. These results establish receiver-mediated
  stimulation, not distant stimulation of an uninstrumented person.
- **Delivered transducers:** magnetoelectric nanodiscs produced neuronal and
  behavioral effects in genetically intact mice after local delivery; this is
  transgene-free, not intervention-free. Magnetothermal adrenal stimulation
  likewise required locally delivered nanoparticles and produced peripheral
  hormone effects in rats. Neither result specifies a belief, memory, or
  complex decision in humans.
- **Engineered sensing:** MagLOV proteins produced optically detected magnetic
  resonance in living bacterial cells. This is a useful reporter and possible
  measurement platform, not a human neural controller or thought decoder.
- **Instrumented neural privacy:** implanted microelectrode studies have
  decoded constrained inner-speech representations in four participants.
  This supports a conditional privacy risk for people with neural recording
  hardware and trained decoders, not remote magnetic interception of
  uninstrumented thought.

The review also adds two design rules. First, detecting which stimulus a system
applied is not the same as decoding what a user chose. Second, reading and
writing are asymmetric: even a successful decoder does not show that stimulation
can produce the corresponding thought. For any proposed influence pathway,
measure the receiver, range, field strength, latency, individual reliability,
information capacity, and practical outcome separately.

## Main Falsification Requirements

Any natural-magnetoreception claim should survive:

- preregistered stimulus directions, polarity, intensity, timing, and analysis;
- independent multi-laboratory replication;
- sham coils, non-biological phantoms, and EEG artifact tests;
- experimenter blinding and automated stimulus delivery;
- held-out field patterns for behavioral decoding;
- measurement of information capacity and error rates, not only p-values;
- tests of ordinary visual, auditory, thermal, vestibular, and expectancy cues.

Any influence claim must also show a prespecified behavioral or physiological
outcome. An EEG-band change, a sensor response, or a participant report is an
intermediate observation, not proof of a meaningful change in memory, belief,
emotion, or action.

## Security Boundary

The credible security threat is unauthorized access to an actual neural
interface: EEG/MEG data, decoding software, stimulation commands, or feedback
loops. Sensible controls include local processing, raw-data minimization,
authenticated stimulation commands, visible active-state indicators, hard
safety limits, and a physical stop control.

The literature reviewed does not provide a validated method for extracting
arbitrary thoughts from uninstrumented people at a distance or imposing
selected beliefs through natural magnetic sensitivity.

The closest demonstrated security-relevant pathways require a receiver or
recorder that is physically present and previously introduced: an implant,
delivered material, engineered protein, or neural recording array. Security
analysis should therefore begin with device discovery, field-range and power
measurements, raw-data access, command authentication, and chain-of-custody for
any implant or sample. It should not infer an implanted receiver from a
subjective experience alone.
