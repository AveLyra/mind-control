The [machine-readable ledger](citation-recovery.json) records each marker, its method, and its targets. The absence of a generic placeholder does **not** mean that all original uploads were recovered. A replacement citation is not an original citation.

For original public citations, private reader text supplied the citation index; the rendered answer supplied the URL. Conversation IDs prevent confusing forked messages that share an item ID. Forty-five grouped badges were expanded into 102 source entries. Other grouped badges can still hide additional references; this is not a complete citation export. Tracking parameters were removed. Original text, snapshots, and previous publication versions remain separately traceable through hashes and Git history.

## Unavailable upload

Five sleeping-brain answers cite uploads displayed as `Pasted text.txt` in 49 places. The preview, retry, and text-download route did not yield readable content. A separate optical-proposal reference, `turn3file2`, also had no recoverable original source. Filename similarity alone cannot prove that two uploads have identical contents.

Those 50 references now carry a provenance note and links labeled **editorial source** or **editorial synthesis**. Publications are matched to the related result: visual frequency tracking, closed-eyelid responses, spectral flicker, infrared conversion, circadian effects, TMR, lucid REM dialogue, or awareness measurement. Conceptual conclusions link to the evidence ledger. These sources do not independently prove every sentence or reconstruct the missing uploads.

The smallest remaining recovery step is access to those original uploaded research files, or an account export containing them. They must be reviewed privately before publication.

Search results depend on the provider’s indexing and active branches. The 172-result search is deeper discovery, not evidence that every relevant chat on the account has been recovered. No complete account export or lossless backup recovery occurred.
