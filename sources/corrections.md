## Lucid-dream citation

The Faraday optical note lists PMID **33879421** as the real-time dialogue experiment. Direct metadata retrieval identifies it as Baird, LaBerge, and Tononi's commentary, **Two-Way Communication in Lucid REM Sleep Dreaming**, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2021.04.004`.

The primary experiment is Konkoly et al., **Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep**, *Current Biology* (2021), DOI [10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026), PMID **33607035**. The commentary metadata itself identifies the primary article. The attempted primary PubMed fetch encountered a provider challenge, so this pass verified the bibliographic correction through that cross-reference, not a new full-text reading.

The archived note is retained as historical evidence; topic pages use the corrected reference.

## Synthetic interaction confounding

The [later Codex recheck](../research/chats/01a08679-d665-7a92-9c20-ce1843c10a68-52-28.md) reports that the interaction generator also made awareness condition-dependent, introducing an apparent effect under a configured zero interaction. Its reported false flags qualify earlier simulation-success language. Neither the old success report nor the later audit was rerun here. The harness should not support study power claims without repair and independent null checks.

## Theory and phenomenon

Hypnotic involuntariness is the phenomenon under discussion; dissociated control and cold control are explanatory accounts. The original dissociated-control proposal and later decoupling revisions should remain distinct. Theories are not independently validated merely because drafts describe them.

## Negative evidence is not interchangeable

The failed replication of sleep-cued counter-bias learning is not a direct replication of the later social-evaluation study. Likewise, a null result for one weak-field paradigm does not establish that all magnetic biological effects are absent. The reverse inference is equally invalid: a positive instrumented stimulation result does not settle natural weak-field perception.

## Absorption and durability are different papers

The recovered evidence archive preserves an original heading that linked **Absorption and hypnotizability: context effects reexamined** to PMID 2001895. Independent metadata identifies the absorption paper as [Nadon et al., PMID 1995834](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1995834/). [PMID 2001895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2001895/) is Berrigan et al.’s **Durability of “posthypnotic suggestions” as a function of type of suggestion and trance depth**. The archive’s own correction agrees. Original headings remain historical; the distinction governs current interpretation.

The archived answer cites it for a peer-review qualification, which is an appropriate distinct role; it should not be counted as another experiment. Likewise, the BCI answer already labels **A generalizable speech neuroprosthesis** as a preprint. Its [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42619707/) independently confirms the title, PMCID, and preprint identity.

The [2019 sleep-vocabulary article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30713104/) has a linked [erratum](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31639343/). Quantitative reuse should consult the corrected publication.
