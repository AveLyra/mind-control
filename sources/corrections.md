## Lucid-dream citation

The Faraday optical note lists PMID **33879421** as the real-time dialogue experiment. Direct metadata retrieval identifies it as Baird, LaBerge, and Tononi's commentary, **Two-Way Communication in Lucid REM Sleep Dreaming**, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2021.04.004`.

The primary experiment is Konkoly et al., **Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep**, *Current Biology* (2021), DOI [10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026), PMID **33607035**. The commentary metadata itself identifies the primary article. The attempted primary PubMed fetch encountered a provider challenge, so this pass verified the bibliographic correction through that cross-reference, not a new full-text reading.

The archived note is retained as historical evidence; topic pages use the corrected reference.

## Theory and phenomenon

Hypnotic involuntariness is the phenomenon under discussion; dissociated control and cold control are explanatory accounts. The original dissociated-control proposal and later decoupling revisions should remain distinct. Theories are not independently validated merely because drafts describe them.

## Negative evidence is not interchangeable

The failed replication of sleep-cued counter-bias learning is not a direct replication of the later social-evaluation study. Likewise, a null result for one weak-field paradigm does not establish that all magnetic biological effects are absent. The reverse inference is equally invalid: a positive instrumented stimulation result does not settle natural weak-field perception.

### Reused data and sample accounting

The [2024 warning/encoding paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38504050/) explicitly reuses fMRI data and behavioral results from the [2020 warning/retrieval report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32868423/). Its selected comparison comprises 21 prewarning and 22 no-warning participants (43 total), drawn from the reported 65-person fMRI sample. This is an additional analysis of a processing stage, not independent behavioral replication. The 2020 report also has a separate behavioral experiment with 81 reported participants.

The fMRI participant-flow descriptions disagree: the 2020 methods report 80 recruited, 19 excluded, and 65 final, while the 2024 methods report 80 recruited, 15 excluded, and 65 final. The former arithmetic does not reconcile. This review preserves the discrepancy; it has not inspected participant records or established which exclusion count is correct. The [new source checks, K05–K06](../research/updates/2026-10-28-context-reinstatement-detection.md#k05) record the complete-body inspection and its limits. This issue is separate from the corrected 2021 hippocampal prediction-error study below.

### Versions, notices and related reports

The [2021 hippocampal prediction-error report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34911768/) has a [June 2026 correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42330295/). The authors describe a preprocessing join error that wrongly excluded 4.8% of trials and mislabeled neural/behavioral pairings in 9.7%. They report revised values/figures with key conclusions unchanged. The new [notice check](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p05) is complete-notice inspection, not independent data reanalysis; the notice is not a replication.

The [reconsolidation/source-monitoring follow-up](../wiki/reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md) adds an interpretation correction: reporting misinformation after retrieval is not, by itself, evidence of original-trace erasure. Positive time-dependent replications coexist with interference accounts, qualified replications, and tests showing original details remain accessible. An AI intervention described as reconsolidation-informed does not establish that mechanism. These distinctions update the current synthesis without rewriting historical assistant research.

The [decoded-feedback follow-up](../wiki/decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#replication) corrects a potential study-counting error: the 2016 and 2017 confidence reports explicitly use identical data, with ten feedback participants. Both may be informative, but they are one intervention dataset rather than independent replications. Their earlier source records remain preserved. The common-fear data mapping needs a different qualification: the later decoder paper also contains additional validation data; it is not wholly identical merely because a shared dataset is listed under both publications. See [R03–R10](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r03).

The original crime-memory paper's 70% classification and the independent recoders' 26%-30% estimate use the same reports and different coding schemes. The separate 2018 corrigendum repairs numerical calculations, detail counts and confidence intervals; it does not substitute the recoders' rate. It is a correction, not a retraction. The 2020 video-judgment experiments add observers using earlier induction footage, rather than independently replicating memory induction. [D15-D17 and D26](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d15)

<a id="memory-persistence-and-harm"></a>
## Memory persistence, emotional harm, and recovery are separate outcomes

A recollective experience can persist after disbelief without demonstrating lasting psychological injury. The negative-picture experiments reported no notable emotional-state change. The 2021 reversal study had 52 final participants and 38 at follow-up; its complete package and true-event comparisons do not isolate an untreated reversal contrast. A different training intervention's null result is not an exact failed replication or proof that correction always fails. [D18-D22](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d18)

The sleep-deprivation false-admission experiment used quasi-random assignment; its initial odds ratio of 4.5 is not a 4.5-fold risk. Signed admissions, internalized autobiographical guilt, and enduring impairment were different endpoints. Coercive sleep loss and drug-induced historical sleep treatments are distinct from sleep learning and pink-noise cueing. [D10-D11](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d10)
