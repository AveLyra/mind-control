# Decoded feedback and AI memory distortion: replication and combinations

[Home](../README.md) · [Combined techniques](combined-techniques.md) · [Research horizon](research-horizon.md) · [Source checks](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md)

**The deeper evidence supports restricted learning and memory-distortion effects, while narrowing several larger claims. Two confidence papers share one dataset; realistic deepfakes do not consistently outperform text; and the direct sleep/hypnosis combinations found here use conventional EEG feedback rather than decoded fMRI feedback.**

Assessed October 28, 2026. This is a bounded follow-up to CT3–CT4, with 26 selected source entries, including reused publications needed to trace study relationships. It is not a systematic review. The source record distinguishes new cohorts, reused data, publication versions, methodological studies, and incomplete access. A paper count is not a replication count.

The next [mechanism follow-up](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md) examines reconsolidation, source monitoring, recoverability, and the opposing predictions sleep can make for AI-related memory errors. It preserves the modality and replication distinctions established here.

<a id="modalities"></a>
## Which technique is being combined?

| Technique | What is fed back | What the distinction prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern-based fMRI DecNef | A trained multivoxel-pattern decoder’s output | A decoded state is not an arbitrary thought that can be written back by reversing the decoder. |
| Decoded fNIRS or EEG feedback | A classifier applied to hemodynamic or electrical features | These are genuine decoding approaches, but a result in another modality is not an exact replication of an fMRI protocol. |
| Conventional EEG feedback | A frequency-band measure such as sensorimotor rhythm or theta/alpha | A conventional-feedback combination with sleep or hypnosis does not establish the same combination with multivoxel DecNef. |
| AI-mediated misinformation | Generated or edited material encountered by a person | Human memory distortion is different from an AI system storing an incorrect record. Neither alone establishes the other. |

“Without stimulus presentation” in the original DecNef work refers to its feedback stage. Participants had seen relevant visual material during decoder construction. Likewise, “unconscious” often refers to lack of knowledge of the target or feedback rule in an awake participant; it does not mean sleep or absence of all experience. [R01](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r01)

<a id="replication"></a>
## Decoded-feedback evidence: what actually replicates?

| Research line | Relationship between reports | What it establishes and leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation learning → local-motion learning | Shibata’s original experiment and Wang’s later new experiment share researchers but use different visual targets. The latter analyzed eight feedback and eight control participants. | A conceptual extension across primitive visual features, not an independent-team exact replication or evidence of flexible instruction learning. [R01–R02](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r01) |
| Confidence, 2016 → 2017 | The 2017 paper explicitly identifies its data as identical to its 2016 companion. Ten people completed feedback; 17 supplied usable initial decoder data. | Complementary analyses support confidence/performance dissociation and order effects within one selected cohort. They are not two independent replications. [R03–R04](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r03) |
| Conditioned fear → common fears → diagnosed phobia | These are successive target/population extensions with overlapping investigators. The small clinical trial has a different cohort and endpoint package. | Convergent restricted neural/physiological findings, not repeated proof of symptomatic relief or everyday behavior change. [R05–R08](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r05) |
| Common-fear intervention → fear/physiology decoder analysis | The release groups shared data from the two reports; the decoder paper also describes discovery and validation datasets. | A new 12-person validation cohort supports decoder generalization, while another 17-person validation task reuses discovery participants. This is not a new treatment replication; the whole paper must not be dismissed as identical data. [R07, R10](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r07) |
| Intended concept–symbol association | A different research team induced target patterns but did not obtain the intended symbol-specific behavioral priming. | A conceptual boundary test with an unintended search consequence, not a direct failed replication of orientation, color, or confidence learning. [R09](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r09) |
| The DecNef data collection | Five previously published study datasets were released, with sharing subsets for some cohorts. | A resource for reanalysis, not five new experiments. Reusing a released dataset cannot supply independent replication. [R10](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r10) |
| Co-adaptive decoding, 2026 | Simulations on earlier data plus new real-time proof-of-concept work target decoder/feedback compatibility. | An engineering advance to investigate, not replication of clinical efficacy or proof of new semantic transfer. [R11](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r11) |

No independent exact replication of the focal confidence protocol was identified in this search. That limits confidence in generalization; it does not negate the measured effect in the original cohort. Independence of investigators, independence of participants, matching the protocol, and matching the endpoint are four different questions.

### New modalities provide useful boundary tests

A 45-participant decoded-fNIRS study reported improved Stroop interference relative to sham. Its full text also reports **no transfer to a Stop-Signal Task**. Individualized decoder requirements and possible physiological-noise confounding limit generalization. This is an instructive case of positive task performance alongside a failed nearby transfer, rather than a general improvement in “control.” [R12](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r12)

A separate decoded-EEG study combined feedback with explicit cognitive reappraisal in 42 healthy adults and reported a benefit for low-positive-valence material. It is a direct decoding-plus-strategy combination, with a restricted endpoint. It does not establish unconscious fear treatment, sleep learning, or hypnotic control. [R13](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r13)

These studies make a universal “no human feedback-mediated change” claim too strong. Their small, task-dependent results also make “a general neural writer” too strong. The discriminating evidence remains independent target-specific behavior and transfer, with appropriate feedback controls.

<a id="ai"></a>
## AI-memory findings: convergence is not exact replication

The original visual-editing, witness-interview, and AI-summary sources remain in the [earlier ledger, D14–D16](../research/updates/2026-10-28-combined-techniques.md#d14). Their publication versions and access limits remain explicit.

| Evidence line | Replication status | Strongest qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Visual editing: 2024 preprint → 2025 CHI paper | One study in two publication versions. | A second publication identity does not add participants or independent evidence. |
| Witness interviews, N = 200 → article discussion, N = 180 | *Slip Through the Chat* is a new experiment from the same research network, changing material, conditions, and memory test. | A conceptual extension, not an independent exact replication of the witness protocol. Its measurement was immediate. [R21](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r21) |
| AI-generated summaries, independent research team | Different task and content/source-label manipulation from the earlier studies. | Supports a misinformation effect without establishing an AI-attribution advantage; it does not replicate a generative-chatbot interaction. [Prior D16](../research/updates/2026-10-28-combined-techniques.md#d16) |
| Deepfake public-event experiments, N = 682 across two experiments | A separate research line tests video against simpler formats. | False recollections occurred, but video did not consistently outperform text or photographs. [R19](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r19) |
| Fictional movie-remake study, N = 436 | A later conceptual extension from the deepfake research group. | Deepfakes were no more effective than written descriptions at distorting these memories. It is not an exact replication of AI-edited photographs or chatbot questioning. [R20](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r20) |

The IUI article-discussion experiment compared misleading chat with misleading summaries, honest interventions, and no intervention. It reported more immediate errors in the misleading-chat condition. This adds a useful comparator, but altered exposure and interaction remain part of the intervention package; a universal mechanism unique to AI is not isolated. The paper did not establish long-term persistence, sleep effects, or hypnotic amplification. [R21](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r21)

The evidence therefore converges on **susceptibility to misleading content under specified conditions**. It does not show that every AI interaction distorts recall, that more realism always produces more distortion, or that the same error rate applies to personal episodes. No independent exact replication of the focal generative-witness protocol was identified in this bounded search.

<a id="sleep"></a>
## What has actually been combined with sleep?

**Conventional EEG feedback plus sleep reactivation has a direct study.** A 2025 double-blind randomized pilot assigned 49 healthy participants to sensorimotor-rhythm feedback with or without odor-context reactivation, or random-frequency feedback with reactivation. It found no immediate reactivation benefit for training success or EEG response and no demonstrated SMR-power increase. A favorable long-delay contrast was exploratory, with only **29/49 returning**; the relevant two follow-up groups contained 12 and seven participants. The design also lacked a random-feedback/no-reactivation arm. [R14](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r14)

That study makes “no neurofeedback-plus-sleep experiment exists” incorrect. It does not close the **decoded-fMRI-plus-sleep** connection, and it does not establish robust added memory benefit. A separate 25-patient insomnia study found conventional SMR feedback no better than sham on its claimed sleep outcomes. It is a counterweight within that modality, not a failed DecNef replication. [R18](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r18)

An adjacent motor-imagery EEG-feedback study observed within-session learning but no overnight improvement in its desynchronization endpoint. Its groups differed in memory-interference tasks, so this is not a randomized sleep-versus-wake test or an exact replication of multivoxel DecNef. [R26](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r26)

### Sleep and false memory do not follow one rule

- A meta-analysis of themed-word-list experiments found no overall sleep effect on accurate or false memory; task features and recall versus recognition moderated results. This synthesis does not pool AI experiments or autobiographical-memory interventions. [R22](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r22)
- A story-based suggestibility experiment found no sleep–wake difference in false memory while accurate memory was better preserved after sleep. [R23](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r23)
- A misinformation experiment found a deprivation-related increase when deprivation affected event encoding, with no significant effect when deprivation occurred after encoding. Deprivation and ordinary sleep are different interventions. [R24](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r24)

These are adjacent mechanism tests. None directly combines generated misinformation with experimentally manipulated sleep in the sources selected here. A remembered effect surviving overnight also does not, by itself, establish a sleep-specific mechanism.

<a id="hypnosis"></a>
## What has actually been combined with hypnosis?

Conventional EEG feedback has been paired with hypnosis in small multiple-sclerosis pain studies. A 20-person pilot compared feedback-plus-hypnosis with relaxation-plus-hypnosis; 18 completed and the feedback group reported greater pain reduction. A later 32-person pilot compared feedback or mindfulness preparation with no enhancing intervention before hypnosis and reported promising symptom results. The shared investigators, changed comparators, small samples, and abstract-only inspection here preclude treating these as independent exact replications or evidence for decoded-feedback enhancement. [R15–R16](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r15)

An earlier 30-person study found theta/alpha feedback **no better than relaxation or self-hypnosis** for increasing hypnotic susceptibility, despite improvement across groups. This limits claims that the EEG manipulation itself supplied a unique enhancement mechanism. [R17](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r17)

For memory, a 60-person experiment separated high/low hypnotizability from hypnotic/waking suggestion conditions. False reports were associated with hypnotizability and test context, while induction itself did not show the proposed effect. This is a specific counterexample to treating the induction as a general amplifier; it is not proof of no hypnotic memory effect in every paradigm. [R25](../research/updates/2026-10-28-decoded-ai-replications.md#r25)

The targeted searches did not identify a direct DecNef–hypnosis trial or an AI-memory-distortion–hypnosis experiment. A theoretical comparison of LLM processing with hypnosis is not such an experiment. Combining the literatures on that basis would promote an analogy into evidence.

<a id="tests"></a>
## Which missing connections are now most informative?

| Proposed connection | What supports investigating it | What remains missing |
|---|---|---|
| Decoded feedback → sleep-dependent stabilization | Restricted feedback learning and retention; an adjacent conventional-feedback/TMR pilot. | A direct decoded-feedback comparison that isolates sleep’s contribution and preserves the intended behavioral endpoint. |
| Decoded feedback → enhanced hypnotic responding | Adjacent conventional-feedback clinical pilots. | The decoded intervention itself, suitable active controls, and separate measures of pain, agency, memory, and susceptibility. |
| Generated misinformation → sleep-related distortion | Some sleep/deprivation effects on other memory tasks. | The actual generated-content combination; neutral and contrary sleep findings oppose assuming amplification. |
| Generated misinformation → hypnosis-related distortion | Suggestion and context can influence memory reports. | A direct combination separating content, induction, trait responsiveness, reporting context, and durable independent outcomes. |
| Decoded learning → generated feedback → personal-memory change | Separate restricted feedback-learning and misinformation findings. | Intended semantic transfer, independent ground truth, component comparisons, and a demonstrated combined effect. |

These are criteria for assessing future research, not protocols executed by this project. Negative findings, same-cohort analyses, and narrower transfer should remain beside the positive reports. The science-fiction horizon moves when the **specified combined endpoint** is demonstrated, not when a new paper uses the same vocabulary.
