# Sleep techniques and learning

[Home](../README.md) · [Sources](../sources/catalog.md) · [Measurement](measurement.md) · [Research network](research-horizon.md) · [Combined techniques](combined-techniques.md) · [Replication follow-up](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md)

The recovered work examines several distinct techniques. They should be evaluated by what was learned beforehand, the verified sleep state, and the measured outcome.

| Technique | Research question | Main evidential limit |
|---|---|---|
| Targeted memory reactivation (TMR) | Does replaying a cue associated with waking learning change later retention? | Consolidating an existing association differs from teaching new instructions. |
| Sleep conditioning | Can a simple cue acquire a physiological association during sleep? | A conditioned sniff response does not establish arbitrary compliance. |
| Targeted dream incubation | Can prompts around sleep onset change reported dream themes or creative performance? | Themes, detailed dream scripts, and durable beliefs are different outcomes. |
| Closed-loop auditory stimulation | Can appropriately timed sounds change sleep oscillations? | An EEG change need not improve memory, vigilance, or behavior. |
| Masked or faint speech | Can speech be processed when unnoticed, unintelligible, or difficult to detect? | These are different awareness criteria; later forgetting cannot establish unconscious reception. |
| Lucid REM dialogue | Can a sleeper receive a question and signal an answer? | Lucid awareness and trained response channels differ from non-lucid unconscious comprehension. |

The archived [acoustic research note](../research/notes/vindication-acoustic-feature-sleep-state-coding.md) reports a TMR meta-analysis estimate of Hedges' g = 0.29, 95% CI 0.21–0.38, across 91 experiments and 2,004 participants. This is a recovered review result, not a newly recalculated estimate. Its proposed feature × state × prior-pairing interaction remains a hypothesis.

The [cross-project dossier](../research/chats/cross-project-dossier.md) collects object-location cueing, dream incubation, social evaluation, and a failed replication of sleep-cued counter-bias training. That failed replication concerns the earlier counter-bias paradigm; it is not a direct replication of the 2025 social-evaluation study.

## Masking, pitch, and EEG

The recovered **Find research on sleep hypnosis** answers distinguish a detectable sound from intelligible speech. One reviewed sleep-priming experiment mixed words with pink and brown noise and found no average semantic priming in its analyzed group. The faint-tone work used 1,000-Hz tones while varying intensity; it cannot identify an optimal pitch for hidden speech.

Auditory N1, P2, and N350 are event-related potentials, not direct readouts of comprehension or obedience. The N1 ERP and N1 sleep stage share a label but are different concepts. Larger P2/N350 responses can occur during sleep, and stimulus intensity and spacing can change their amplitude.

Find the seven recovered answers under **Find research on sleep hypnosis** in the [catalog](../sources/catalog.md). Their public study links are retained in each answer.

## Most useful unresolved test

Can a non-lucid sleeper learn a genuinely new rule and apply it to held-out examples after waking, with awareness and brief awakenings independently assessed? The archived proposals identify this as a larger claim than reactivating prior learning. No collected simulation tests it. Ordinary waking exposure, arousal, expectancy, and selective recall are strong alternative explanations that any interpretation must address.

The [2025 social-evaluation paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-025-00356-9) reports changed evaluations after name–positive-trait pairings during sleep. Its discussion also identifies no follow-up test, no wakefulness comparison, restricted participant/stimulus generalizability, and difficulty separating new integration from reactivation. Those limits prevent inferring durable, arbitrary persuasion in everyday settings. Lack of later recall alone is also insufficient to settle awareness during stimulation.
