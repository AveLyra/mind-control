## New literature update — October 28, 2026

### What the evidence supports

The 2026 multisite Registered Report found above-chance delayed orientation judgments on trials rated unseen. That supports a behavioral observation under reported unawareness. Whether those reports isolate entirely absent experience remains a separate measurement and modeling question. The study’s overall detection sensitivity was above zero, and selection of unseen-rated trials should remain visible in interpreting its results. [Franco-Martínez et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41561361/)

An independent intensive arrow-priming study reported an indirect-task advantage consistent with unconscious priming in three of six participants, conditional on the awareness task being at least as sensitive as the priming task. This is a restricted task result, with substantial variation between people, rather than a population prevalence estimate or a demonstration of new semantic learning. [Sánchez-Fuenzalida et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626369/)

Sleep stimulation studies support evoked responses through closed eyelids. Neither a response nor conventional sleep staging establishes comprehension or absence of experience. The later 40-Hz study included an optical-block control; its fixed night order qualifies sleep-quality conclusions. [Norton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098351/), [Hainke et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39700417/)

The “invisible spectral flicker” label also requires qualification: the supplied review reports higher perceived-flicker ratings than steady-light controls in the 2024 study. Reduced visibility and a neural response should not be converted into a claim of completely absent awareness. [Hansen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39613776/)

Lucid-dream communication remains evidence of meaningful responding during some dreams, including a trained optical example. The successful optical participant reported experiencing the code; results pooled across modalities are not an optical-only success rate. Auditory sleep-learning studies supply comparison evidence, not validation of an optical channel. [Konkoly et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026), [Koroma et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.801666)

The supplied review did not locate a directly and independently replicated demonstration combining unaided optical reception, arbitrary-message comprehension, delayed retention, and absent experience in ordinary non-lucid sleep. This is a finding within its reported search, not proof of impossibility or an exhaustive census of every study.

### Objective access and subjective experience

**Reliable choices between a code’s meanings already demonstrate objective content access.** The further question can concern access without reported subjective experience, or an indirect-task advantage under explicit assumptions about measurement. A successful meaning-choice task and an information-identical forced-choice “awareness” task cannot coherently be used to claim both access and zero objective access without explaining their discrepancy.

Keep awareness of light, modulation, pattern, code, meaning, experimental regularity, and later recollection separate. Confidence and visibility are different reports. During sleep, stage, arousal, dream content, lucidity, and recall likewise require distinct assessment. The supplied review’s [method comparison and conceptual design](../research/updates/2026-10-28-awareness/awareness-optical-review-2026-10-28.md) develop these distinctions.

A nonsignificant awareness test does not establish absence. Use uncertainty intervals and justified equivalence bounds or explicit model comparisons; retain prior/model sensitivity and the precision of individual measurements. Selecting low awareness scores and reusing those same noisy scores to certify unawareness can create an apparent dissociation. Preregistration makes choices inspectable but does not remove that problem. The [evidence appendix](../research/updates/2026-10-28-awareness/awareness-optical-evidence-2026-10-28.md) preserves both positive results and methodological objections.

### Remaining tests and limits

Label each result by its demonstrated achievement: physical delivery, evoked response, neural discrimination, participant discrimination, access to learned meaning, new association learning, delayed retention, or transfer. An EEG classifier’s access is distinct from the participant’s access. A new association to a familiar concept is narrower than acquiring a new concept or comprehending an arbitrary sentence.

The supplied conceptual proposal begins with a previously learned simple code and separates waking open-eye, closed-eye, and staged-sleep tests. Its controls address physical delivery, other sensory cues, decoding artifacts, subjective reports, arousals, missing responses, delayed memory, and transfer. This remains an unexecuted design. If contemporaneous experience cannot be measured adequately during sleep, the conclusion must retain the narrower description of processing during scored sleep with specified arousal and recall findings.
