# Population influence and fictional extrapolation

[Home](../README.md) · [Conditioning](conditioning.md) · [Experiments](experiments.md)

The [population influence agenda](../research/notes/vindication-population-influence-agenda.md) maps questions about polarization, demobilization, shared salience, commercial activation, timing, semantic self-generation, and notification interruption. It is an agenda, not an empirical finding that a particular actor manipulated anyone.

Aggregate effects require their own evidence. A narrow individual task effect does not establish durable political conversion or population-level precision. Ecological data cannot by themselves identify an individual's exposure or response. Curation, ordinary persuasion, selection, common shocks, dropout, and platform use are potential competing explanations.

The recovered **Summarize mind-control techniques** discussion also developed fiction using memory uncertainty, sleep cues, and altered agency. Those excerpts retain explicit speculative boundaries. Story proposals do not become observed mechanisms because they cite real experiments. A system that reliably selects associations, generalizes across people, controls detailed dreams, or produces durable mass obedience requires evidence beyond this collection.

The public collection excludes character biographies, personal allegations, and unrelated literary canon. The **Research mind control topics** chat was useful for locating the October 15 cross-project evidence dossier; its project-specific fictional decisions are not imported as research truth.

The strongest objection to an all-explaining control theory is lack of a discriminating prediction: if both compliance and resistance count as success, it cannot be tested. A useful claim specifies an exposure, outcome, comparison, time window, and result that would count against it.
