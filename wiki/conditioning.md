# Conditioning, repetition, and suggestion

[Home](../README.md) · [Hypnosis](hypnosis.md) · [Sleep](sleep.md)

The original **Research conditioning and hypnosis** conversation distinguishes learning a response from obeying an instruction. Its recovered answers cover fear learning, anticipatory nausea, conditioned immune responses, repetition, familiarity, memory confidence, and false recollection.

## Separate mechanisms

- Classical conditioning pairs a cue with an event or response. A cue's acquired emotional or bodily effect does not encode an arbitrary command.
- Operant learning depends on consequences linked to behavior. Repetition of a phrase alone does not establish that contingency.
- Repetition may increase familiarity or change confidence; repeated checking can reduce confidence in memory. Neither effect establishes a universal induction method.
- Suggestion can influence reports, experience, and selected tasks. Accurate recall and confidence must be measured separately.
- Awareness is paradigm-dependent. Evidence about one fear-learning task cannot settle every form of conditioning without awareness.

The human [conditioned immunosuppression experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12468450/) is a primary-source lead from the recovered work. A conditioned laboratory endpoint does not show that necessary treatment can be replaced, or that all effects of a medicine can be reproduced by a cue.

## Self-generated language and bodily cues

The [semantic and kinesthetic note](../research/notes/vindication-semantic-kinesthetic-association.md) asks whether self-generated language, speech, typing, posture, movement, or haptic cues strengthen associations beyond matched passive exposure. It is a hypothesis map. Wrong-feature controls, matched word counts, expectancy checks, and ordinary arousal are necessary to interpret any proposed interaction.

These are research questions about measured responses. They do not establish an explanation for a particular person's experiences or an actor's intent. A result explained equally well by intensity, expectancy, ordinary learning, or selective recall would narrow the proposed mechanism.
