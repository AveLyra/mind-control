# Evidence ledger

[Home](../README.md) · [Coverage](../sources/coverage.md) · [Research network](research-horizon.md)

These are editorial evidence classifications, not new experimental results. Original publications and records must be checked before quantitative reuse.

| Claim | Type / present support | Strongest relevant objection | What would change the assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior-learning cues can affect later memory under some sleep conditions | Literature finding recovered in multiple sources | Variable effects and negative studies limit transfer | Independent replication with comparable task, stage verification, and delayed outcomes |
| Sleep-onset prompts can affect reported dream themes | Literature finding | Reporting demand, awakenings, and incubation procedure matter | Blinded controlled replication with prespecified dream scoring |
| Hypnotic responses may feel involuntary | Reported phenomenon; mechanism disputed | Subjective agency does not identify executive mechanism | Discriminating tests of dissociated-control, metacognitive, and sociocognitive predictions |
| Conditioning can affect specific physiological responses | Primary-source leads recovered | Specific endpoints do not imply arbitrary behavior | Replication and transfer tests tied to the stated endpoint |
| Covert behavioral experimentation occurred historically | Supported by official historical records | Objectives, injury, and successful programming are different claims | Original project results establishing a specified capability |
| Light can evoke neural or physiological responses without ordinary reported flicker | Literature leads; some source retrieval blocked | Response and awareness measures do not establish semantic comprehension | Objective message decoding and retention with suitable awareness controls |
| Engineered magnetic receivers support neural stimulation | Primary engineering demonstration | Receiver, distance, and species restrictions prevent broad extrapolation | Reproducible outcome under the newly claimed receiver and exposure conditions |
| Natural weak-field response provides a useful human message channel | Unresolved hypothesis | Artifacts, null studies, and no demonstrated information channel | Independent blinded replication plus held-out behavioral decoding |
| Sleep-EDF simulation shows influence on people | Unsupported | No exposure occurred; outcomes were synthetic | A separate measured human study; rerunning simulation cannot supply it |
| Scopolamine can impair memory and self-monitoring | Controlled human-study finding; [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20158291/) | The experiment did not measure obedience; components of cognition are affected differently | Independent controlled replication tied to the specific proposed outcome |
| Scopolamine reliably produces programmable cooperation | Not established by the recovered review | Mixed substances, delirium, social pressure, and uncontrolled case selection | Replicated evidence isolating the drug and measuring the specified behavior |
| A realistic generated scene faithfully recovers autobiographical details | Not established; [counterexample research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40499302/) | A generator can invent plausible details from broad categories | Held-out ground truth, shuffled neural controls, and improvement beyond interview-only baselines |
| An isolated gesture identifies deception | Unsupported diagnostic inference | Context, base rates, and weak behavioral cues; [meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr1003_2) | Independent calibrated prediction beyond appropriate base-rate and context baselines |
| A person or institution caused a particular unexplained event | Not established by this collection | Ordinary causes, missing exposure evidence, and selection bias | Independent exposure/source evidence and a discriminating causal analysis |
| Imagery rehearsal plus sleep cueing can improve a specified nightmare endpoint | Direct combination study; [CT1](combined-techniques.md#ct1) | Small trial; other augmentation procedures have null results; component interaction not fully isolated | Independent exact-procedure replication with delayed outcomes and relevant component comparisons |
| Hypnotically increased slow-wave sleep automatically improves memory | Counterexample in a reanalysis; [CT2](combined-techniques.md#ct2) | Sleep depth, oscillatory coordination, and memory are separate endpoints | A direct retained-learning benefit in the proposed combination, rather than a favorable sleep proxy |
| Decoder-guided reinforcement can alter restricted human responses | Primary human studies; [CT3](combined-techniques.md#ct3) | Intended symbol–meaning transfer failed; neural response changed without significant reported-fear change | Intended independent transfer and meaningful durable behavior under adequate controls |
| Generated material can distort recall of studied details | Primary human experiments; [CT4](combined-techniques.md#ct4) | Detail errors do not establish arbitrary personal-memory replacement or synergy with sleep/neural techniques | Independent ground truth, matched content controls, interaction analysis, and delayed transfer |
| Two confidence publications independently replicate a DecNef effect | Incorrect for the 2016/2017 companions; [cohort trace](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#replication) | Their experimental data are identical; a new analysis is not a new sample | An appropriately matched independent experiment with its own participants and prespecified endpoints |
| Realistic deepfakes necessarily distort memory more than text | Counterexamples in controlled format comparisons; [AI evidence](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#ai) | Content, task, reporting measure, and exposure matter; vividness is not a universal causal advantage | A content-matched format effect with independent replication and defined generalization |
| Existing neurofeedback-plus-sleep or hypnosis studies close the DecNef combination gap | Not established; [sleep](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#sleep) / [hypnosis](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#hypnosis) | Conventional band feedback, decoded patterns, retention, and causal sleep tests are distinct | Direct decoded-feedback component comparisons with the specified retained-learning or behavioral outcome |
| A post-retrieval misinformation error uniquely demonstrates reconsolidation | Underdetermined mechanism; [replication and alternatives](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md#replication) | Context binding, new learning, retrieval competition, source attribution, and reporting criteria can overlap with updating endpoints | Reactivation/time-dependent specificity and tests of original-memory accessibility that discriminate the competing accounts |
| Sleep necessarily increases AI-related false memory | Untested cross-field direction; [sleep evidence](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md#sleep) | Sleep can stabilize old memories, preserve spatial context, or alter gist errors; the net sign is task dependent | Direct AI-content comparisons separating factual accuracy, source memory, gist, and delayed retention |
| Hypnotic familiarity or chatbot-related emotional improvement proves personal-memory rewriting | Endpoint substitution; [hypnosis](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md#hypnosis) / [AI](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md#ai) | Familiarity, context recollection, distress, confidence, and factual content are separable | Independent factual/source measures and direct component comparisons; symptom or reality ratings alone do not establish replacement |
| Greater prediction error necessarily produces more memory rewriting | Unsupported generalization; [boundary tests](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md#prediction-error) | Error definition, prediction outcome, context, and endpoint change the result; a modified replication had limited interpretable retention | Isolated mnemonic-error manipulation and original/source outcomes, distinguished from novelty, context and new learning |
| Initial retrieval uniformly harms eyewitness memory | Conditional behavioral effect; [RES boundaries](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md#retrieval) | Uncontaminated details can improve; protective testing, discrepancy detection and delay placement qualify RES | Matched component comparisons and unconditional/conditional scores rather than a universal format rule |
| A larger sleep/hypnosis mismatch response demonstrates a stronger memory-writing channel | Unsupported endpoint transfer; [sleep](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md#sleep) / [hypnosis](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md#hypnosis) | ERP amplitude can dissociate from information; hypnotic MMN findings are mixed; semantics/source outcomes are separate | Intended content transfer and later independent memory/source tests, beyond sensory response magnitude |
| Restoring context necessarily makes a memory more accurate | Unsupported generalization; [competing reinstatement results](context-reinstatement-and-detection.md#context) | Matching backgrounds can increase true and false recognition; strategy/odor effects can instead protect | Specify context, timing, hits, false alarms and source judgments; test both predicted directions |
| Noticing a discrepancy guarantees later resistance to misinformation | Conditional support, not a guarantee; [detection and remembered change](context-reinstatement-and-detection.md#detection) | Detection can be forgotten; postselected detectors may have stronger initial memories | Manipulated comparison support plus delayed memory for the conflict, with initial-strength and unconditional outcomes |
| Neural or sleep context reinstatement establishes AI-driven memory replacement | Unsupported combined-mechanism inference; [neural](context-reinstatement-and-detection.md#neural) / [sleep](context-reinstatement-and-detection.md#sleep) | Wrong-source reinstatement, restricted sleep nulls, and source-decision effects are different endpoints | Direct component comparisons and independently known episodic facts; confidence and neural response alone are insufficient |
| A later encoding paper independently replicates the warning fMRI experiment | Incorrect study counting; [dataset/sample check](../sources/corrections.md#study-relationships-and-sample-accounting) | The 2024 paper reuses the 2020 fMRI data and selects two conditions; published exclusion counts disagree | Separate new analyses from new cohorts and resolve participant flow against original records |
| A successful posthypnotic cue test weeks later establishes continuous behavioral control | Overextended durability inference; [follow-up distinctions](posthypnotic-sleep-and-pink-noise.md#durability) | Repeated tests/reactivation, selected participants, and waking-request controls qualify the interpretation | Behavior between tests, matched instructions, independent replication, and persistence without renewed cueing |
| Sleep conditioning reliably changes later habits | Restricted positive evidence with transfer limits; [behavioral studies](posthypnotic-sleep-and-pink-noise.md#sleep-conditioning) | Motivated smokers and diary outcomes differ from craving, implicit scores, and opposed goals; replication/transfer is mixed | Independent replication with specified objective behavior, longer follow-up, and matched exposure controls |
| Pink noise is a uniform relaxation or suggestibility signal | Unsupported generalization; [three roles](posthypnotic-sleep-and-pink-noise.md#pink-noise) | Masking, evoked oscillations, and learned context have different endpoints; a learned cue had opposing sleep/wake effects | Separate acoustic exposure from prior association and measure the intended behavioral outcome |
| Increased slow-wave activity proves a lasting behavioral benefit | Unsupported proxy substitution; [acoustic replications](posthypnotic-sleep-and-pink-noise.md#pink-noise) | Oscillations can improve without performance; repeated exposure is not persistence after stopping | Replicated behavioral contrasts and post-withdrawal follow-up, rather than an EEG endpoint alone |

The evidence chain is: **delivery → detection → discrimination → learned meaning → comprehension → retention → specified behavior → source and intent**. Evidence at one step does not certify the next. Neural response, memory impairment, suggestion response, and subjective loss of agency should remain separate measurements.

These classifications combine preserved research with the bounded primary-source checks described in the [citation audit](../sources/citation-audit.md). Bibliographic identity and successful HTTP access do not establish that every historical quantitative claim is correct.
