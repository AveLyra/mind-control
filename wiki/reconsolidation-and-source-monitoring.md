# Reconsolidation and source monitoring: links between AI misinformation, sleep, and hypnosis

[Home](../README.md) · [Memory](memory.md) · [Replication follow-up](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md) · [Research horizon](research-horizon.md) · [Source checks](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md)

**Source monitoring provides a supported account of some memory-report errors. Reconsolidation is a candidate mechanism for some post-retrieval changes, with positive replications and important competing explanations. The proposed AI–sleep–hypnosis chain remains an inference across experiments, rather than a demonstrated combined capability.**

Assessed October 28, 2026. This bounded follow-up selects 34 source entries: 33 PubMed publications and one AI-intervention preprint. They include primary experiments, theory, and synthesis, not 34 independent replications. Access and study relationships are recorded separately in the source checks.

The [prediction-error/RES follow-up](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md) now checks the mismatch and retrieval pathways more closely, including protective testing results, mixed sensory findings, and a correction to the 2021 fMRI report. This page's dated source record remains preserved.

<a id="distinctions"></a>
## Two mechanisms that can look alike in a memory test

| Concept | What it means | What a changed answer does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Reconsolidation | Restabilization after a previously consolidated memory has been reactivated and destabilized. It can support strengthening or updating. | Retrieval need not destabilize a memory; a later error does not by itself show that the original trace was overwritten. |
| Source monitoring | Judging whether remembered content came from an event, a later account, imagination, or another source. | Recognizing an item or finding it vivid does not establish its origin. A source error need not imply loss of the original information. |
| Retrieval-enhanced suggestibility | Under some conditions, taking a test before misinformation increases later reporting of that misinformation. | This behavioral result does not uniquely identify reconsolidation, permanent impairment, or a mechanism specific to AI. |
| Reappraisal or rescripting | Changing the interpretation or emotional response associated with an event. | Reduced distress is different from altered factual content, source attribution, or obedience. |

The source-monitoring framework describes judgments based on perceptual detail, contextual information, reflective operations, and flexible criteria. It is a theoretical framework supported by a research literature, not an experiment demonstrating every proposed combination. [M01](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m01)

The accounts can coexist: updating concerns changes to a memory, while source monitoring concerns how retrieved information is attributed. A combined explanation must establish both steps rather than treating one as a substitute for evidence of the other.

<a id="replication"></a>
## Reconsolidation: the replication record cuts both ways

| Evidence | Relationship and result | What remains disputed |
|---|---|---|
| Hupbach's object-list studies | A reminder preceded asymmetric intrusions of later objects into an earlier episode. A follow-up explicitly tested source attribution. | The asymmetry supports updating but does not, alone, identify a unique biological restabilization process. [M03–M04](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m03) |
| Parks and colleagues' 2022 replication | A different research team reproduced the critical delayed-versus-immediate intrusion pattern, with some materials adapted. | Positive direct replication of a specified behavioral pattern; not proof that all retrieval causes rewriting. [M06](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m06) |
| Klingmüller and colleagues' three experiments | Ordinary-context replication failed; a salient context produced a small qualitative replication. A further test favored an interference/context-binding account. | Similar intrusions can arise without accepting destabilization of the original trace. [M07](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m07) |
| Other failed or qualified replications | Four direct motor-memory attempts and three conceptual sequence-learning experiments did not support the expected updating impairment. A separate N = 96 picture-memory replication lacked substantial support for the crucial reactivation-plus-learning versus learning-only difference. | These target different original paradigms; they are not seven failed replications of the object-list study. [M08–M09](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m08) |
| Episodic-memory meta-analysis | The synthesis found reactivation-related interference and intrusions. | This 2017 synthesis does not incorporate all subsequent replication work; its intrusion endpoint also needs mechanistic interpretation. [M05](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m05) |

Prediction error is a plausible boundary condition. Sinclair and Barense's video experiments linked interrupted, surprising reminders to later intrusions and tested a time-course prediction. The often-cited **N = 408** sample rated stimuli; the memory experiments had **N = 48 and N = 24**. A later same-network fMRI study connected prediction errors, hippocampal representation changes, and updating. These are converging behavioral/neural results, not a direct measurement of trace erasure or an independent replication of an AI intervention. [M10–M11](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m10)

**Current correction status:** the fMRI report has a June 2026 preprocessing correction. The authors report revised numerical results/figures with key conclusions unchanged. The [complete-notice check](prediction-error-and-suggestibility.md#correction) distinguishes this from independent data reanalysis or a new experiment.

The strongest objection to a general rewriting account is **non-unique explanation**: new learning, context binding, retrieval competition, reporting criteria, and source confusion can produce overlapping outcomes. Positive time-dependent results constrain alternatives; they do not make every later error diagnostic of reconsolidation.

<a id="ai"></a>
## What connects this to AI misinformation?

The [existing AI experiments](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#ai) establish restricted effects of misleading generated material and conversations on later reports. The next question is which process produces those reports.

1. **Source confusion is a plausible link.** Content from a chatbot can later be mistaken for content from the original event. Garry and colleagues explicitly apply reality/source monitoring to LLM misinformation, but their article is a theoretical analysis, not a mediation experiment or a combined sleep/hypnosis trial. [M02](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m02)
2. **Retrieval can change what is learned next.** Early eyewitness work found increased suggestibility after testing. A later new-team methods comparison reproduced increased reporting under some tests without finding impairment of the original memory under a modified recognition test. Another study found that testing increased susceptibility immediately but reduced it at a longer interval. [M12–M14](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m12)
3. **Self-reference is adjacent evidence, not an AI result.** A 2026 N = 28 ERP study found greater acceptance of misinformation about self-referential scenarios and neural differences interpreted as reactivation/source-monitoring demands. ERP components do not uniquely establish reconsolidation or successful rejection of misinformation. [M33](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m33)

A 2026 preregistered chatbot preprint reported reduced breakup distress versus assessment-only control. It was reconsolidation-informed, but did not experimentally establish that mechanism or test misinformation. Seven-day completion was **171/254**; control, feedback, and attrition limitations remain material. This is evidence for an AI-delivered intervention package, not for AI-driven factual memory rewriting. [M34](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m34)

Labeling content “AI-generated” tests an external attribution cue; it is not equivalent to measuring whether a person remembers where a specific detail originated. The earlier summary experiment's source-label null therefore cannot settle source monitoring as the mediator. Nor does calling an intervention “reconsolidation-informed” demonstrate destabilization and restabilization.

<a id="sleep"></a>
## Sleep can stabilize, update, and preserve context

| Direct or adjacent evidence | Result | Limit on the proposed link |
|---|---|---|
| Reactivation during slow-wave sleep versus waking | The same associated odor cue stabilized memories against interference during sleep, while waking reactivation destabilized them. | Reactivation has state-dependent consequences; sleep is not simply an open rewriting window. [M15](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m15) |
| Sleep after waking reactivation | Studies report faster restabilization or stronger retention after reactivation. | Strengthening and resistance to interference differ from incorporation of false content; some measures are indirect stability proxies. [M16–M17](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m16), [M19](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m19) |
| Incomplete versus complete sleep cues | Incomplete cues supported longer-lasting stabilization in the tested task; both cue types supported the shorter-term result. | Cue completeness and delay matter, but the endpoint is stabilization, not new narrative transmission. [M20](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m20) |
| Episodic updating tracked across nights | Sleep/spindle measures correlated with more updating under particular prior-night conditions. | Observational associations do not isolate a causal spindle mechanism. [M18](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m18) |
| A contrary interference result | One word-pair study found interference specifically after evening learning followed by sleep. | Sleep's protection is not universal across timing, task, and later reactivation. [M21](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m21) |
| Autobiographical rescripting plus a nap, N = 44 | Immediate advantages in selected cognition/heart-rate outcomes were no longer evident one week later. | Emotional updating is closer to the proposed application, but it does not demonstrate changed factual memory or enduring added benefit. [M22](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m22) |

The source-monitoring prediction has a useful counterweight: two experiments found a sleep benefit for **spatial source memory**, replicated within the same paper, but not for frame color or bound spatial/color memory. The latter nulls may involve a floor effect. These online night-versus-day comparisons used a multinomial model to separate memory and guessing; they do not establish a benefit for every contextual feature or isolate all time-of-day effects. [M23](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m23)

A registered DRM report, **N = 488**, further separates outcomes: sleep was associated with better recall of studied words and fewer unrelated intrusions; critical-lure recall increased in the analysis accounting for intrusions/time-of-day. Gist-like errors and unrelated inventions are different measures. These word-list findings do not directly test AI misinformation, hypnosis, or autobiographical replacement. [M24](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m24)

**Inference:** sleep might preserve misleading content, preserve evidence of its source, strengthen an original episode, or affect these differently. The net direction in an AI-misinformation task must be measured. The existing results do not justify choosing amplification in advance.

<a id="hypnosis"></a>
## Hypnosis connects more directly to attribution and retrieval than to proven trace rewriting

Bryant and Mallard's N = 20 pilot and later N = 40 real–simulator comparison tested perceived reality during a suggested visual experience. The later study opposed a simple acting explanation, but used a different comparison within the same research team. These are perception/reality-rating experiments, not independent exact replications of autobiographical memory alteration; expectancy and contextual explanations are not eliminated wholesale. [M25–M26](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m25)

Kihlstrom's report of three experiments on posthypnotic amnesia is more directly relevant to memory source. Selected responsive participants could recognize some items they could not freely recall, yet had difficulty identifying list membership and temporal order. Looser recognition criteria increased hits **and false alarms**. This dissociates familiarity, recollection, contextual information, and reporting threshold; it does not demonstrate stored memories being deleted or replaced. The experiments are conceptual extensions of earlier amnesia work, not AI/sleep combinations. [M27](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m27)

The focused reconsolidation/hypnosis search found reviews and clinical/theoretical discussions, not a selected experiment isolating hypnosis-induced destabilization and restabilization. The [earlier induction/trait comparison](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#hypnosis) also cautions against treating induction itself as a universal false-memory amplifier. Memory reports, perceived reality, symptom relief, trait responsiveness, and agency remain separate endpoints.

<a id="counterevidence"></a>
## Source errors can sometimes be corrected

Lindsay and Johnson eliminated a suggestibility effect in their task by orienting participants to source information. Lane and colleagues reduced misattributions with relevant feature knowledge or feedback. Wyler and Oswald's N = 429 experiments found that the reasons for reporting misinformation, and the benefit of a warning/source test, differed by item centrality. [M28–M30](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m28)

Oeberst and Blank restored access to original details in three experiments, including a five-week condition, using a task that separated competing items and sources. Wulff and colleagues found warnings reduced retrieval-enhanced suggestibility near misinformation exposure, but not after an additional delay. These are specific recoverability findings, not a universal reversal technique. Together they challenge an inference from one wrong answer to permanent destruction of the original memory. [M31–M32](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m31)

Those delay comparisons did not isolate sleep from elapsed time. A failed warning after a delay also does not, by itself, demonstrate that the original trace has been erased.

<a id="connections"></a>
## Which connections remain hypotheses?

| Proposed connection | Established components | Evidence still needed / strongest objection |
|---|---|---|
| AI misinformation → source confusion | Generated-content memory errors; source errors in non-AI misinformation tasks; an explicit theoretical link. | Item-level source judgments and original-memory accessibility, rather than only confidence or an AI-authorship label. New learning/reporting explanations remain viable. |
| AI misinformation → reconsolidation updating | Post-retrieval updating in some paradigms, including a positive direct replication. | Reactivation-dependent, time-dependent specificity for the AI effect, with measures that distinguish original-memory impairment from source competition. Mixed replications constrain generalization. |
| Sleep → persistence or correction of AI-related errors | Sleep-related stabilization, updating, context preservation, and task-dependent false recall. | A direct AI-content comparison separating factual accuracy, source accuracy, gist, and delayed retention. The plausible effects have opposing signs. |
| Hypnosis → AI-related source misattribution | Suggested-reality and posthypnotic context-memory effects in selected participants. | A direct content/source test separating induction, trait responsiveness, expectation, and reporting criteria. Changed perceived reality does not establish changed history. |
| AI + hypnosis + sleep → durable personal-memory replacement | Separate findings in restricted tasks. | A demonstrated combined endpoint, independent ground truth, and component comparisons. No such system is established by these selected sources. |

These are criteria for interpreting research, not intervention protocols performed by this project. The most informative advance would identify **which information remains available, where the person attributes it, and which component changes that result**. Evidence of a lasting error would still need to distinguish altered content, altered access, altered source judgment, and altered willingness to report.
