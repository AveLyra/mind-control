# Documented abuse, psychological harm, and the limits of control claims

[Home](../README.md) · [Hypnosis](hypnosis.md) · [Sleep](sleep.md) · [Durability and pink noise](posthypnotic-sleep-and-pink-noise.md) · [History](history-and-drugs.md) · [Source checks](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md)

Updated October 29, 2026. **Professional exploitation involving hypnosis, disputed memory recovery, and harmful coercive treatment have documentary support. Reliable installation of lifelong guilt, selected attachments, or obedience through hypnosis and sleep cues remains unestablished.** The eight earlier fictional examples are mapped below to their closest evidence; they are not eight reconstructed patient histories.

<a id="reading-the-record"></a>
## What each kind of evidence establishes

| Evidence | What can be established | What remains separate |
|---|---|---|
| Filing, affidavit, or applicant's account | A named person or institution made the recorded allegation; some statements describe firsthand observation. | An allegation's truth, independent corroboration, and a technique's causal contribution. |
| Order or appellate disposition | The court entered the specified procedural or evidentiary decision. | Proof of every underlying allegation, a clinical diagnosis, or a controlled mechanism test. |
| Retrospective clinical/survivor report | Reported experiences, symptoms, relationships, and sequences of events. | Population prevalence, absence of selection/recall effects, and isolation of one treatment component. |
| Laboratory study | The measured response in its selected sample, task, and observation period. | Enduring disability, real-world compliance against resistance, or transfer to intimate relationships. |
| Fictional extension | A proposed consequence worth exploring in a story. | Evidence that the proposed technology or complete outcome exists. |

Cases can support serious concern without identifying a unique mechanism. Ordinary authority, dependence, expectation, deception, other treatment components, and pre-existing illness must remain candidate explanations. A lack of corroboration does not establish that an abuse allegation is false; neither does retracting it.

<a id="hypnosis-cases"></a>
## Hypnosis within an exploitative relationship

**Michael Fine, Ohio, 2014-2015.** The public emergency suspension motion includes an investigator's sworn account of recordings and a monitored encounter, plus allegations of clients' memory gaps and sexual exploitation. The two client-affidavit exhibits are marked sealed in the public copy; their underlying text was not recovered. The Ohio Supreme Court subsequently accepted Fine's resignation with disciplinary action pending. These are primary records of a professional-abuse investigation and an actual licensing disposition. They do not provide an independent experiment separating hypnotic effects from the surrounding relationship or a standardized long-term clinical follow-up. The later criminal judgment was not retrieved in this pass. [D01-D02](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d01)

**Dutch lay-hypnotist case, reported by expert witness E. Hoencamp, 1990.** His report concerns allegations involving nine women and describes subjective nonvolition within a purported therapeutic relationship. The abstract attributes vulnerability to the interpersonal context as well as hypnosis. The report supports taking such exploitation seriously; its accessible abstract does not establish the judicial outcome, individual long-term diagnoses, or a success rate for compelling strangers. [D03](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d03)

These records undercut an absolute assurance that hypnosis-related abuse is impossible. They also fail to establish that hypnosis is necessary or sufficient for the abuse, or that a person's apparent calm, participation, or bodily response establishes consent.

<a id="memory-and-family"></a>
## Memory recovery, family rupture, and damaged evidence

**Ramona v. Superior Court, California, 1997.** The opinion records a sodium-amytal interview, assurances about truthfulness, subsequent certainty about alleged abuse, divorce, and job loss. The appellate court excluded the challenged testimony. This concerns drug-assisted memory recovery and psychotherapy; it is not an exposure to pink noise or an experiment in hypnosis. The evidentiary ruling does not establish that every alleged event was false or that all later-recovered memories are unreliable. [D04](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d04)

**Retraction reports.** Lief and Fetkewicz surveyed 100 people approached through the False Memory Syndrome Foundation; 40 returned questionnaires. Their accounts include perceived worsening during therapy and efforts to restore family relationships. Ost, Costall, and Bull separately analyzed retraction accounts in relation to false-confession models. These are primary reports about consequential experiences, with substantial selection, retrospective-report, and verification limits. Neither report warrants treating a retraction as an independent truth test, diagnosing all participants, or estimating the frequency of harmful psychotherapy. [D08-D09](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d08)

**R. v. Trochym, Canada, 2007.** The court's bulletin describes a witness changing the day of a sighting following hypnosis and jurors not being told about hypnosis or the earlier account. The conviction was set aside and a new trial ordered; the decision also involved separate similar-fact evidence. This documents a consequential evidentiary failure, rather than proving intentional memory fabrication or the accused's factual innocence. The retrieved source is the court's official bulletin and headnote, not the full reasons or a review of later proceedings. [D07](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d07)

<a id="sleep-and-treatment"></a>
## Sleep-related coercion and lasting impairment

**Cameron and Orlikow.** The 1988 federal opinion records concealed CIA funding and exposure to components of a mixed treatment package. It addressed the government's summary-judgment defenses; injury causation and the disputed character of the treatment remained issues for trial. It is not a finding that personality reconstruction succeeded. [D05](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d05)

**Tanny and other applicants, Quebec, 2025.** A class-action authorization judgment recounts medical-note material describing Charles Tanny's confusion and disorientation, followed by his daughter's allegations of enduring family detachment and harm. It also records Lana Ponting's allegations of nightmares and cognitive/functional loss. The court allowed claims to proceed; it did not finally determine their merits. These histories concern mixed drug, electroconvulsive, and other interventions. Ordinary sleep conditioning, targeted memory reactivation, and pink noise cannot be assigned their alleged effects. The 1988 and 2025 proceedings are related historical evidence, not independent experimental replications. [D06](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d06)

**Coercive sleep deprivation.** A retrospective study of 600 former Palestinian detainees associated sleep-deprivation categories with acute/chronic suffering and, for systematic deprivation, longer-term family, social, and physical impacts. Detention exposures and pre-existing differences complicate causal attribution. More signed statements did not correspond to significantly more convictions or longer sentences in that sample. Sleep loss is distinct from learning while asleep. [D10](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d10)

A separate laboratory study used quasi-random assignment of 88 participants to rested/deprived conditions. On the first request, 22/44 deprived versus 8/44 rested participants signed a false admission: odds ratio 4.5, 95% CI 1.7-11.8. This is an odds ratio, not a 4.5-fold risk. The admission lacked a detailed post-admission narrative; the study did not establish lifelong guilt, actual criminal culpability, or long-term injury. [D11](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d11)

<a id="experimental-boundaries"></a>
## Experiments that support and constrain the scenarios

| Question | Relevant finding | Decisive limit |
|---|---|---|
| Can a suggested event be reported as remembered after hypnosis? | An early experiment found acceptance in 13/27 highly hypnotizable participants. | A different study had no retained pseudomemory among 47 participants at its final test. These are different procedures, not a clean exact-replication pair. A 60-person study linked false reports to hypnotizability rather than induction itself. [D12-D14](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d12) |
| Can someone report remembering a crime they did not commit? | Shaw and Porter classified 70% of the crime-condition sample as false memories. | Independent recoding of the same reports yielded 26%-30% under other criteria. A separate corrigendum repaired calculations; it did not replace the headline rate with the recoders' estimate. No durable disabling guilt was established. [D15-D17](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d15) |
| Can an image-like recollection survive disbelief? | Survey and experimental work distinguish recollective experience from belief, including after debriefing. | The 2024 negative-picture experiments changed belief more than recollection but found no notable emotional-state change. Persistent phenomenology is not evidence of persistent torment. [D18-D20](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d18) |
| Must induced false memories be permanent? | A 52-person experiment reported substantial reversal; 38 attended follow-up roughly a year later. | No untreated reversal-control arm isolates every component, and follow-up attrition matters. A different 2026 report found no benefit from a different training intervention. Neither universal irreversibility nor guaranteed repair follows. [D21-D22](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d21) |
| Does a confident posthypnotic answer become more accurate? | A selected N=24 study found faster, more confident recognition across a week. | Accuracy did not improve. Confidence and accuracy are different outcomes. [D23](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d23) |
| Can learned pink-noise cues affect fear? | One experiment found greater laboratory fear expression after sleep cueing of a learned context, with a different direction during wake. | No established lasting phobia, chosen attachment, or control of a complex personal belief. Unassociated background noise is a different exposure. [D24](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d24) |
| Can pink noise worsen measured sleep? | A 25-person trial found reduced REM and worse subjective outcomes under tested conditions. | Morning cognition was unaffected; permanent cognitive injury and psychological crippling were not established. [D25](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d25) |
| Can observers identify truthful recollection by its presentation? | New observers assessed videos from the earlier crime-memory study; the authors reported poor discrimination. | Reused induction footage and its classification controversy remain relevant. These were laboratory judgments, not a validated clinical, consent, or courtroom truth test. [D26](../research/updates/2026-10-29-documented-abuse-cases.md#d26) |

<a id="scenario-crosswalk"></a>
## Crosswalk for the eight fictional examples

| Fictional example | Closest documented support | What remains fictional or unknown |
|---|---|---|
| **F1: Guilt for something never done** | False crime-memory reports and false admissions, with the coding/measurement limits above. | Reliable installation of a specific moral debt, lifelong guilt, or resulting disability. |
| **F2: Losing family twice** | Family rupture and attempted reconciliation in the opinion/retraction reports. | The earlier invented parent's death and irretrievable reconciliation are not a recovered case history. |
| **F3: Childhood affection becomes sinister** | Belief/recollection can diverge; accounts can be reinterpreted. | Wholesale, controllable corruption of every positive autobiographical meaning. |
| **F4: Sleep becomes frightening** | Nightmares and impairment alleged after mixed treatments; suffering associated with coercive sleep loss. | A demonstrated pathway from pink-noise conditioning to durable fear of sleeping safely at home. |
| **F5: Love cannot be authenticated** | Exploitation within trusted relationships and uncertainty about personal experience. | Reliable selective attachment programming, or evidence that ordinary doubt reveals past manipulation. |
| **F6: Reactions become evidence against the survivor** | Subjective nonvolition reports and failures of memory/appearance-based judgments. | A specific documented adjudication in which engineered calm proved consent; calm and distress do not settle truth or consent. |
| **F7: Recovery itself becomes threatening** | Selected retraction accounts describe harmful therapy and leaving treatment. | A proven hypnosis-induced inability to trust every later helper, or a population prognosis of failed recovery. |
| **F8: Correction cannot restore the lost life** | Recorded family/employment consequences and reports of attempted reconciliation. | A measured irreversible-loss trajectory for the invented character. Memory reversal and repair of social consequences are separate endpoints. |

The scenarios are exploratory fiction. They should not be attributed to the named people above, treated as explanations for an individual's symptoms, or used to infer exposure from distress alone.

<a id="what-would-change-the-assessment"></a>
## What would change the assessment

Evidence for a stronger control claim would need independently replicated, specified behavioral effects; credible controls for ordinary authority and demand; measures of cooperation and resistance; corroborated exposure; and follow-up after renewed cues or treatment cease. Evidence of harm needs baseline functioning, comparison exposures, measured clinical outcomes, and retention/attrition accounting.

The strongest objection to a universal-control interpretation is that the case records describe heterogeneous relationships and treatment packages, while the controlled experiments measure narrower and sometimes reversible effects. Documented abuse deserves accurate attribution even when the proposed science-fiction mechanism remains unsupported.
