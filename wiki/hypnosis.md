# Hypnosis and agency

[Home](../README.md) · [Conditioning](conditioning.md) · [Evidence ledger](evidence-ledger.md) · [Research network](research-horizon.md) · [Combined techniques](combined-techniques.md) · [Replication follow-up](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md)

The recovered ChatGPT **wiki pages on hypnosis** conversation contains research and draft material on **hypnotic involuntariness** and **dissociated control theory**. These are explanations of hypnotic responding and subjective agency, not demonstrations of unrestricted control over another person.

## Concepts

| Concept | Distinction preserved in the research |
|---|---|
| Hypnotic involuntariness | A response may feel as though it occurs without ordinary voluntary authorship. Subjective involuntariness does not settle how the response was produced. |
| Original dissociated control theory | Bowers proposed reduced executive initiation or supervision of lower-level behavioral systems. |
| Later decoupling / second-order accounts | Executive processes may remain involved while their coordination with monitoring changes. These revisions should not be flattened into the original theory. |
| Cold-control accounts | Changes in awareness of intentions offer a competing explanation for altered agency. |
| Sociocognitive accounts | Expectations, goals, context, and response sets provide alternatives to a single special-state mechanism. |
| Posthypnotic suggestion | Later task responses can follow a suggestion, especially in selected responsive participants; complexity and context matter. |
| Posthypnotic amnesia | Failure to remember an instruction later does not establish that it was unconscious when delivered. |

## What the collection supports

The original conditioning/hypnosis answers collect pain, perception, false-memory, and posthypnotic-response studies. Effects vary with responsiveness, task, wording, selection, and comparison condition. Confidence and vividness do not verify an autobiographical memory. Apparently objectionable behavior also requires comparison with nonhypnotic compliance, simulation, and trust in the experimenter.

The theory review [Kirsch and Lynn, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9461855/) describes competing dissociation accounts. It supports the existence and content of the theories; it does not prove their mechanisms. The historical draft supplies additional DOI and PMID leads, including Bowers (1992), Weitzenhoffer (1974), and later Woody/Jamieson discussions. These leads have not all been reverified in this collection pass.

## Additional recovered research

The [response-durability summary](../research/chats/supplement-235b91fa-5cab-58cd-bb2a-819a70248b5e.md) examines a 1991 repeated-follow-up study, distinguishing responsiveness within the testing situation from generalization outside it. The [absorption summary](../research/chats/supplement-9ec3e30f-348e-566e-8a1e-54eae2622447.md) treats absorption as a limited predictor rather than a diagnostic shortcut.

The [compliance and experience review](../research/chats/supplement-61ab65d6-91b2-414d-a69f-599e081abc03.md) separates performing the requested action from having the suggested subjective experience. The [agency discussion](../research/chats/supplement-6e8ca28b-c37e-4391-89bc-b8159bf3e0df.md) and [correction about execution](../research/chats/supplement-570bb1d6-6ca9-44c8-8aeb-fc0a2b7967d4.md) preserve competing interpretations of intention, monitoring, and apparently automatic responding. These are explanatory research discussions, not proof that a person has lost executive control.

A [clinical-outcomes review](../research/chats/supplement-c3aeac62-da2e-5e0f-b2ad-e46b85ec902b.md) collects condition-specific treatment results. Those historical clinical summaries are not current treatment recommendations, and benefit on one endpoint cannot establish general susceptibility or behavioral control.

The decisive objection to a general “loss of will” interpretation is that subjective agency, suggestibility, memory, and compliance are separable measures. A stronger claim would require reproducible specified behavior across independent settings, suitable controls, and results beyond a selected laboratory task.
