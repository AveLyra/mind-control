# Prediction error and retrieval-enhanced suggestibility

[Home](../README.md) · [Reconsolidation and source monitoring](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md) · [AI-memory replications](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md) · [Research horizon](research-horizon.md) · [Source checks](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md)

**Retrieval can strengthen original information while also improving learning of later misinformation. Prediction error can alter memory, but its effects depend on the task, context, and outcome of the prediction. Neither is a general memory-rewriting switch. Their contribution to a combined AI–sleep–hypnosis effect remains unestablished in this bounded review.**

Assessed October 28, 2026. Thirty source entries include 28 PubMed publications, a doctoral dissertation, and a journal meta-analysis outside PubMed. One PubMed entry is a correction notice. These are publication/source entries, not 30 independent experiments. The new checks deepen the earlier mechanism review rather than replace its preserved source record.

The [context/detection follow-up](context-reinstatement-and-detection.md) now examines which context returns, at what stage, and whether a discrepancy is later remembered. It pairs protective results with context-induced false recognition and separates repeated analyses from independent cohorts.

<a id="distinctions"></a>
## Which prediction, which error, and which memory?

| Term or measurement | What was compared | Limit on transfer between fields |
|---|---|---|
| Mnemonic prediction error | A remembered event's expected outcome versus a changed or missing outcome | A task-specific mismatch does not establish destabilization of every autobiographical memory. |
| Model-derived learning error | A computational estimate based on expectations and outcomes | The estimate depends on the model and can have different effects for correct versus incorrect predictions. |
| Auditory mismatch response | Neural responses to regular versus deviant sounds | Sensory adaptation, prediction, attention, and state affect the response. It is not a direct measure of narrative comprehension or memory alteration. |
| Predictive-coding account of hypnosis | A model relating expectations, attention/precision, action, and perceived agency | A theoretical explanation is different from an experiment demonstrating a particular memory-update mechanism. |
| Retrieval-enhanced suggestibility (RES) | Later misinformation reporting after initial retrieval versus an appropriate no-retrieval comparison | An error after conversation is not automatically RES; the initial retrieval component needs its own comparison. |

An AI model's next-token prediction error, or its retrieval of documents, is also different from a person's prediction error or retrieval of an episode. Shared vocabulary does not establish a shared causal mechanism.

<a id="prediction-error"></a>
## Prediction error: supportive results and important limits

Sevenster and colleagues' associative-fear studies supported a boundary-condition account: the mismatch during reactivation mattered, and changing the reactivation conditions could engage different processes. These were fear-conditioning/pharmacological studies, not tests of AI-generated episodic misinformation. [P01–P02](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p01)

A later same-lab replication attempt did not obtain the predicted selective effect. It also lacked differential fear retention in its comparison groups, making the null difficult to interpret. Its conditioned stimuli differed from the original study. This is an unsuccessful, modified replication attempt with a control/measurement limitation—not decisive proof that prediction error never matters. [P03](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p03)

| Additional evidence | Measured result | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Positive-memory interference study | A prediction-error-related difference appeared in free recall but not recognition. | One endpoint cannot certify global loss of the original episode. [P06](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p06) |
| Four event-segmentation experiments | Contextual stability explained segmentation better than prediction error in the tested comparisons. | A boundary on a general prediction-error account, not a direct replication of the video-updating or fear experiment. [P07](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p07) |
| Computationally modeled encoding experiments | Stronger estimated errors predicted better or worse later recognition depending on whether the prediction was correct. | Greater error is not a monotonic route to either stronger memory or greater suggestibility. [P08](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p08) |

The earlier [behavioral video work](../research/updates/2026-10-28-reconsolidation-source-monitoring.md#m10) remains relevant positive evidence, including a time-course test. It does not isolate the proposed AI/sleep/hypnosis combination. Context change, interrupted content, retrieval effort, and prediction error require separate comparisons before assigning the effect to one of them.

<a id="correction"></a>
### Correction to the hippocampal prediction-error paper

The 2021 fMRI paper now has a **June 2026 correction** for a preprocessing join error that excluded or mislabeled some trials. The authors report revised numbers/figures with key findings and conclusions unchanged. This review checked the complete notice, not a new analysis of their data; fresh main-body retrieval failed. The notice is not another experiment or replication. [P04–P05](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p04)

<a id="retrieval"></a>
## Retrieval can support both accuracy and misinformation

The research is more specific than “recalling makes memory unreliable.” Repeated retrieval produced increased misinformation reporting under some conditions, while delayed tests showed stronger benefits for uncontaminated details. Free recall also produced RES in several experiments; it is not a universal exemption. [P09–P11](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p09)

The clearest candidate for linking these results to conversation is **test-potentiated new learning**: retrieval changes how later material is processed. A misinformation study found attention/allocation effects without reduced access to original information in its test. A broader meta-analysis supports potentiated new learning, but also documents conditions under which testing helps or harms. Neither establishes a special mechanism unique to AI. [P12](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p12), [P19](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p19)

| Boundary test | Finding | Strongest qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Context versus misinformation format | Context reinstatement/coherence mattered across narrative and question formats; disrupting coherence eliminated RES in one experiment. | “Chat” or “narrative” is a package, not an isolated mechanism. [P13](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p13) |
| Discrepancy detection | Concurrent consistency assessment negated the overall RES effect; retrospective detection was associated with resistance. | Detection did not guarantee immunity in every subgroup. Retrospective detection groups are not a randomized moderator. [P14](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p14) |
| Processing demands | A dual-task manipulation changed RES and learning of the later account. | Attention/new learning is a viable explanation alongside original-trace updating. [P15](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p15) |
| Protective testing in a scene task | Testing protected conditionalized recall/source performance across misinformation formats; early intrusions could persist. | Protection and error propagation can coexist. Conditionalized and unconditional scores are different endpoints. [P16](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p16) |
| Warning timing | An early warning's benefit survived a delayed test; delaying the warning itself removed its benefit. | The location of a delay matters. Elapsed nights were not an isolated sleep manipulation. [P17](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p17) |
| Warning frequency | Warnings improved misinformation-related accuracy/confidence, while repeated warnings also reduced confidence in uncontaminated information. | A safeguard can have metacognitive costs; warning more is not uniformly better. [P18](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p18) |

The strongest objection to treating RES as proof of reconsolidation is that **enhanced later learning, contextual integration, retrieval competition, and reporting choices can produce the behavioral effect**. Some tests find original information still accessible. This does not negate reconsolidation in other tasks; it prevents using the RES label as a unique mechanistic diagnosis.

<a id="synthesis"></a>
## How large and general is the RES effect?

A 2020 doctoral meta-analysis reported **g = 0.212, 95% CI [0.12, 0.30]**, across 57 effects from 30 reports and 4,898 participants. Its moderator associations did not remain significant after accounting for research group/publication status. A 2026 journal synthesis reported **d = 0.34, 95% CI [0.23, 0.45]**, across 72 comparisons. The samples and contrasts overlap across the literature; these are not two independent intervention replications. [P20–P21](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p20)

The newer review distinguishes reports, experiments, and comparisons, and includes warning contrasts that need careful mapping to its baseline RES contrast. Its comparison-level supplement and image-only table were not recovered in this pass. Neither estimate was recalculated here. They describe bounded laboratory literature, not an individual risk or an effect size for AI plus sleep/hypnosis.

<a id="sleep"></a>
## Sleep: mismatch-related stabilization and restricted prediction signals

The incomplete-reminder study now has selected complete-body inspection. In its tested learning task, incomplete waking reminders were associated with vulnerability; sleep cueing supported stabilization. Cue completeness mattered differently at shorter and longer delays. That is evidence of state/task dependence, not a way to transmit new personal history. [P22](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p22)

The auditory literature adds a useful distinction:

- A 2015 local/global study reported loss of the global response and of a particular local prediction-related peak, with other sensory responses preserved. [P23](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p23)
- A 2025 EEG/MEG study found feature-specific preactivation during N1/N2 sleep while higher-order statistical tracking remained restricted to waking. [P24](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p24)
- A 2026 N = 29 study found local mismatch processing across stages, with delayed/reduced information and increased redundancy. **Larger NREM ERP amplitude did not mean richer encoded information.** [P25](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p25)

These studies change tasks, states, and analyses; they are not three exact replications of one endpoint. Residual local processing and impaired higher-order tracking can coexist. Neither response amplitude nor a retained local signal establishes semantic understanding, later suggestibility, or source attribution. The [earlier sleep/source findings](reconsolidation-and-source-monitoring.md#sleep) also leave plausible effects in opposing directions.

<a id="hypnosis"></a>
## Hypnosis: predictive models are ahead of a stable mismatch biomarker

A 2005 study reported MMN changes across conditions and susceptibility groups. A 2019 study of nine highly hypnotizable participants found no significant MMN-amplitude differences. A 2024 conference study reported preliminary modulation. These are mixed, small/early findings with changed designs, not a validated universal susceptibility readout. [P26–P28](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p26)

A predictive-coding account models motor suggestion and perceived nonagency through expectations, attention, and inferred authorship. That is a theory with testable predictions, not an experiment showing that hypnosis supplies the mnemonic prediction error needed to alter an episode. [P29](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p29)

Auditory MMN, experienced agency, factual recall, and source memory remain separate outcomes. The selected hypnosis papers do not manipulate AI misinformation plus initial retrieval or experimentally isolate an added sleep effect.

<a id="ai"></a>
## What the AI connection currently supports

The [earlier generated-content experiments](decoded-feedback-and-ai-memory.md#ai) establish restricted memory-report effects. They do not establish that a model's fluency, a person's prediction error, and RES form one mechanism.

**Reasonable inference:** a conversation that retrieves prior material and introduces a later account could involve test-potentiated learning and context processing. Its contribution must be separated from content, exposure, interaction, source attribution, and ordinary learning. A chatbot-related error alone cannot identify RES or reconsolidation.

An adjacent 2026 ERP study reported lower credibility ratings under AI-source conditions, alongside neural differences interpreted through predictive coding. Its endpoint was credibility, not later false memory; the abstract does not resolve actual composition versus source-label effects. It is a counterweight to assuming AI cues invariably increase acceptance. [P30](../research/updates/2026-10-28-prediction-error-retrieval.md#p30)

<a id="tests"></a>
## Which evidence would change the assessment?

| Proposed connection | Current status | Discriminating evidence / contrary result |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction error mediates AI-related memory change | Plausible, underdetermined | A mnemonic-error manipulation distinct from novelty/context/retrieval effort. Equal effects without that error would weaken specificity. |
| Retrieval explains the chatbot effect | Supported non-AI candidate, unisolated in the combined claim | A retrieval-component comparison with matched later content/exposure and separate event/source tests. A persistent error without retrieval would leave other mechanisms necessary. |
| Sleep amplifies prediction-error-related misinformation | Direction unknown for the proposed task | A causal sleep comparison measuring factual memory, source, and delayed errors. Stabilized originals or preserved context could oppose amplification. |
| Hypnosis joins the mnemonic-error/RES pathway | Theoretical/adjacent components | Direct memory/source outcomes separated from induction, trait responsiveness, agency, and sensory mismatch signals. MMN change alone would not close the connection. |
| The full combination supplies durable personal-memory replacement | Unestablished in selected evidence | Independent factual ground truth, component comparisons, persistence, and recoverability tests. Confidence, distress relief, credibility, or a neural response cannot substitute for that endpoint. |

Confidence is higher for the restricted behavioral and measurement distinctions than for the proposed combined mechanism. These are criteria for evaluating future studies; no intervention, experiment, or scientific reanalysis was performed by this project.
